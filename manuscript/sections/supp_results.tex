\subsection{RGB Rendering Check}
\label{sup:rgb}
The gate uses depth only. As a check that the same block also supports a photometric surfel model, one
2D Gaussian splatting model~\cite{gs_2dgs} was trained with gsplat~\cite{gs_gsplat} on the 185 RGB training images of
MatrixCity block~3, all clean, at half resolution ($960\times540$). Training ran for 30\,000 iterations with an
L1 plus 0.2 D-SSIM loss and, after the first third, the normal-consistency and distortion terms of 2DGS. It
started from the 81\,152 E1 surfel centres, used the default gsplat densification, and was capped at
$1.5\times10^{6}$ Gaussians so that it fits an 8~GB GPU. It took 31~min on one RTX~2070 (peak GPU memory
3.2~GB; largest temperatures 66\,$^{\circ}$C GPU and 57\,$^{\circ}$C CPU). The 19 held-out test views of the
block lie between the training positions and point in headings that the training views do not use.
Table~\ref{tab:rgb} and Figure~\ref{fig:rgbtest} give PSNR and SSIM on them. The five test views at
$x=70$~m (frames 20--24) are the weakest, at 16.7--17.7~dB; in frame~22 the upper part of the image
lies beyond the area the training views cover and renders black (Figure~\ref{fig:rgbviews}, bottom row).

\subsection{Surface Comparison}
\label{sup:surface}
\emph{Fusion.} Every surface is a digital surface model on a fixed grid over block~3: each view's depth is
back-projected along its pixel rays, each view keeps the highest point per cell, and the cell's height is
the median of these per-view maxima over the views that see it (at least three); cells with fewer views
are left empty. The same operator is applied to every source, so that the residuals compare depths, not
fusion methods. The ground truth uses MatrixCity's exact z-depth of the 185 training frames, point-sampled at
$480\times270$. The RGB 2DGS model of Supplement~\ref{sup:rgb} was rendered at the same pixels (median depth,
pixels with accumulated opacity above 0.5) and gridded at 2~m. The E1 surfels have one depth per pixel at
$96\times54$, $\hat D=\bar d+J\boldsymbol\theta$ with $\boldsymbol\theta$ the fused posterior offset of
each surfel, and are gridded at 4~m against a ground truth gridded the same way. Cells whose
ground-truth height is below $-2$~m are water (the plane at $-3.4$~m; land and roads start at $+0.5$~m). No model
was retrained; rendering and fusion took about 3~min (largest temperatures 40\,$^{\circ}$C GPU and
60\,$^{\circ}$C CPU).

\emph{Findings.} The RGB 2DGS surface is close to the ground truth on land, slightly high (median
$+0.49$~m), with errors at building edges and a tail of larger errors (RMSE 6.0~m against a 90th
percentile of the absolute residual of 1.7~m); on water it is high by metres to tens of
metres with streaks along the viewing directions, and its depth-derived normals there are wrong by a median
of 30$^{\circ}$ against 12$^{\circ}$ on land (Figure~\ref{fig:surfacenormals}). In the E1 fault scene, the
faulty view pulled the fused surface under its fault down by a median of 1.6~m (on minus off: median
$+1.59$~m, 95th percentile $+6.5$~m); without it the surface returns to the no-fault reference. The E1 RMSE
of 6--9~m in all three cases is dominated by building edges (the blue outlines in
Figure~\ref{fig:surfacegate}d,e), where 3~m voxel surfels on the exact geometry cannot follow vertical walls;
it is the same with and without the fault.

\subsection{H7 Protocol Details}
\label{sup:h7}
\emph{World and renderer.} The E1 surfels (3~m voxels on the exact geometry) are rendered as discs of radius
2.1~m, z-buffered, with ray--plane depth; the measured depth of each view is this render at its true pose
plus noise of 1\% of depth, so that the renderer used by the gate and by the refinement is exact at the true
poses. The fault of Figure~\ref{fig:e1qualviews} is re-injected into the same rectangle of view 59. At the true
poses the gate rejects only view 59, as in E1.
\emph{Perturbed groups.} Per seed, five isolated views on different strips of eight frames, one block of three
consecutive frames, and view 59, each perturbed on its own; a block shares one perturbation. Rotation axes and
translation directions are drawn at random per group. A first attempt that perturbed all groups at once
swamped 69 unperturbed views at 1$^{\circ}$ and 25~GSD and was replaced before any result was used.
\emph{Gate.} $\Sigma_r$ of \eqref{M-eq:res} with the pose term, rank six, added by a second Woodbury step;
$J_q$ by central differences with the surfel assignment held fixed; $\Sigma_q$ from the strip spline with the
GNSS/IMU and smoothness priors only. Because a perturbed group changes the statistics only of views that
share surfels with it, those are recomputed and the others reused; this is exact.
\emph{Refinement.} One spline control per frame interval; the spline is first fitted to the input poses with
the two priors and then refined by Gauss--Newton on corrections to it, with Huber weights at $3\sigma$ on the
render-and-compare residuals, the per-view scale and offset marginalised, step halving on the total cost, and
eight iterations. Plain least squares diverged at building edges, where a pixel sees a different surfel, and
was replaced by the Huber form before any H7 outcome was computed. A first full run had applied the spline to
corrections of the input poses rather than to the trajectory, which kept a perturbed frame at its input; it
was fixed and rerun, with no change to priors, thresholds or criteria (\texttt{configs/experiments/h7\_matrixcity\_pose.yaml}).
\emph{Cost.} 51~min on four CPU threads, largest temperature 60\,$^{\circ}$C (CPU).
