\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setting and notation}
\label{sec:setting}
We are given posed views $\{(I_i, K_i, T_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ of a static scene, with images $I_i$, intrinsics $K_i$ and camera-to-world poses $T_i$.
Poses and intrinsics are taken as known (from a structure-from-motion system or a dataset), the scene is assumed static, and faults, where present, are assumed to affect a minority of the views and, within a faulty view, a minority of its pixels.
For a tested view $j$ we write $n$ for the number of pixels at which the test is evaluated and stack all per-pixel quantities of view $j$ into vectors in $\R^n$.
Three objects enter the test.
(i) $M$ \emph{unguided} depth samples $\{d^{(k)}\}_{k=1}^M$ of view $j$, drawn from the model conditioned on $I_j$ alone.
(ii) A depth render $D^{-j}$ of view $j$ from a surface built from the other views $\{i\neq j\}$, and its per-pixel variance $\Sigma_r^{-j}$ (diagonal, or diagonal plus low rank).
(iii) A support mask: the pixels of view $j$ at which the surface is observed by at least two of the other views; all vectors are restricted to these pixels.
The superscript $-j$ marks quantities that are, by construction, independent of every datum of view $j$ (image, depth, pose).
This independence is checked in the implementation by perturbing and by poisoning view $j$ and verifying that the render does not change beyond the run-to-run floor of the rasteriser.

The samples are affine-invariant: each is defined up to an unknown positive scale and an unknown shift, and the render is in scene units.
The test therefore compares the \emph{local shape} of the samples with the render after fitting a single scale and shift for the view, and no other parameter is estimated from view $j$.

\subsection{Affine-invariant depth diffusion}
\label{sec:model}
\runin{Representation.} The target for an image is its depth after cropping, normalised per image with its 2nd and 98th percentiles to $[-1,1]$ and clipped, as in Marigold~\cite{ke2024marigold}.
The normalised depth is replicated to three channels and encoded with the frozen image VAE of the backbone, so that image and depth share one latent space~\cite{rombach2022latent}.
No metric scale is predicted and no camera embedding is used; the model sees only the RGB latent of $I_j$.

\runin{Backbone and adaptation.} The backbone is the SDXL base U-Net~\cite{podell2023sdxl}, frozen and stored in half precision.
Low-rank adapters~\cite{hu2021lora} of rank $32$ (scale $\alpha=32$) are attached to the query, key, value and output projections of every attention block.
The input convolution is widened from $4$ to $8$ channels, to take the concatenated RGB latent and noisy depth latent, by duplicating its pretrained weights and halving them; it is trained in full.
Text conditioning is replaced by the embedding of the empty prompt from both CLIP encoders, computed once.
The VAE is run in single precision, because the SDXL VAE can overflow in half precision.

\runin{Objective.} The model is trained with $v$-prediction~\cite{salimans2022progressive} on a noise schedule rescaled to zero terminal signal-to-noise ratio~\cite{lin2023common}, with timesteps drawn uniformly.
The loss is the mean squared error on the depth latent, masked at latent resolution to where the ground truth is valid (an $8\times8$ pixel block counts as valid if at least half its pixels are).
Pixels without ground truth are filled with the nearest valid value before encoding, so that holes in the label do not appear as depth edges in the target latent.

\runin{Sampler.} Samples are drawn with DDIM~\cite{song2021ddim} with trailing timestep spacing, from independent Gaussian noise per sample and with no classifier-free guidance~\cite{ho2022classifierfree}.
A sample is decoded by the VAE back to a normalised depth map.
The number of sampler steps is a free parameter; Section~\ref{sec:stage1} reports its effect on the first-stage model.

\subsection{Unguided and guided sampling}
\label{sec:guidance}
\emph{Unguided} samples use only $I_j$. They are the measurement and carry their own spread, and they are the only samples the test of Section~\ref{sec:statistic} sees: if the render entered the samples, agreement with it would be built in.

\emph{Guided} samples are the intended product. At the last denoising steps only (initially the last two of about five to ten), the predicted clean depth $\hat x_0$ of the current step is decoded, aligned to the render by a closed-form weighted least-squares scale and shift (variable projection), and the gradient of
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{guide}}(\hat x_0)=\min_{s,t}\ \bigl\lVert s\,\mathrm{dec}(\hat x_0)+t\mathbf 1-D^{-j}\bigr\rVert^2_{(\Sigma_r^{-j})^{-1}}
\label{eq:guide}
\end{equation}
with respect to the latent is added to the update, in the manner of diffusion posterior sampling~\cite{chung2023dps}.
Restricting guidance to late steps follows the observation that early predictions of $\hat x_0$ are too noisy to guide~\cite{depthsync2025}.
Because $(s,t)$ is solved at every step, the guided sample inherits the render's scale and shift without the model ever being asked to predict them.

\runin{Surface.} The surface is a structure-only two-dimensional Gaussian splat~\cite{huang2024twodgs}: surfels are initialised from the depth of every view except $j$, optimised with a depth and a photometric term on those views only, and rasterised into view $j$~\cite{ye2024gsplat}.
Each surfel carries a depth variance proportional to the square of its depth; each other view's surfels are rasterised into view $j$ separately, the per-view depths within a tolerance of the nearest one are kept, and their depths and variances are averaged, so that $\Sigma_r^{-j}$ is the mean variance divided by the number of supporting views. A view supports a pixel where its rasterised opacity exceeds $0.5$, and the support mask keeps pixels supported by at least two views.

\subsection{Leave-view-out statistic with a fitted scale and shift}
\label{sec:statistic}
\runin{Aligning the samples.} Each sample is itself affine-invariant, because the percentile normalisation of the training target fixes scale and shift only approximately.
Each sample is therefore aligned to the raw mean $\tilde m=\tfrac1M\sum_k d^{(k)}$ by its own least-squares scale and shift, $a^{(k)}=\alpha_k d^{(k)}+\beta_k\mathbf 1$; using the fixed raw mean as the reference avoids a drift of the common scale.
Let $\bar m=\tfrac1M\sum_k a^{(k)}$ and let $\Sigma_U$ be the covariance of the aligned deviations $a^{(k)}-\bar m$, which describes the local shape uncertainty of one sample.

\runin{Null model.} If the samples are draws from a calibrated posterior of the local shape and the truth is one more such draw, the render of a fault-free view satisfies
\begin{equation}
D^{-j}=s\,\bar m+t\mathbf 1+\varepsilon,\qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal N\!\bigl(0,\ C(s)\bigr),\qquad C(s)=s^{2}\bigl(1+\tfrac1M\bigr)\Sigma_U+\Sigma_r^{-j},
\label{eq:null}
\end{equation}
where $(s,t)$ is the unknown map from the model's normalised units to scene units.
The factor $1+1/M$ is the predictive variance of a new draw about the mean of $M$ draws.
The sample covariance is rescaled by $s^2$ because it is expressed in the model's units; this is what makes a scale error detectable (below).

\runin{Fit.} Only $(s,t)$ is fitted with view $j$'s data:
\begin{equation}
(\hat s,\hat t)=\argmin_{s,t}\ \rho\Bigl(L(s)^{-1}\bigl(s\,\bar m+t\mathbf 1-D^{-j}\bigr)\Bigr),\qquad L(s)L(s)^\top=C(s),
\label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the Huber loss (threshold $1.345$) summed over the whitened residual, so that a fault covering a minority of pixels does not drag the fit.
Because $C$ depends on $s$, the fit alternates between whitening with the current $s$ and an iteratively reweighted least-squares solve for $(s,t)$, started from the ordinary least-squares solution (three outer and eight inner iterations).

\runin{Statistic.} With $r=\hat s\,\bar m+\hat t\mathbf 1-D^{-j}$, the statistic is the squared norm of the whitened residual,
\begin{equation}
T_j=r^\top C(\hat s)^{-1}r .
\label{eq:stat}
\end{equation}
With a known covariance and an ordinary least-squares fit, $T_j$ would follow $\chi^2_{n-2}$ under~\eqref{eq:null}.
This reference is not used: the robust fit, the dependence of $C$ on $\hat s$, and above all an estimated $\Sigma_U$ break it (Section~\ref{sec:toy}).

\runin{Covariance estimate.} With $M\approx10$ samples, the sample covariance has rank $M-1$ and cannot be inverted on its own.
$\Sigma_U$ is instead modelled as a stationary kernel plus a nugget, $\Sigma_U=\sigma^2 k_\ell(\lVert p-p'\rVert)+\nu I$, fitted to the empirical autocovariance of the aligned deviations pooled over the $M$ samples and over all pixel pairs at short lags (up to five pixels; at longer lags the per-sample alignment biases the autocovariance downward).
The kernel family (exponential, Mat\'ern-$3/2$ or squared exponential) and the length scale $\ell$ are chosen by the residual of this lag fit.
A rank-$(M-1)$ sample covariance shrunk toward its diagonal is the baseline estimator.
Dense matrices are used for the synthetic check; at image resolution the same kernel-plus-diagonal structure admits fast solves.

\runin{Threshold.} The threshold $\tau_\alpha$ is the empirical $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of $T$ over a set of clean calibration views passed through the identical pipeline (alignment, covariance estimate, robust fit), with the conformal $(k+1)$ correction~\cite{angelopoulos2021gentle}: for $K$ null values, $\tau_\alpha$ is the $\lceil (K+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$-th smallest.
View $j$ is flagged when $T_j>\tau_\alpha$.
This presumes that the calibration views are exchangeable with the test views.

\runin{What the statistic can and cannot see.} A fault $f$ added to the samples is invisible exactly when it lies in the span of the fitted directions. A constant offset, $f\propto\mathbf 1$, is absorbed by $\hat t$ and cannot be detected by construction.
A global scale error, $f\propto\bar m-\mathrm{mean}(\bar m)\mathbf 1$, is absorbed in the mean by $\hat s$ but not in the covariance: the fitted scale also rescales $s^2\Sigma_U$, so the implied scale disagrees with the sample spread and the view is flagged.
The robust fit assumes that the faulty pixels are a minority; when they are a majority, the fit follows them and the clean pixels are what look inconsistent, so the statistic still detects an inconsistency but cannot attribute it.
A fault common to all views is invisible to any test of multi-view consistency.

\subsection{Synthetic check of the statistic}
\label{sec:toy-design}
Before any learned model exists, the statistic is checked in a synthetic Gaussian world in which every covariance is known, so that the cost of each approximation can be measured separately.
On a $g\times g$ grid, a fixed mean relief $\mu$ (a smooth surface plus a step edge, large compared with the noise so that $\bar m$ and $\mathbf 1$ are not collinear) is perturbed by a Mat\'ern-$3/2$ process with covariance $K_U$ (standard deviation $1$, length scale $3$ pixels) to give the truth; the truth is one more draw of the posterior that the samples come from.
The render is the truth under a random scale $a\sim\mathcal U(0.8,1.25)$ and shift $b\sim\mathcal U(-1,1)$ plus Mat\'ern-$3/2$ noise with covariance $K_r$ (standard deviation $0.7$, length scale $6$).
The $M$ samples are independent draws of $\mu$ plus $K_U$ noise, each with its own scale and shift wobble of standard deviation $0.05$, which imitates the per-image percentile normalisation.
A fault of magnitude $\kappa$ is added to the samples, in units of the per-pixel total residual standard deviation $\sqrt{\mathrm{median\,diag}(a^2(1+1/M)K_U+K_r)}$.
Fault families are a square region and a region following a level set of the truth (both $12\%$ of the view), a smooth warp over the whole view, a constant offset and a change of scale, and a band covering a chosen fraction of the view.
A miscalibrated sampler is simulated by scaling the sample deviations by $0.6$ or $1.6$ while the truth keeps the calibrated spread.

Five variants of the statistic are compared:
the true $(a,b)$ with the true covariance and no fit (an unattainable bound);
the robustly fitted $(s,t)$ with the true covariance (the oracle);
the fitted $(s,t)$ with the stationary-kernel estimate of $\Sigma_U$, robust and non-robust;
and the fitted $(s,t)$ with the shrunk sample covariance.
The kernel estimator is restricted to the exponential family in this check, while the truth is Mat\'ern-$3/2$, so the estimator's shape is deliberately misspecified.
For each variant the threshold is taken from the clean-view null of that variant, power is the fraction of faulty trials above it, and the minimum detectable fault is the magnitude at which the power reaches $80\%$, interpolated between the magnitudes tested.
The results are reported in Section~\ref{sec:toy}.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}
\runin{Stage 1: supervised.} The model of Section~\ref{sec:model} is first fine-tuned on labelled synthetic and rendered data only, with the masked $v$-prediction loss on the normalised ground-truth depth.
Training data, augmentation and optimiser settings are given in Section~\ref{sec:stage1-setup}.

\runin{Stage 2: semi-self-supervised (planned).} Labelled views keep the stage-1 loss.
Unlabelled views use a scale-and-shift-invariant render-and-compare loss of the form~\eqref{eq:guide} between the decoded prediction $\mathrm{dec}(\hat x_0)$ and the stop-gradient leave-view-out render, weighted by $(\Sigma_r^{-j})^{-1}$ and restricted to pixels supported by at least two other views, plus a photometric loss that warps view $j$ into the other views with its own depth and is independent of the surface.
Because of the invariance, the render never imposes a scale on the model.
A slowly updated (exponential moving average) copy of the model builds the surfaces, and every batch contains labelled data.
