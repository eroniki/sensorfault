\subsection{Setting and Faults}
\label{sec:method:setting}
A static scene is observed by $N$ RGB images $I_i$ with known intrinsics and poses (camera centre
$\mathbf{c}_i$, rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$). A monocular metric depth model gives one depth map
$\hat d_i$ per image. The surface is a set of 2DGS-style surfels \cite{gs_2dgs} fitted by
depth-supervised splatting and rendered with gsplat \cite{gs_gsplat} into expected depth, alpha
weights and accumulated opacity. Colour is a separate appearance block (per-surfel colour): the
photometric loss updates only that block, its gradients are stopped at the geometry, and colour
reaches geometry only through the depth model. The gate uses depth only.

The nominal measurement of pixel $u$ in view $i$ is
\begin{equation}
  \hat d_i(u)=a_i\,d_i(u)+b_i+\varepsilon_i(u),\qquad \boldsymbol\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_\varepsilon),
  \label{eq:meas}
\end{equation}
with a per-view scale and offset $(a_i,b_i)$ absorbed by alignment and
$\Sigma_\varepsilon=DCD$, $D=\mathrm{diag}\,\sigma(r,\tau,\theta)$ depending on range, texture and
incidence, and $C=(1-\nu)K_\ell+\nu I$ a spatial correlation with length scale $\ell$ and nugget
$\nu$. These are fitted on clean development data before anything else (H1,
Section~\ref{sec:setup:hyp}). A \emph{fault} is a regional deviation that \eqref{eq:meas} cannot
explain: a connected region of a view whose depth is wrong by many $\sigma$, typically on a
reflective or textureless surface. A whole-view affine error is absorbed by $(a_i,b_i)$ and is not a
fault. We make no claim about faults below the per-pixel noise.

\subsection{Surfels with an Uncertainty That Renders}
\label{sec:method:surfels}
Surfel $k$ has a \emph{shape} used for rendering only: centre $\boldsymbol\mu_k$, rotation
$\mathbf{R}_k=[\mathbf{t}_1\ \mathbf{t}_2\ \mathbf{n}]$ with normal $\mathbf{n}$, scales
$\mathbf{s}_k$ and opacity. Separately, it carries an \emph{uncertainty} over the perturbation
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol\theta_k=\bigl(\delta\boldsymbol\mu,\ \delta\boldsymbol\phi,\ \delta\log\mathbf{s}\bigr)\in\R^{9},
  \qquad \mathbf{R}_k\leftarrow\mathbf{R}_k\exp([\delta\boldsymbol\phi]_\times),
\end{equation}
kept in information form as a $9\times9$ block $\Lambda_k$. Keeping the two apart matters: if one
covariance served both, a confident surfel would shrink in the render and lose its weight.

Depth observes only the normal offset and the tilt of a surfel. With
$\nu=\mathbf{n}^{\top}\delta\boldsymbol\mu$ and the tangent components of the normal
perturbation, $\tau_1=\delta\phi_2$ and $\tau_2=-\delta\phi_1$, the sub-block is
$(\nu,\tau_1,\tau_2)=G_k\boldsymbol\theta_k$ with a fixed $3\times9$ map $G_k$. The ray of pixel $u$
of view $i$, $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{R}_i\mathbf{K}^{-1}[u\ v\ 1]^{\top}$, meets the surfel plane at the
z-depth $d_k=\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\boldsymbol\mu_k-\mathbf{c}_i)/\mathbf{n}^{\top}\mathbf{w}$ and the
point $\mathbf{x}$, and to first order
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \frac{\partial d_k}{\partial(\nu,\tau_1,\tau_2)}&=
  \frac{\bigl[\,1,\ -\mathbf{t}_1^{\top}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu_k),\ -\mathbf{t}_2^{\top}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu_k)\bigr]}{\mathbf{n}^{\top}\mathbf{w}},\\
  J_{uk}&=\frac{\partial d_k}{\partial(\nu,\tau_1,\tau_2)}\,G_k ,
\end{split}
  \label{eq:jac}
\end{equation}
The scale columns of $J_{uk}$ vanish (scales move the alpha weights, which are held fixed), so their
information comes from a weak prior (Supplement~\ref{sup:derivations}). The rasteriser composites
$\hat D_i(u)=\sum_k w_{uk}d_k$ with normalised alpha weights $w_{uk}$; the rendered depth variance is
\begin{equation}
  V_i(u)=\sum_k w_{uk}^{2}\,J_{uk}\,\Lambda_k^{-1}J_{uk}^{\top},
  \label{eq:var}
\end{equation}
which needs only the $3\times3$ marginal $G_k\Lambda_k^{-1}G_k^{\top}$ that the gate renders.

\subsection{Bayesian Fusion on Rendering and Leave-View-Out}
\label{sec:method:fusion}
Linearising the rendered depth at the fitted surfels, view $i$ adds to surfel $k$
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{k}^{(i)}=\sum_{u}\frac{a_i^{2}\,w_{uk}^{2}J_{uk}^{\top}J_{uk}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}(u)},\qquad
  \boldsymbol\eta_{k}^{(i)}=\sum_{u}\frac{a_i\,w_{uk}J_{uk}^{\top}e_i(u)}{\sigma_{i}^{2}(u)},
  \label{eq:info}
\end{equation}
with $e_i(u)=\hat d_i(u)-a_i\hat D_i(u)-b_i$, and the posterior is
$\Lambda_k=\Lambda_0+\sum_i\Lambda_k^{(i)}$, $\boldsymbol\eta_k=\sum_i\boldsymbol\eta_k^{(i)}$,
$\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}_k=\Lambda_k^{-1}\boldsymbol\eta_k$. This is the alpha-weighted
Gauss--Newton information of the rasteriser; it needs no correspondences, because each pixel
reaches the surfels it renders. Removing view $j$ is a subtraction,
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{k}^{(-j)}=\Lambda_k-\Lambda_k^{(j)},\qquad \boldsymbol\eta_{k}^{(-j)}=\boldsymbol\eta_k-\boldsymbol\eta_k^{(j)},
  \label{eq:loo}
\end{equation}
exact for the linearised model: the posterior without view $j$ does not depend on the
linearisation point in the directions the data observe (Supplement~\ref{sup:derivations}). The
statistic of a view against the rest equals the solution-separation statistic
\cite{flt_raim_joerger}, so leaving a view out and comparing it with the full solution are the same
test.

\subsection{The View Gate}
\label{sec:method:gate}
To test view $j$, the other views' surface is rendered into it. A surfel enters this render only if
the other views give it normal-offset information,
$\mathbf{g}^{\top}(\Lambda_k^{(-j)}-\Lambda_0)\mathbf{g}\ge\lambda_{\min}$ with
$\mathbf{g}=G_k^{\top}\mathbf{e}_1$; surfels that only view $j$ supports are dropped and the
compositing weights are recomputed, so the tested view spawns nothing and cannot hide its own fault
behind surfels it created. A pixel whose accumulated opacity after dropping is below $\tau_W$ is
\emph{untestable}: no other view sees it. On the testable pixels, with the leave-$j$-out depth
$\hat D_{-j}(u)$ and variance $V_{-j}(u)$ from \eqref{eq:var}, the alignment $(a_j,b_j)$ is fitted
by weighted least squares and the residual
\begin{equation}
  r_j(u)=\hat d_j(u)-\bigl(a_j\hat D_{-j}(u)+b_j\bigr),\qquad
  \Sigma_r=\Sigma_\varepsilon+a_j^{2}\,\mathrm{diag}\,V_{-j}
  \label{eq:res}
\end{equation}
is whitened. Two statistics are used: the energy $T_j=\mathbf{r}_j^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_j$, with $M$ the
precision $\Sigma_r^{-1}$ projected orthogonally to the alignment, which is $\chi^{2}_{n-2}$ under the
model; and a region scan $\max_R |z_R|$ over candidate regions $R$ (appearance segments of view $j$),
$z_R=\mathbf{g}_R^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_j/\sqrt{\mathbf{g}_R^{\top}M\mathbf{g}_R}$, with a Bonferroni
threshold. For a region bias $\boldsymbol\phi=\beta\mathbf{g}_R$ the matched statistic is
$\mathcal{N}(\beta\kappa,1)$ with $\kappa^{2}=\mathbf{g}_R^{\top}M\mathbf{g}_R$, so the minimum detectable
bias is $(z_\alpha+z_\beta)/\kappa$, as in Baarda's reliability theory. Thresholds are set for a 5\%
per-view false alarm and calibrated on clean development views, which also absorbs the
overconfidence of \eqref{eq:var} (H5). A view is kept, excluded, or reported untestable; a
scene-level alarm is raised if more than half of the views are excluded. Excluded views are
removed from the depth supervision and the surface is refitted. Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate}
summarises the gate.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Depth-only view gate}
\label{alg:gate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE fitted surfels, depth maps $\{\hat d_i\}$, poses, $\Sigma_\varepsilon$, thresholds
\FOR{each view $i$}
  \STATE rasterise; accumulate $\Lambda_k^{(i)},\boldsymbol\eta_k^{(i)}$ \hfill \eqref{eq:info}
\ENDFOR
\FOR{each view $j$}
  \STATE subtract view $j$ \hfill \eqref{eq:loo}
  \STATE drop surfels without other-view support; render $\hat D_{-j},V_{-j}$ \hfill \eqref{eq:var}
  \STATE mark untestable pixels; align; whiten \hfill \eqref{eq:res}
  \STATE keep, exclude or untestable by energy and region scan
\ENDFOR
\STATE refit the surfels without the excluded views
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Cost}
One rasterisation per view accumulates \eqref{eq:info}; the test of a view needs one more
rasterisation and a $9\times9$ solve per surfel it touches. The cost is linear in the number of views
and pixels and needs no correspondence search.

\paragraph{Approximation}
Each surfel has its own block. Cross-surfel correlations (pixels shared by overlapping surfels), the
correlation of depth error within a view and the shared pose error of a view are ignored, so the
rendered variance is overconfident; H5 measures by how much, and deflating each view's information by
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}/n$ is an ablation.
