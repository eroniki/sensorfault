\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, the manuscript provides its principal contribution: a view-level gate
that performs fault detection and exclusion (FDE) on the views, in the manner of integrity
monitoring, deciding which views' depth maps to keep before pose graph optimisation and
fusion and raising an alarm when it cannot decide.
One monocular metric depth model is applied to each RGB image, so each view has one
depth source. A pose graph couples the views through geometric and depth-induced
constraints. The gate tests each depth map \emph{once} against a single shared
Gaussian-splat surface, fitted from the other views and rendered at its view, instead of
against every overlapping view; it keeps the depth maps that agree with the surface and
passes only those to the graph and to fusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The surface is fitted
without the views it is tested on, so its cost grows with the number of views rather than
the number of view pairs, and the surface refitted on the kept depth maps is the fused
output. The gate, including this consensus, is the contribution; the surrounding pipeline
(Section~\ref{sec:method:pipeline}) is the test bed on which it is evaluated, and the use of
several models per view is an ablation (Section~\ref{sec:method:ensemble}).

\subsection{Definitions and Notation}
\label{sec:method:defs}
A static scene is observed by $N$ RGB images $I_i$, $i\in\mathcal{V}=\{1,\dots,N\}$, with
approximately known intrinsics $\mathbf{K}_i$ and unknown poses
$\mathbf{T}_i\in SE(3)$ (camera to world). A monocular metric depth model $f$ predicts a
depth map $D_i=f(I_i):\Omega_i\to\R_{>0}$ for each view; $D_i$ is the \emph{depth source}
of view $i$. Depth from a metric model is used through a per-view scale and shift
$\mathbf{a}_i=(s_i,t_i)$ with a prior at $(1,0)$, giving the aligned depth
$\tilde D_i=s_iD_i+t_i$; a model with affine-invariant output uses the same form without the
prior. A pose graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ has an edge $(i,j)$ for each
pair of views with enough feature matches and co-visibility. The gate outputs
$g_i\in\{0,1\}$ for each view, where $g_i=0$ means the depth map of view $i$ is excluded
from the depth constraints and from fusion; a view whose depth is excluded still takes part
in the graph through its geometric constraints. A \emph{shared surface} $\mathcal{S}_\theta$
is a set of $N_s$ Gaussians with means, covariances and opacities $\theta$; rendering it at
view $i$ with pose $\mathbf{T}_i$ gives a depth $\hat S_i(u)$, a depth variance
$v_{S,i}(u)$ and an accumulated alpha $\alpha_i(u)$ at each pixel. In the ensemble ablation,
$M$ models $f_m$ give $M$ depth maps $D_{m,i}$ per view, each of which is a source.

In integrity-monitoring terms, \emph{detection} decides whether any view is inconsistent
with the others, \emph{exclusion} sets $g_i=0$ for the view identified as faulty, and the
\emph{no-consensus alarm} is raised for a view that the other views cannot test, or for a
scene in which the test itself cannot be trusted. The \emph{false-alarm probability} $\alpha$
is the probability that a healthy view is excluded, and the \emph{missed-detection
probability} $\beta$ is the probability that a faulty view is kept.

A set $\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{V}$ of views is \emph{faulty}. A fault is \emph{coherent}
when it moves a whole view in a consistent way, and we parametrise its size by the
\emph{per-pixel bias} $\delta$, in units of the per-pixel noise $\sigma$, and by the
\emph{fraction} $f$ of the view's pixels that it affects. \emph{Depth faults} are
$\tilde D_i(u)=d_i(u)+\beta_i(u)+\varepsilon$ with a fault field $\beta_i$ that is not removed
by the scale-and-shift alignment. We consider four classes. \emph{Coherent bias:}
$\beta_i(u)=\delta\,\sigma(d_i(u))\,m_i(u)$ with a mask $m_i\in\{0,1\}$ that covers a fraction
$f$ of the pixels ($f=1$ is the whole view), the case of Section~\ref{sec:method:detection}.
\emph{Smooth warp:} the inverse depth is perturbed by a low-frequency field,
$1/D'_{i}=1/D_{i}+w_i(u)$. \emph{Region failure:} on a connected region covering a
fraction of the view, the depth is replaced by one that is unrelated to the true
geometry (for example a plane or a blurred copy), as for glass or sky.
\emph{Non-affine distortion:} $D'_{i}=D_{i}^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma\neq1$.
\emph{Pose faults} move the view instead of its depth: the believed pose is
$\tilde{\mathbf{T}}_i=\Delta\mathbf{T}_i\mathbf{T}_i$ with an unknown error
$\Delta\mathbf{T}_i$. We consider a \emph{spoofed pose prior}, a consistent or slowly drifting
offset of the pose prior (from GNSS or odometry) over a block of views; \emph{intermittent
pose glitches}, isolated views with large jumps of the prior; and \emph{false loop closures},
a spurious relative-pose constraint between two views that are not neighbours. A fault is
\emph{view-level} if it affects a subset of the views, either scattered or clustered in
neighbouring views, and \emph{model-level} if it affects all of them in the same way.
Neither $\mathcal{F}$ nor its size is known. The gate relies on the other views to expose a
faulty one, so we assume that faulty views are a minority in any region: the fraction of faulty
views among those that observe a region stays below one half, and a model-level fault is
undetectable by construction (Section~\ref{sec:disc:limits}). Scale-and-shift errors are not
faults in this sense, because the alignment, both in the gate and in the pose graph, removes
them; the gate targets the structure that alignment cannot remove. The goal is a trajectory
and a fused surface that are close to those obtained from the healthy views alone, together
with the identity of the excluded views and a statement of when this cannot be guaranteed.
The concrete magnitudes and the injection procedure are given in
Section~\ref{sec:setup:faults}.

\subsection{Forward Problem: Monocular Depth as a Measurement}
\label{sec:method:forward}
For a healthy view, the aligned depth equals the true depth $d_i(u)$ up to noise whose
spread grows with depth,
$\tilde D_{i}(u)=d_i(u)+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\sigma^{2}(d_i(u))\bigr)$,
with $\sigma(d)=\sigma_{0}+\kappa d$ fitted for the model on clean development data. When
a depth map is compared with the surface rendered at its view, the uncertainty of the pose
used for rendering adds a pointing error to the ray. For a ray of length $r$ that meets a
locally planar surface at incidence angle $\theta$ (the angle between ray and surface
normal), a range noise $\sigma_r=\sigma(r)$ and a pointing noise $\sigma_\varphi$ taken from
the rotational covariance of the pose give a depth \emph{residual} variance
\begin{equation}
  v_{\mathrm{res}}(r,\theta)=\sigma_r^{2}+\bigl(r\,\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta\bigr)^{2}.
  \label{eq:vres}
\end{equation}
This follows from $\mathrm{d}r/\mathrm{d}\theta=r\tan\theta$ for a plane at
perpendicular distance $h$, where $r=h/\cos\theta$ (Appendix~\ref{app:variance}); it is
the variance of the discrepancy between the measured depth and the intersection of the
nominal ray with the surface. Equation~\eqref{eq:vres} diverges at grazing incidence and
its linearisation fails once $\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta\gtrsim 0.3$, so we use the
regularised form
\begin{equation}
  \tilde v_{\mathrm{res}}=\operatorname{clip}\!\Bigl(\sigma_{r}^{2}
  +r^{2}\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}\,\tfrac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta+\epsilon^{2}},\;
  v_{\min},\,v_{\max}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:vreg}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon\approx\cos 80^{\circ}$ and a hard gate at $\theta>85^{\circ}$. It is not the
covariance of the back-projected 3D point: in the ray frame $(\ray,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})$
that covariance is $\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_r^2,(r\sigma_\varphi)^2,(r\sigma_\varphi)^2)$, and
its component along the surface normal,
\begin{equation}
  v_{n}(r,\theta)=\sigma_r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+r^{2}\sigma_\varphi^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,
  \label{eq:vn}
\end{equation}
stays bounded as $\theta\to\pi/2$. We therefore use \eqref{eq:vres} to weight depth
residuals and \eqref{eq:vn} to initialise splat covariances and to floor their scale
(Section~\ref{sec:method:surface}). Pixels that are not visible in the rendered view, or whose
incidence angle exceeds the gate, do not enter any comparison. Natural failures of the model
(reflective or transparent surfaces, sky, textureless regions) are not variance but
structured errors, and are the faults the gate is designed to catch.

A pose fault enters the same comparison through the same term. If the believed rotation of a
view is off by $\delta\varphi$, the depth rendered at the believed pose differs from the
depth seen by the camera by $\delta r\approx r\tan\theta\,\delta\varphi$ at each pixel, which
is the second term of \eqref{eq:vres} with $\sigma_\varphi$ replaced by the actual error; a
translation error adds a similar, depth-dependent shift. For $\delta\varphi$ of a fraction of
a degree these shifts are below the per-pixel noise on most pixels but are coherent across the
view, so a pose fault and a depth bias are the same kind of fault for the gate: both appear as
a consistent shift of the residual of a whole view, and the test of
Section~\ref{sec:method:detection} applies to both. Telling them apart is a separate
isolation step, discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc:limits}.

\subsection{Inverse Problem: Pose Graph Optimisation with Depth Maps}
\label{sec:method:inverse}
The poses and the alignments are estimated jointly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \min_{\{\mathbf{T}_i\},\{\mathbf{a}_{i}\}}\;
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\Bigl[&\rho_{g}\bigl(\|\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{geo}}_{ij}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_g}\bigr)\\
  &+g_{i}\,g_{j}\,
  \rho_{d}\bigl(\|\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{dep}}_{ij}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_d}\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{split}
  \label{eq:pgo}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{geo}}_{ij}$ are epipolar or reprojection residuals of the
feature matches between views $i$ and $j$, $\rho_g$ and $\rho_d$ are robust kernels
(a quadratic kernel when no robust baseline is used), and the depth-induced residual
back-projects a pixel $u$ of view $i$ with its aligned depth, transforms it into view
$j$, and compares its depth there with the depth of view $j$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  r^{\mathrm{dep}}_{ij}(u)&=\tilde D_{j}\bigl(\pi_j(\mathbf{q})\bigr)-q_z,\\
  \mathbf{q}&=\mathbf{T}_j^{-1}\mathbf{T}_i\,\pi_i^{-1}\bigl(u,\tilde D_{i}(u)\bigr).
\end{split}
  \label{eq:rdep}
\end{equation}
The weights $g_ig_j$ are where the gate acts: a depth constraint is built only from two
kept depth maps. These constraints are pairwise because the pose graph is, but they are
built after the gate, only from kept depth maps, and they are not part of the gate's cost
(Section~\ref{sec:method:cost}). The problem is \emph{partly end-to-end} in the following
sense: the matcher and the depth model are learned and kept fixed, while the back-end is a
differentiable least-squares solver \cite{opt_theseus} and the surface is renderable, so that
the depth residual of a view against the rendered surface, $\tilde D_i-\hat S_i(\mathbf{T}_i)$,
can be used as an additional, linear-cost constraint on $\mathbf{T}_i$ and gradients flow to
residual weights; in this paper the gate is training-free and the back-end is used as an
ordinary solver \cite{opt_g2o,opt_factorgraphs}. The differentiable, renderable optimiser is
the test bed the gate plugs into, not the framing. Its closest relative of the gate is
\emph{soft down-weighting}: replacing $g_i\in\{0,1\}$ by a weight $w_i\in[0,1]$ estimated
inside the optimisation from the view's residual. This is a baseline (H5): it reduces the
influence of a view but neither excludes it nor states a false-alarm or missed-detection
probability. \TODO{Author to confirm this

\subsection{View-Level Gate}
\label{sec:method:gate}

\subsubsection{Requirements}
Given evidence from the other views about the depth of a view, an exclusion rule should
(R1)~give the same answer for any ordering of the views; (R2)~respond to
\emph{disagreement} between a view and the others, not only to the view's own precision,
since a sharp but biased depth map must not be favoured; (R3)~return \emph{no consensus}
rather than guess when attribution is not identifiable, for example where fewer than three
other views observe the region; (R4)~keep the false-exclusion rate on fault-free data at a
stated level; (R5)~have a cost that grows with the number of views, not with the number
of view pairs; and (R6)~state its integrity: a false-alarm probability, a minimum detectable
bias for a stated missed-detection probability, and an alarm when these cannot be met. The entropy criterion of our earlier work does not meet (R1) and (R2) as
published (challenge C5), and a comparison with every overlapping view does not meet (R5)
when overlap is dense (challenge C6); this subsection gives a gate that meets all six.

\subsubsection{Shared-surface consensus by cross-fitting}
\label{sec:method:surface}
Instead of comparing a depth map with the depth maps of every overlapping view, the gate
compares it once with a single shared surface $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ rendered at the view. A
surface fitted to the view under test would absorb its fault, so the surface is
\emph{cross-fitted}: the views are split into $K$ folds of neighbouring views, and for each
fold $f$ a surface $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$ is fitted to the depth maps of the views outside $f$
and rendered at the views in $f$. A depth map is therefore never compared with a surface
that was fitted to it (challenge C2), and a block of neighbouring faulty views is held out
together, so it cannot support itself. The fit is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \min_{\theta,\{\mathbf{a}_{i}\}}\;&
  \sum_{i\notin f}\sum_{u}\rho\!\left(
  \frac{\bigl(\tilde D_{i}(u)-\hat S_i(u;\theta)\bigr)^{2}}{\tilde v_{\mathrm{res},i}(u)}\right)\\
  &+\lambda_\alpha L_\alpha(\theta)+\lambda_r R(\theta),
\end{split}
  \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
where $\hat S_i$ is the depth rendered from the current poses, $\rho$ is a robust kernel,
$L_\alpha$ penalises the accumulated alpha on valid and invalid pixels, and $R$ holds the
opacity and scale regularisers of 3DGS-MCMC \cite{gs_mcmc}. The number of splats $N_s$ is
fixed and dead splats are relocated to live ones rather than densified \cite{gs_mcmc};
this bounds the capacity with which the surface could add a second layer that absorbs a
faulty depth map (H6c). The fit uses depth only, so floaters are controlled by $L_\alpha$, a
cap on splat anisotropy, and the depth-distortion and normal-consistency terms of planar
splats \cite{gs_2dgs}, and the noise injected by the MCMC sampler is kept below the
normal-direction standard deviation from \eqref{eq:vn}. Splat covariances are initialised
from \eqref{eq:vn}. The rendered surface gives, at each pixel of a held-out view, the
depth $\hat S_i(u)$, a variance $v_{S,i}(u)$ (the spread of the splat depths along the
ray plus a floor from the splat scale) and the accumulated alpha $\alpha_i(u)$. The
alignment of a held-out depth map is fitted afterwards, by a robust affine fit to
$\hat S_i$ with the prior at $(1,0)$ for a metric model, so only the non-affine part of its
deviation remains.

\subsubsection{Visibility-aware residuals and statistic}
\label{sec:method:consensus}
A pixel $u$ of a held-out depth map enters the comparison only if it is valid, if the surface
supports it ($\alpha_i(u)\ge\tau_\alpha$; occlusion is handled by the depth-ordered
rendering), and if the incidence angle is below the grazing gate. With
$\Omega_{i}^{\mathrm{vis}}$ the set of such pixels, the normalised residual and the
per-view statistic are
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \rho_i(u)&=\frac{\tilde D_i(u)-\hat S_i(u)}{\sqrt{\tilde v_{\mathrm{res},i}(u)+v_{S,i}(u)}},\\
  T_i&=\frac{1}{|\Omega_i^{\mathrm{vis}}|}\sum_{u\in\Omega_i^{\mathrm{vis}}}\rho_i(u)^{2}.
\end{split}
  \label{eq:Ts}
\end{equation}
The signed mean $\bar\rho_i=|\Omega_i^{\mathrm{vis}}|^{-1}\sum_u\rho_i(u)$ is the second
statistic of the gate, and the one that detects a uniform bias
(Section~\ref{sec:method:detection}). Pixel residuals are spatially correlated, so the nominal
$\chi^2$ quantile is not used as a threshold; thresholds are calibrated on fault-free
development runs to a 5\% per-view false-exclusion rate (Section~\ref{sec:setup:protocol}). We also record
Moran's~I of $\rho_i$ as a diagnostic: a view-level fault leaves spatially structured
residuals, whereas sparse outliers do not. If too few pixels of a view are supported
($|\{u:\alpha_i(u)\ge\tau_\alpha\}|<s_{\min}P$), the surface cannot test the view and the
gate returns \emph{no consensus} for it.

\subsubsection{Detection statistics and minimum detectable bias}
\label{sec:method:detection}
Let $n=|\Omega_i^{\mathrm{vis}}|$ be the number of visible pixels and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}\le n$ the
number of effectively independent ones, estimated from the spatial autocorrelation of the
residuals on fault-free development data. For a healthy view the normalised residuals
$\rho_i(u)$ have zero mean and unit variance, so the \emph{mean-shift statistic}
$z_i=\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\bar\rho_i$ is approximately $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$, and the
\emph{energy statistic} $n_{\mathrm{eff}}T_i$ is approximately $\chi^2_{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$;
heavy tails and the remaining correlation are absorbed by the calibration of the threshold. A
view is flagged when $|z_i|>z_{\alpha}$, with $z_\alpha=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2)$ and false-alarm
probability $\alpha$ per view. A coherent bias of $\delta$ on a fraction $f$ of the pixels
shifts the mean of the standardised residual by $f\delta$, so $z_i\sim\mathcal{N}(f\delta\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}},1)$ and
the detection probability is $\Phi(f\delta\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}-z_\alpha)$ up to the far tail.
The \emph{minimum detectable bias} for a missed-detection probability $\beta$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{\min}=\frac{z_{\alpha}+z_{\beta}}{f\,\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}},\qquad z_\beta=\Phi^{-1}(1-\beta),
  \label{eq:mdb}
\end{equation}
the analogue of the minimum detectable bias of RAIM. For $\alpha=0.05$ and $\beta=0.1$,
$z_\alpha+z_\beta=3.24$, so with $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1000$ the gate detects $0.10\sigma$ when the
whole view is biased ($f=1$), $0.51\sigma$ when a fifth of it is, and $2.05\sigma$ when a
twentieth of it is. A zero-mean warp has $\bar\rho_i\approx0$ and is invisible to the mean
statistic, but not to the energy statistic, whose mean is $1+f\delta^2$ for a warp of rms
amplitude $\delta$, and whose minimum detectable amplitude scales as $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/4}$
instead of $n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/2}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:detection}); the gate uses both.

A pixel-wise filter flags a pixel when $|\rho_i(u)|>k$, with $k=3$ typically. Under the same
bias the flagging probability rises only from $2\Phi(-k)=0.0027$ to $\Phi(\delta-k)+\Phi(-\delta-k)$,
which is $0.0040$ at $\delta=0.3$ and, to second order, exceeds the null by $k\phi(k)\delta^{2}$:
a pixel at $0.3\sigma$ is practically indistinguishable from a healthy one. Even if the
flagged pixels were counted over the view, the resulting test statistic is smaller than
$z_i$ by a factor of about 12 at $\delta=0.3$ ($0.76f$ versus $9.5f$ for $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1000$),
because thresholding discards the sub-noise information, and the count overtakes the mean
only above $\delta\approx1.6$ (Appendix~\ref{app:detection}). The gate therefore wins when a
fault is sub-noise per pixel but coherent across the view, and pixel filters are the better
tool when the bias is large and confined to few pixels, which is what the regime map of H1
measures. The effective number of pixels is the weak point: a fault is detectable only if
$f\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ is large enough, and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ falls as the correlation of
the residuals rises.

\subsubsection{Corrected admission criterion}
\label{sec:method:admission}
In our earlier work \cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, each sensor $i$ has a PDF $f_i$ over a
common grid $\Sgrid$, the product over a consensus set $C$ is formed, and $C$ is grown
greedily from the best pair using an entropy test. Revisiting that work, the prose and
Eq.~(14) seed the set with the pair of \emph{maximum} entropy after fusion, whereas the
corresponding line of Algorithm~1 tests $h_{ij}\le h^{*}$ with $h^{*}$ initialised to
$-\infty$, so the seed branch never executes; the expansion test accepts a sensor when
entropy does not decrease, which is the opposite of the stated aim of sharpening the
joint PDF, and it depends on sensor order.

A more basic problem is that the entropy of a normalised product cannot detect a
biased source. For one-dimensional Gaussians $\mathcal{N}(m_C,\sigma_C^2)$ and
$\mathcal{N}(m_k,\sigma_k^2)$, the normalised product has variance
$\sigma_C^2\sigma_k^2/(\sigma_C^2+\sigma_k^2)$ regardless of the offset $m_C-m_k$, so
\begin{equation}
  H(C\cup k)-H(C)=-\tfrac12\log\!\bigl(1+\sigma_C^{2}/\sigma_k^{2}\bigr)<0
  \label{eq:dH}
\end{equation}
for every source, healthy or faulty; a sharp biased source lowers entropy the most.
Entropy measures the precision of a consensus, not its consistency.

We therefore test consistency directly. For a pixel $u$, the consensus is the rendered
surface, a Gaussian $p_{C,u}=\mathcal{N}(\hat S_i(u),v_{S,i}(u))$, and the depth map of view
$i$ has the PDF $f_{i,u}$ on an interval $\Sgrid_u$ of admissible depths. The view is
\emph{compatible} with $C$ at $u$ when its Bayes factor against a uniform null on $\Sgrid_u$
is at least one:
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{i,u}(C)=|\Sgrid_u|\int_{\Sgrid_u}p_{C,u}\, f_{i,u}\,\mathrm{d}z\;\ge\;1 .
  \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
For Gaussians, $\Lambda_{i,u}(C)\ge 1$ is equivalent to
$\tfrac12\nu^{2}/(v_S+\sigma_i^2)\le \log\!\bigl(|\Sgrid_u|/\sqrt{2\pi(v_S+\sigma_i^2)}\bigr)$
with $\nu=\hat S_i(u)-\tilde D_i(u)$, so the offset enters exponentially. A view is kept on
the \emph{mean log Bayes factor} over its visible pixels,
$\bar\ell_i(C)=|\Omega_i^{\mathrm{vis}}|^{-1}\sum_u\log\Lambda_{i,u}(C)\ge\tau$, where
$\tau=0$ is the nominal null and the calibrated $\tau$ absorbs the correlation of
pixels. The interval volume $|\Sgrid_u|$ is part of the test and must be fixed and
reported. Because every view is tested against a surface that does not depend on it, the
result does not depend on the order of the views (R1), and the gate returns \emph{no
consensus} when the surface does not support the view (R3). Algorithm~\ref{alg:admission}
summarises the gate.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Shared-surface view-level gate}
\label{alg:admission}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE views split into $K$ folds, depth maps $\{D_i\}$, provisional poses, threshold $\tau$, support $(\tau_\alpha,s_{\min})$
\FOR{each fold $f$}
  \STATE fit $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$ to the depth maps of views outside $f$ \hfill (fixed $N_s$, eq.~\eqref{eq:fit})
  \FOR{each view $i\in f$}
    \STATE render $\hat S_i,\;v_{S,i},\;\alpha_i$ from $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$
    \IF{supported pixels $<s_{\min}P$}
      \STATE $g_{i}\gets0$; \textbf{continue} \hfill (no consensus)
    \ENDIF
    \STATE fit the alignment of $D_i$ to $\hat S_i$; compute $\bar\ell_{i}$ on visible pixels
    \STATE $g_{i}\gets\mathbb{1}[\bar\ell_{i}\ge\tau]$
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\{g_{i}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Relation to leave-one-out and solution-separation tests]
\label{prop:loo}
Let source $k$ measure a state $x$ directly with noise covariance $R_k$, let
$\hat x_{-k}$ be the estimate without it with covariance $P_{-k}$, and let
$\nu=z_k-\hat x_{-k}$ with $S=R_k+P_{-k}$. Then the solution-separation statistic
$d^{\top}\mathrm{Cov}(d)^{-1}d$, with $d=\hat x_{\mathrm{all}}-\hat x_{-k}$,
equals the leave-one-out innovation statistic $\nu^{\top}S^{-1}\nu$. For Gaussian
PDFs the negative log-evidence of $k$ under the consensus,
$-\log\!\int p_C f_k=\tfrac12\nu^{\top}S^{-1}\nu+\tfrac12\log\det(2\pi S)$, is the
leave-one-out statistic plus a covariance-volume term.
\end{proposition}
(Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.)
\begin{remark}
Under equal covariances and Gaussian residuals, ranking depth maps by this evidence is
therefore the same as ranking by the leave-one-out chi-square statistic, and the
evidence criterion can add value only where noise is heteroscedastic (as depth noise is),
residuals are non-Gaussian or multimodal, or occlusion and grazing geometry break the
chi-square assumptions. We test exactly this in H2 (Section~\ref{sec:setup:hyp}) and treat
a tie as a reportable outcome.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Cost}
\label{sec:method:cost}
A pairwise gate warps the depth maps of the neighbouring views into the view under test
(Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise}). With $P$ pixels per view, mean co-visibility degree $\bar d$
and a warp cost $c_w$ per pixel, its cost is $C_{\mathrm{pair}}\approx N\bar d\,P\,c_w$,
which grows with the number of view pairs when overlap is dense ($\bar d\propto N$). The
shared-surface gate fits $K$ surfaces of $N_s$ splats for $n_{\mathrm{it}}$ iterations and
renders every view once, at a render cost $c_r$ per pixel,
$C_{\mathrm{surf}}\approx\bigl((K+1)\,n_{\mathrm{it}}+1\bigr)\,N\,P\,c_r$, which is linear
in $N$ for fixed $K$, $N_s$ and $n_{\mathrm{it}}$ (the $+1$ in $K+1$ is the final refit
below). The surface therefore wins only when overlap is dense: the break-even degree is
$\bar d^{*}\approx\bigl((K+1)\,n_{\mathrm{it}}+1\bigr)\,c_r/c_w$, and for sparse view graphs
the pairwise gate is cheaper. Depth inference adds $N$ model evaluations per scene, which
can be cached and which do not depend on the gate; with $M$ models the ensemble ablation
multiplies this term by $M$. H6 measures the scaling and the break-even point.

\subsubsection{Exclusion, no-consensus alarm and availability}
\label{sec:method:elim}
The gate returns $g_i=1$ for kept depth maps and $0$ for excluded ones, and the excluded view
is removed from the depth constraints and from fusion; it still takes part in the pose graph
through its geometric constraints. Two alarms are raised. A \emph{no-consensus} alarm is raised
for a view that the other views cannot test, because the surface does not support it, or
because too few other views observe the region; the view is then not trusted, which is the
analogue of a loss of integrity monitoring for a satellite that is not covered by the redundancy.
A \emph{scene-level} alarm is raised if more than half of the views are rejected: the gate
cannot then tell a failing model from a surface that is itself wrong, and the result should not
be relied on. \emph{Availability} is the fraction of views that are tested and kept, and it is
reported next to the false-alarm rate, because a monitor that excludes too much is also
unusable. After pose graph optimisation, the surface is refitted once on all kept depth maps at
the refined poses; this surface is the fused output. Excluded views are reported in clusters of
neighbouring views, because region-specific failures (a glass surface seen from many views)
produce runs of rejections.

\subsubsection{Protection level on the fused output (conditional)}
\label{sec:method:pl}
Integrity monitoring does not stop at detection: a protection level bounds the error in the
output that an undetected fault can cause. For a scalar output $q$ of the reconstruction, for
example the fused depth at a query pixel or one coordinate of a camera position, linearise its
response to a fault of view $i$ of size $\delta_i\sigma$ on a fraction $f_i$ of the pixels as
$\Delta q=s_i\delta_i$, where the sensitivity $s_i$ is estimated by perturbing the view and
refitting. If at most one view is faulty, a fault larger than $\delta_{\min,i}$ of
\eqref{eq:mdb} is excluded with probability at least $1-\beta$, and the remaining error is
bounded by
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PL}_q=\max_i\,|s_i|\,\delta_{\min,i}\,\sigma+z_q\,\sigma_q,
  \label{eq:pl}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_q$ is the standard deviation of $q$ under the healthy fit and $z_q$ a Gaussian
quantile. This has the structure of a RAIM protection level, a slope times a minimum
detectable bias plus a noise term \cite{flt_araim_pl_exclusion}, with an important difference:
RAIM allocates integrity risk using prior fault probabilities, which are unknown for learned
depth, so \eqref{eq:pl} is \emph{conditional on a fault being present}. It rests on four
assumptions, each of which fails in a regime we test: (A1) at most one faulty view, (A2)
$\sigma$ and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ calibrated on clean data, (A3) a linear response for faults of
the size of the minimum detectable bias, and (A4) a surface that is not itself corrupted, which
fails for model-level faults. We therefore state \eqref{eq:pl} as a bound whose coverage is
tested (H9), not as a guarantee, and it is the first item cut if the schedule slips.

\subsection{Ensemble Variant (Ablation)}
\label{sec:method:ensemble}
The ensemble ablation gives each view $M$ depth maps $D_{m,i}$ from $M$ models and asks two
questions: whether models that share training data fail on the same views, and whether
voting across models breaks where the gate does not. Cross-model voting takes, at each
pixel, the median (or majority) of the $M$ aligned depth maps and flags a view when the
models disagree beyond their noise; it needs no other view. The ensemble gate tests each of
the $M$ depth maps of a view against the same surface rendered at the view, so that
$g_{m,i}$ is defined per model and a view is dropped when every model is rejected or when
the kept models disagree. Both are evaluated by H7 (Section~\ref{sec:setup:hyp}): they
should differ most on views where several models fail together, since cross-model voting then
agrees with itself, whereas the surface is built from other views.

\subsection{Test Bed: Differentiable, Renderable Pose Graph}
\label{sec:method:pipeline}
The test bed runs five steps. (i)~\emph{Depth and matches:} each view is processed by the
depth model, and feature matches are computed for view pairs. (ii)~\emph{Provisional poses:}
a pose graph with the geometric constraints only is solved with a robust kernel, to give
poses that are good enough to render a surface at each view. (iii)~\emph{Gate:}
Section~\ref{sec:method:gate}: cross-fit the shared surface and test every depth map against
its rendering. (iv)~\emph{Pose graph optimisation:} \eqref{eq:pgo}, with gated depth
constraints and, optionally, a robust kernel, a soft per-view weight (baseline) or the
render-based constraint of Section~\ref{sec:method:inverse}. (v)~\emph{Fused output:} the surface is
refitted on the kept depth maps at the refined poses; its rendered depth is the fused depth,
and a truncated signed distance function \cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion} of the same
kept depth is a secondary check. The gate may be repeated once with the refined poses.
Because the gate relies on provisional poses, errors in those poses can corrupt both the
surface and the gate; the repetition, and the comparison of gating before versus after the
pose graph (H4), test this. The baseline families replace or complement step (iii)
(Section~\ref{sec:setup:baselines}).
