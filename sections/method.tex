\subsection{Setting, Measurement Model and Faults}
\label{sec:method:setting}
A static scene is observed by $N$ RGB images $I_i$ with known intrinsics. A monocular metric depth
model gives one depth map $\hat d_i$ per image. The surface is a set of 2DGS-style surfels
\cite{gs_2dgs} fitted by depth-supervised splatting and rendered with gsplat \cite{gs_gsplat} into
expected depth, alpha weights and accumulated opacity. Colour is a separate appearance block: the
photometric loss updates the colours and may refine the poses, but its gradients are stopped at the
surfel geometry and opacity, so colour reaches the map geometry only through the depth model. The
gate uses depth only. Poses (camera centre $\mathbf{c}_i$, rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$) start from the
dataset and are refined by feature matches and photometric refinement over all frames; the pose of a
tested view never uses that view's own depth, since a pose fitted to it would absorb its fault
(Supplement~\ref{sup:setup}). Pose faults are out of scope.

The nominal measurement of pixel $u$ in view $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \hat d_i(u)&=a_i\bigl(d_i(u)+\beta\,d_i(u)^{2}\bigr)+b_i+\varepsilon_i(u),\\
  \boldsymbol\varepsilon_i&\sim\mathcal{N}\bigl(\mathbf{0},\ \gamma^{2}DCD\bigr),
\end{split}
  \label{eq:meas}
\end{equation}
with a per-view scale and offset $(a_i,b_i)$ absorbed by alignment, a \emph{shared distance bias}
$\beta$ common to all views, and a noise covariance whose shape is calibrated offline on development
data: $D=\mathrm{diag}\,\sigma(\text{range},\text{texture},\text{incidence})$, and
$C=(1-\nu_{\mathrm{nug}})K_\ell+\nu_{\mathrm{nug}}I$ is a spatial correlation with length scale $\ell$ and nugget $\nu_{\mathrm{nug}}$. The only
free noise variable is the scene temperature $\gamma$, in the sense of temperature scaling
\cite{cal_guo_temperature}; there are no per-view or per-pixel noise parameters, because any noise
parameter fitted to a view's own residual would absorb that view's fault. Further shared bias terms
(grazing angle, texture) are added only if the development residuals show them. A \emph{fault} is a
regional deviation that \eqref{eq:meas} cannot explain: a connected region of a view whose depth is
wrong by many $\sigma$, typically on a reflective or textureless surface. A whole-view affine error is
absorbed by $(a_i,b_i)$ and is not a fault.

\subsection{Surfels with an Uncertainty That Renders}
\label{sec:method:surfels}
Surfel $k$ has a \emph{shape} used for rendering only: centre $\boldsymbol\mu_k$, rotation
$\mathbf{R}_k=[\mathbf{t}_1\ \mathbf{t}_2\ \mathbf{n}]$ with normal $\mathbf{n}$, scales
$\mathbf{s}_k$ and opacity $\alpha_k$. Separately, it carries an \emph{uncertainty} over the
perturbation
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \boldsymbol\theta_k&=\bigl(\delta\boldsymbol\mu,\ \delta\boldsymbol\phi,\ \delta\log\mathbf{s},\ \delta\,\mathrm{logit}\,\alpha\bigr)\in\R^{10},\\
  \mathbf{R}_k&\leftarrow\mathbf{R}_k\exp([\delta\boldsymbol\phi]_\times),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
kept in information form as a $10\times10$ block $\Lambda_k$. Keeping shape and uncertainty apart
matters: if one covariance served both, a confident surfel would shrink in the render and lose its
weight. Opacity is trained during a warm-up and then frozen (Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate}); its
coordinate is kept for the support rule of Section~\ref{sec:method:gate} and is not re-estimated
when a view is left out.

Depth observes the normal offset and the tilt of a surfel. With
$\nu=\mathbf{n}^{\top}\delta\boldsymbol\mu$ and the tangent components of the normal
perturbation, $\tau_1=\delta\phi_2$ and $\tau_2=-\delta\phi_1$, the sub-block is
$(\nu,\tau_1,\tau_2)=G_k\boldsymbol\theta_k$ with a fixed $3\times10$ map $G_k$. The ray of pixel $u$
of view $i$, $\mathbf{w}=\mathbf{R}_i\mathbf{K}^{-1}[u\ v\ 1]^{\top}$, meets the surfel plane at the
z-depth $d_k=\mathbf{n}^{\top}(\boldsymbol\mu_k-\mathbf{c}_i)/\mathbf{n}^{\top}\mathbf{w}$ and the
point $\mathbf{x}$, and to first order
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \frac{\partial d_k}{\partial(\nu,\tau_1,\tau_2)}&=
  \frac{\bigl[\,1,\ -\mathbf{t}_1^{\top}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu_k),\ -\mathbf{t}_2^{\top}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol\mu_k)\bigr]}{\mathbf{n}^{\top}\mathbf{w}},\\
  J_{uk}&=\frac{\partial d_k}{\partial(\nu,\tau_1,\tau_2)}\,G_k .
\end{split}
  \label{eq:jac}
\end{equation}
Opacity acts through the compositing weights: the rasteriser's backward pass gives
$\partial\hat D_i(u)/\partial\,\mathrm{logit}\,\alpha_k$, including the transmittance of the surfels
behind $k$. The scale columns vanish, so the scales keep a weak prior
(Supplement~\ref{sup:derivations}). The rasteriser composites $\hat D_i(u)=\sum_k w_{uk}d_k$ with
normalised alpha weights $w_{uk}$; with opacity frozen, the rendered depth variance is
\begin{equation}
  V_i(u)=\sum_k w_{uk}^{2}\,J_{uk}\,P_k\,J_{uk}^{\top},
  \label{eq:var}
\end{equation}
with $P_k$ the inverse of $\Lambda_k$ restricted to the nine geometric coordinates; only the $3\times3$
marginal of $(\nu,\tau_1,\tau_2)$ enters.

\subsection{Bayesian Fusion on Rendering and Leave-View-Out}
\label{sec:method:fusion}
At the fitted surfels (the MAP), view $i$ adds to surfel $k$ the Laplace information
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \Lambda_{k}^{(i)}&=\sum_{u}\frac{a_i^{2}\,\mathbf{g}_{uk}^{\top}\mathbf{g}_{uk}}{\gamma^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}(u)},\qquad
  \boldsymbol\eta_{k}^{(i)}=\sum_{u}\frac{a_i\,\mathbf{g}_{uk}^{\top}e_i(u)}{\gamma^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}(u)},\\
  \mathbf{g}_{uk}&=\Bigl[w_{uk}J_{uk},\ \frac{\partial\hat D_i(u)}{\partial\,\mathrm{logit}\,\alpha_k}\Bigr],
\end{split}
  \label{eq:info}
\end{equation}
with $e_i(u)$ the residual of view $i$ at the MAP, so that $\boldsymbol\eta_k^{(i)}=-\nabla_k\mathcal{L}_i$, and $\Lambda_k=\Lambda_0+\sum_i\Lambda_k^{(i)}$. This is the alpha-weighted Gauss--Newton information of
the rasteriser; it needs no correspondences, because each pixel reaches the surfels it renders.
Leaving view $j$ out is a subtraction plus one Newton step on the mean,
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{k}^{(-j)}=\Lambda_k-\Lambda_k^{(j)},\qquad
  \boldsymbol\mu_{k}^{(-j)}=\boldsymbol\mu_k+\bigl(\Lambda_{k}^{(-j)}\bigr)^{-1}\nabla_{k}\mathcal{L}_j(\boldsymbol\mu),
  \label{eq:loo}
\end{equation}
taken on the nine geometric coordinates, with $\mathcal{L}_j$ the depth loss of view $j$. Both
$\Lambda^{(j)}$ and $\nabla\mathcal{L}_j$ come from one backward pass of view $j$, so nothing per view
and surfel has to be stored. The step is the infinitesimal jackknife; it is exact for the linear
Gaussian model (Supplement~\ref{sup:derivations}). The statistic of a view against the rest equals the
solution-separation statistic \cite{flt_raim_joerger}. The shared bias $\beta$ is a weighted least-squares
fit over all views, and its leave-view-out value is again a subtraction of view $j$'s normal equations.

\paragraph{A message-passing reading}
Within each round, train, freeze, fuse, test (Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate}) can be read as message passing
among views. In the fuse step the views share information: each view sends the surfels it renders an information-form
message, its alpha-weighted $\Lambda_k^{(i)}=\sum_u J^{\top}R^{-1}J$ and the matching vector
$\boldsymbol\eta_k^{(i)}$ of \eqref{eq:info}, and a surfel's belief is the product of its prior and the
messages it receives. Leaving view $j$ out by subtraction, \eqref{eq:loo}, is the cavity step of
expectation propagation \cite{mp_minka_ep}: what remains is the cavity, the belief of all other views,
and the gate tests view $j$ against that cavity rendered into view $j$. This is a framing of a known
identity, not a contribution, and it is not full expectation propagation: we take one exact Gaussian
subtraction at the MAP and run no moment-matching iterations. The loop parallels the optimisation loop
of vanilla 3DGS, which fits every view's rendering by gradient steps; the difference is the explicit
exchange of information between views, which gives both the fused uncertainty and the leave-view-out
reference. Gaussian belief propagation for spatial AI \cite{mp_davison_gbp}, which passes Gaussian
messages on factor graphs, is the closest relative in vision.

\subsection{The View Gate}
\label{sec:method:gate}
To test view $j$, the other views' surface is rendered into it. A surfel enters this render only if
(i)~the other views give it normal-offset information,
$\mathbf{e}_1^{\top}G_k(\Lambda_k^{(-j)}-\Lambda_0)G_k^{\top}\mathbf{e}_1\ge\lambda_{\min}$, and (ii)~view
$j$ supplied at most a fraction $\rho$ (one half) of its opacity information,
$\Lambda_k^{(j)}[\alpha,\alpha]/\Lambda_k[\alpha,\alpha]\le\rho$. Surfels that fail either rule were made
or shaped by view $j$; they are dropped and the compositing weights are recomputed, so the tested view
spawns nothing and cannot hide its fault behind surfels it created. A pixel whose accumulated opacity
after dropping is below $\tau_W$ is \emph{untestable}: no other view sees it. On the testable pixels,
with the leave-$j$-out depth $\hat D_{-j}(u)$ and variance $V_{-j}(u)$ from \eqref{eq:var}, the
alignment $(a_j,b_j)$ is fitted by weighted least squares and the residual
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  r_j(u)&=\hat d_j(u)-\bigl(a_j\hat D_{-j}(u)+b_j\bigr),\\
  \Sigma_r&=\hat\gamma_{-j}^{2}DCD+a_j^{2}\,\mathrm{diag}\,V_{-j}+J_q\Sigma_qJ_q^{\top}
\end{split}
  \label{eq:res}
\end{equation}
is whitened, with $\Sigma_q$ the marginal covariance of view $j$'s pose, estimated without its depth,
and $J_q$ the Jacobian of the rendered depth with respect to that pose (the shared bias term is part
of the rendered prediction and is omitted from the notation). The scene temperature is fitted without view $j$: $\hat\gamma_{-j}^{2}$ solves
$\mathrm{median}_{i\ne j}\,T_i(\gamma)/q_{0.5}(\chi^{2}_{m_i})=1$, a one-dimensional root, with $T_i$
the energy statistic of view $i$ and $m_i$ its degrees of freedom. A minority of faulty views can only
raise $\hat\gamma$, which makes the test conservative; a $\hat\gamma$ beyond its development range
raises a scene-level alarm.

Two statistics are used. The energy $T_j=\mathbf{r}_j^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_j$, with $M$ the precision
$\Sigma_r^{-1}$ projected orthogonally to the alignment, has $m_j$ degrees of freedom based on
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathrm{tr}(C)^{2}/\mathrm{tr}(C^{2})$, not on the pixel count. With $m$ of order $10^{3}$
it is as much a test of the noise level as of faults: a clean view whose noise is 2\% above the scene
temperature already has a 23\% false-alarm rate at a nominal 5\% (Supplement~\ref{sup:derivations}).
Its threshold therefore absorbs the per-view heterogeneity $\omega$ of the noise level, measured on
development views after temperature scaling. The region scan $\max_{\mathcal{R}}|z_{\mathcal{R}}|$ over candidate regions $\mathcal{R}$
(appearance segments of view $j$),
$z_{\mathcal{R}}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_j/\sqrt{\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\top}M\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{R}}}$, with a Bonferroni
threshold, is barely affected by $\omega$; it leads if $\omega$ is large. For a region bias
$\boldsymbol\phi=\delta\,\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{R}}$ the matched statistic is $\mathcal{N}(\delta\kappa,1)$ with
$\kappa^{2}=\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\top}M\mathbf{g}_{\mathcal{R}}$, so the minimum detectable bias at missed-detection
probability $\beta_{\mathrm{md}}$ is $(z_\alpha+z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}})/\kappa$,
as in Baarda's reliability theory \cite{stat_teunissen_dia}. Thresholds are set for a 5\% per-view
false alarm and calibrated on clean development views (H5). A view is kept, flagged, or reported
untestable; flagged views are handled by the outer loop below.

\subsection{The Outer Loop}
\label{sec:method:loop}
A single pass of training, freezing, fusing and testing leaves the geometry shaped by every view,
including the faulty ones. We therefore repeat it in rounds (Algorithm~\ref{alg:gate}). Each round
trains on the active views, with opacity unfrozen again and densification and pruning back on,
warm-started from the previous round; then it freezes opacity, fuses, and tests every active view
against its cavity. The views the gate flags are dropped, and the next round trains without them.
The loop stops when a round flags no view, when the round cap $R_{\max}$ is reached, or when more
than half of the views are excluded (scene-level alarm). Gradients run only inside the train step; the
fuse and test steps are not differentiated, so fusion need not be differentiable.

\paragraph{False-alarm control across rounds}
A fixed $\alpha$ per round would let the false-alarm rate grow with the number of rounds. Instead the
Holm step-down schedule runs over a family that counts every test the loop may perform: each of the
$N$ views once in each of up to $R_{\max}$ rounds, $M=N R_{\max}$ tests, re-tests of the same view in later
rounds included. The $(k{+}1)$-th rejection overall uses the threshold $\alpha/(M-k)$; within a round the
p-values are sorted and compared step-down, continuing the global count $k$, and the first failure
ends the round's rejections. Because $M$ is fixed before the loop starts, the union bound holds over
all rounds; the step-down relaxation is Holm's, which is exact for a fixed set of p-values, whereas
here the p-values change as the surface is retrained, so the level of the whole loop is calibrated by
clean-run permutation, as the single-pass threshold is.

\paragraph{Defaults and risks}
By default a round drops only the most significant rejected view, there is no readmission, and
$R_{\max}=10$. Dropping one view at a time guards against \emph{swamping}, in which a clean view looks
faulty only because a faulty view still shapes the surface; it is resolved when the next round
retrains without the faulty view. Retraining also resolves \emph{masking}, in which a faulty view
passes while other faulty views in the same region support it, as long as the faulty views are a
minority there. Without readmission the loop is monotone and terminates; a swamped clean view that was
dropped is not recovered, which the one-view-per-round default makes rare. Readmission, dropping all
rejected views per round, down-weighting instead of dropping, and a single pass (loop off) are
ablations (H6). If the cap is reached while views are still being flagged, an alarm is raised. The
loop also removes a risk of the single pass: a surfel that a faulty view caused to be pruned during
the warm-up can return once that view is caught, because the next round re-densifies without it.

\paragraph{Known ideas}
The loop is Baarda-style iterative data snooping \cite{stat_teunissen_dia}, which tests, removes the
most significant observation and re-adjusts, and it is hard expectation--maximisation
\cite{stat_dempster_em}: the gate is the E-step that sets each view's fault indicator, and training is
the M-step that refits the geometry given those indicators. Within each round the message-passing
reading of Section~\ref{sec:method:fusion} applies unchanged. None of this is claimed as a contribution.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Train, freeze, fuse, test (outer loop)}
\label{alg:gate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE cached depth maps $\{\hat d_i\}$; poses; offline noise shape $\sigma(\cdot),\ell,\nu_{\mathrm{nug}}$; $\alpha$; $R_{\max}$
\STATE active views $\mathcal{A}\gets$ all $N$ views; rejections $k\gets0$; family $M\gets NR_{\max}$
\FOR{round $r=1,\dots,R_{\max}$}
  \STATE \textbf{train:} on $\mathcal{A}$, warm-started; $\boldsymbol\mu,\mathbf{R},\mathbf{s},\alpha$ on depth (opacity unfrozen); $(a_i,b_i)$ closed form; $\beta$ by least squares; densify only with $\ge2$ supporting views; prune
  \STATE \textbf{freeze} opacity; stop densification and pruning; fit $\boldsymbol\mu,\mathbf{R},\mathbf{s}$ and poses to convergence; colour on the frozen geometry
  \STATE \textbf{fuse:} accumulate $\Lambda_k$ from the views in $\mathcal{A}$ at the MAP \hfill \eqref{eq:info}
  \FOR{each view $j\in\mathcal{A}$ \textbf{(test)}}
    \STATE backward pass of $j$: $\Lambda^{(j)}$, $\nabla\mathcal{L}_j$; cavity by Newton leave-out \hfill \eqref{eq:loo}
    \STATE drop surfels by the support rules; render $\hat D_{-j},V_{-j}$ \hfill \eqref{eq:var}
    \STATE $\hat\gamma_{-j}$; mark untestable pixels; align; whiten; p-value $p_j$ \hfill \eqref{eq:res}
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE Holm step-down on the sorted $p_j$ with thresholds $\alpha/(M-k),\alpha/(M-k-1),\dots$
  \IF{no view is rejected}
    \STATE \textbf{stop}
  \ENDIF
  \STATE drop the most significant rejected view from $\mathcal{A}$; $k\gets k+1$
  \IF{more than $N/2$ views are excluded}
    \STATE scene-level alarm; \textbf{stop}
  \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE alarm if $R_{\max}$ was reached with a rejection; the last fit on $\mathcal{A}$ is the output
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Cost}
Each round costs one training run, warm-started from the previous round, plus the tests. The test of a
view costs one backward pass, one render and one solve on the nine geometric coordinates of each surfel
it touches, so it is linear in the number of views and pixels and needs no correspondence search; the
depth maps are computed once and cached. With one view dropped per round, the number of rounds is the
number of excluded views plus one, capped at $R_{\max}$.

\paragraph{Approximation}
Each surfel has its own block. Cross-surfel correlations (pixels shared by overlapping surfels), the
correlation of depth error within a view and the shared pose error of a view are ignored, so the
rendered variance is overconfident. This stays a stated limitation; H5 measures it, and deflating each
view's information by $n_{\mathrm{eff}}/n$ or inflating the variance by a jackknife are ablations.
Within a round, opacity frozen after the warm-up has a residual risk: a surfel that a not-yet-caught view
caused to be pruned cannot be restored by any leave-out. The outer loop removes this risk for views it
catches; a fold-wise warm-up is the ablation that measures what remains.
