\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, the manuscript provides its principal contribution: a view-level gate
that performs fault detection and exclusion (FDE) on the views, in the manner of integrity
monitoring, deciding which views' depth maps to keep before pose graph optimisation and
fusion and raising an alarm when it cannot decide.
One monocular metric depth model is applied to each RGB image, so each view has one
depth source. A pose graph couples the views through geometric and depth-induced
constraints. The gate tests each depth map \emph{once} against a single shared
Gaussian-splat surface, fitted from the other views and rendered at its view, instead of
against every overlapping view; it keeps the depth maps that agree with the surface and
passes only those to the graph and to fusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The surface is fitted
without the views it is tested on, so its cost grows with the number of views rather than
the number of view pairs, and the surface refitted on the kept depth maps is the fused
output. The gate, including this consensus, is the contribution; the surrounding pipeline
(Section~\ref{sec:method:pipeline}) is the test bed on which it is evaluated, and the use of
several models per view is an ablation (Section~\ref{sec:method:ensemble}).

\subsection{Definitions and Notation}
\label{sec:method:defs}
A static scene is observed by $N$ RGB images $I_i$, $i\in\mathcal{V}=\{1,\dots,N\}$, with
approximately known intrinsics $\mathbf{K}_i$ and unknown poses
$\mathbf{T}_i\in SE(3)$ (camera to world). A monocular metric depth model $f$ predicts a
depth map $D_i=f(I_i):\Omega_i\to\R_{>0}$ for each view; $D_i$ is the \emph{depth source}
of view $i$. Depth from a metric model is used through a per-view scale and shift
$\mathbf{a}_i=(s_i,t_i)$ with a prior at $(1,0)$, giving the aligned depth
$\tilde D_i=s_iD_i+t_i$; a model with affine-invariant output uses the same form without the
prior. A pose graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ has an edge $(i,j)$ for each
pair of views with enough feature matches and co-visibility. The gate outputs
$g_i\in\{0,1\}$ for each view, where $g_i=0$ means the depth map of view $i$ is excluded
from the depth constraints and from fusion; a view whose depth is excluded still takes part
in the graph through its geometric constraints. A \emph{shared surface} $\mathcal{S}_\theta$
is a set of $N_s$ Gaussians with means, covariances and opacities $\theta$; rendering it at
view $i$ with pose $\mathbf{T}_i$ gives a depth $\hat S_i(u)$, a depth variance
$v_{S,i}(u)$ and an accumulated alpha $\alpha_i(u)$ at each pixel. In the ensemble ablation,
$M$ models $f_m$ give $M$ depth maps $D_{m,i}$ per view, each of which is a source.

In integrity-monitoring terms, \emph{detection} decides whether any view is inconsistent
with the others, \emph{exclusion} sets $g_i=0$ for the view identified as faulty, and the
\emph{no-consensus alarm} is raised for a view that the other views cannot test, or for a
scene in which the test itself cannot be trusted. The \emph{false-alarm probability} $\alpha$
is the probability that a healthy view is excluded, and the \emph{missed-detection
probability} $\beta$ is the probability that a faulty view is kept.

A set $\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{V}$ of views is \emph{faulty}. A \emph{fault} is any
deviation of a view from the nominal measurement model of Section~\ref{sec:method:forward}
that the model cannot explain, and a fault is \emph{coherent} when it moves a whole view in a
consistent way. Its size is parametrised by the \emph{per-pixel bias} $\delta$, in units of the
per-pixel noise $\sigma$, and by the \emph{fraction} $f$ of the view's pixels that it affects.
Depth faults add a field $\phi_i(u)$ to the measurement. We consider four classes.
\emph{Coherent bias:} $\phi_i(u)=\delta\,\sigma(u)\,m_i(u)$ with a mask $m_i\in\{0,1\}$ that
covers a fraction $f<1$ of the pixels; for $f=1$ it is a uniform bias, which the scale and
offset of the model absorb and which is therefore not a fault (Section~\ref{sec:method:faultdef}).
\emph{Smooth warp:} the inverse depth is perturbed by a low-frequency field,
$1/D'_{i}=1/D_{i}+w_i(u)$, a fault to the extent that it is not affine. \emph{Region failure:}
on a connected region covering a fraction of the view, the depth is replaced by one that is
unrelated to the true geometry (for example a plane or a blurred copy), as for glass or sky.
\emph{Non-affine distortion:} $D'_{i}=D_{i}^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma\neq1$.
\emph{Pose faults} move the view instead of its depth: the believed pose is
$\tilde{\mathbf{T}}_i=\Delta\mathbf{T}_i\mathbf{T}_i$ with an error $\Delta\mathbf{T}_i$ beyond
what the pose covariance of the model predicts. We consider a \emph{spoofed pose prior}, a
consistent or slowly drifting offset of the pose prior (from GNSS or odometry) over a block of
views; \emph{intermittent pose glitches}, isolated views with large jumps of the prior; and
\emph{false loop closures}, a spurious relative-pose constraint between two views that are not
neighbours. A fault is \emph{view-level} if it affects a subset of the views, either scattered or
clustered in neighbouring views, and \emph{model-level} if it affects all of them in the same
way. Neither $\mathcal{F}$ nor its size is known. The gate relies on the other views to expose a
faulty one, so we assume that faulty views are a minority in any region: the fraction of faulty
views among those that observe a region stays below one half, and a model-level fault is
undetectable by construction (Section~\ref{sec:disc:limits}). The goal is a trajectory and a
fused surface that are close to those obtained from the healthy views alone, together with the
identity of the excluded views and a statement of when this cannot be guaranteed. The concrete
magnitudes and the injection procedure are given in Section~\ref{sec:setup:faults}.

\subsection{Measurement Model}
\label{sec:method:forward}
The gate is derived from an explicit model of what a healthy view looks like, so that a fault
can be defined as a deviation that this model cannot explain. This subsection states the model,
lists its sources of uncertainty, propagates them to the residual against the rendered surface,
and shows how the test statistics and the effective number of pixels follow from the resulting
covariance instead of being assumed.

\subsubsection{Nominal measurement}
\label{sec:method:nominal}
Let $u$ index the pixels of view $i$ and $d_i(u)$ be the true depth along the pixel ray. The
model's output is
\begin{equation}
  \hat d_i(u)=a_i\,d_i(u)+b_i+\varepsilon_i(u),\qquad
  \boldsymbol\varepsilon_i\sim\mathcal{N}\bigl(\mathbf{0},\,\Sigma_\varepsilon\bigr),
  \label{eq:meas}
\end{equation}
where $a_i$ and $b_i$ are the monocular scale and offset ambiguity, with a prior around
$(1,0)$ for a metric model and none for an affine-invariant one, and the error field is
heteroscedastic and spatially correlated,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \Sigma_\varepsilon&=D\,C\,D,\qquad D=\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\sigma(r_u,\tau_u,\theta_u)\bigr),\\
  C&=(1-\nu)\,K_\ell+\nu I,
\end{split}
  \label{eq:sigeps}
\end{equation}
with $K_\ell(u,v)=\exp(-\|u-v\|^{2}/2\ell^{2})$ a spatial correlation kernel of length scale
$\ell$ (in pixels) and a nugget $\nu$, the fraction of the variance that is independent from
pixel to pixel. The standard deviation $\sigma(r,\tau,\theta)$ depends on the range $r$, on the
image texture $\tau$ (for example the local gradient energy) and on the incidence angle
$\theta$, for instance $\log\sigma=\gamma_0+\gamma_1\log r+\gamma_2 g(\tau)+\gamma_3\tan^{2}\theta$
with $g$ decreasing; any positive function fitted on clean data will do. The pose used to
render the surface at the view is $\exp(\boldsymbol\xi_i)\mathbf{T}_i$ with
$\boldsymbol\xi_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_q)$, $\Sigma_q\in\R^{6\times6}$, and the
intrinsics carry a small error $\boldsymbol\zeta_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_K)$.

\subsubsection{Sources of uncertainty}
\label{sec:method:uncertainty}
The model has six sources of uncertainty, each of which enters the test:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{U1.}] \textbf{Scale and offset ambiguity} $(a_i,b_i)$ of the monocular depth: a
        nuisance, absorbed by the alignment.
  \item[\textbf{U2.}] \textbf{Heteroscedastic depth noise} $\sigma(r,\tau,\theta)$, growing with
        range, in textureless regions and at grazing incidence.
  \item[\textbf{U3.}] \textbf{Spatial correlation} of the depth error, with length scale $\ell$
        and nugget $\nu$.
  \item[\textbf{U4.}] \textbf{Pose error} $\boldsymbol\xi_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_q)$
        of the pose used for rendering.
  \item[\textbf{U5.}] \textbf{Intrinsics error} $\boldsymbol\zeta_i\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_K)$,
        small when the intrinsics are given and larger when the model estimates the focal
        length itself.
  \item[\textbf{U6.}] \textbf{Surface uncertainty}: the rendered surface is itself an estimate,
        and its spread across the cross-fitted surfaces is $\Sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}$.
\end{enumerate}
Not modelled are occlusion and visibility, which a mask handles, dynamic content, and
non-Gaussian tails, which the calibration of the threshold absorbs.

\subsubsection{Residual against the rendered surface}
\label{sec:method:residual}
Let $\mathbf{r}_0\in\R^{n}$ be the residual $\hat d_i(u)-\hat S_i(u)$ over the $n$ visible pixels,
with $\hat S_i$ the depth rendered from the surface fitted without the view
(Section~\ref{sec:method:surface}). Linearising the rendering in the pose and intrinsics,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \mathbf{r}_0&\approx A\boldsymbol\eta+\boldsymbol\varepsilon_i+J_q\boldsymbol\xi_i+J_K\boldsymbol\zeta_i+\mathbf{e}_S,\\
  A&=[\,\mathbf{d}_i,\ \mathbf{1}\,],\quad \boldsymbol\eta=(a_i-1,\,b_i),
\end{split}
  \label{eq:resid}
\end{equation}
where $J_q\in\R^{n\times6}$ is the Jacobian of the rendered depth with respect to the pose
(Appendix~\ref{app:variance}), $J_K$ the Jacobian with respect to the intrinsics, and $\mathbf{e}_S$
the error of the rendered surface. The terms are independent, so
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \mathbf{r}_0&\sim\mathcal{N}(A\boldsymbol\eta,\Sigma_r),\\
  \Sigma_r&=\Sigma_\varepsilon+J_q\Sigma_qJ_q^{\top}+J_K\Sigma_KJ_K^{\top}+\Sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}.
\end{split}
  \label{eq:Sigmar}
\end{equation}
The surface term is estimated from the spread between the $K$ cross-fitted surfaces. Let
$\mathbf{s}_k\in\R^{n}$ be the depth that the $k$-th surface renders at the view; the
delete-a-group jackknife covariance is
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}=\tfrac{K-1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\mathbf{s}_k-\bar{\mathbf{s}})(\mathbf{s}_k-\bar{\mathbf{s}})^{\top}$,
of rank at most $K-1$, to which a small floor is added because $K$ is small. The structure of
$\Sigma_r$ is a scaled stationary kernel plus a low-rank term of rank at most
$6+\dim\boldsymbol\zeta+K-1$, so $\Sigma_r^{-1}$ follows from the Woodbury identity and
fast kernel solves, at a cost that is near-linear in $n$; the pixels are subsampled to the
correlation length $\ell$ where this is not enough. For rotation errors the diagonal of the pose
term reduces to $(r\,\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta)^{2}$, the pointing-error variance of the
earlier, independent-pixel treatment (Appendix~\ref{app:variance}), which is therefore the
diagonal special case of \eqref{eq:Sigmar}. The regularisation of that term at grazing
incidence, $\tan^{2}\theta\to\sin^{2}\theta/(\cos^{2}\theta+\epsilon^{2})$ with
$\epsilon\approx\cos80^{\circ}$ and a hard gate at $\theta>85^{\circ}$, is kept in $J_q$.

\subsubsection{Whitening, projection and the effective number of pixels}
\label{sec:method:whitening}
The scale and offset are a nuisance that the alignment absorbs, so the test uses the precision
with the nuisance projected out,
\begin{equation}
  M=\Sigma_r^{-1}-\Sigma_r^{-1}A\bigl(A^{\top}\Sigma_r^{-1}A\bigr)^{-1}A^{\top}\Sigma_r^{-1},
  \label{eq:M}
\end{equation}
which annihilates $A$ ($MA=0$). Equivalently, the residual is whitened, $\mathbf{w}=L^{-1}\mathbf{r}_0$
with $\Sigma_r=LL^{\top}$, and the whitened nuisance directions $L^{-1}A$ are projected out. Under
the nominal model $\mathbf{r}_0^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_0\sim\chi^{2}_{n-2}$. The correlation that
remains in the unwhitened, diagonally standardised residual is
$C_r=D_r^{-1}\Sigma_rD_r^{-1}$, with $D_r=\mathrm{diag}(\Sigma_r)^{1/2}$, and the effective number
of independent pixels follows from it,
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\mathrm{tr}(C_r)^{2}}{\mathrm{tr}(C_r^{2})},
  \label{eq:neff}
\end{equation}
the participation ratio of the eigenvalues of $C_r$. It equals $n$ for independent pixels and
$1$ for fully correlated ones, and it is the quantity that sets the variance of the energy
statistic of standardised residuals, $\mathrm{Var}[\boldsymbol\rho^{\top}\boldsymbol\rho/n]=2/n_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
It replaces a hand-set value: for the kernel of \eqref{eq:sigeps},
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx n/[(1-\nu)^{2}\pi\ell^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)+\nu^{2}]$, which for $n=10^{5}$ pixels,
$\ell=6$ and $\nu=0.2$ is about $1.4\times10^{3}$. The whitened tests below do not need
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, but it is the summary of how many independent looks a view gives and the
quantity in the closed-form approximations of the minimum detectable bias.

\subsubsection{Faults as unexplained deviations}
\label{sec:method:faultdef}
Under the model, a healthy view has $\mathbf{r}_0=A\boldsymbol\eta+\mathbf{n}$ with
$\mathbf{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_r)$. A \emph{fault} is a deviation
$\boldsymbol\phi$ with $\mathbf{r}_0=A\boldsymbol\eta+\mathbf{n}+\boldsymbol\phi$ and
$M\boldsymbol\phi\neq\mathbf{0}$: anything that neither the nuisance nor the noise can explain.
Four consequences follow. First, a pure scale or offset error, including a uniform additive bias
over a whole view and a bias proportional to $\sigma$ when $\sigma$ is affine in depth, lies in
$\mathrm{span}(A)$: it is absorbed by $\boldsymbol\eta$ in the gate and by the per-view alignment
in the pose graph, and is not a fault. Second, a coherent bias confined to a fraction $f$ of the
view is a fault, with detectability $\kappa^{2}=\boldsymbol\phi^{\top}M\boldsymbol\phi$, which for
independent pixels is $n\,f(1-f)\,\delta^{2}$: it peaks at $f=\tfrac12$ and vanishes at
$f=1$. Third, a pose error is a fault through $J_q$ to the extent that it exceeds $\Sigma_q$.
Fourth, noise correlation and surface spread are part of the model: a smooth deviation of the
size that they predict is not a fault, and a sharp-edged fault pattern can be more detectable than
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ suggests, because correlated noise cannot imitate a sharp edge, whereas a
smooth warp is less detectable.

\subsubsection{Calibration of the nominal model}
\label{sec:method:calibration}
The functions $\sigma(r,\tau,\theta)$, the length scale $\ell$ and the nugget $\nu$ are fitted
once per depth model, on clean development data, by maximum likelihood of the residuals of the
model against the laser reference depth, where the surface term is absent; $\Sigma_q$ comes from
the marginal covariances of the provisional pose graph, $\Sigma_K$ from the intrinsics source, and
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}$ is computed online. Alternatively, a depth model that outputs a
confidence supplies it as a covariate of $\sigma(\cdot)$. According to their public documentation
(checked 2026-11-14), Metric3D v2 returns a depth confidence (and a normal confidence) for its
ViT models, and UniDepthV2 lists a confidence output; Depth Pro returns depth and an estimated
focal length but no confidence, MoGe returns a binary validity mask rather than a confidence, and
the documentation of Depth Anything V2 and Marigold describes no uncertainty output. The default
model therefore needs its $\sigma(\cdot)$ fitted, and the confidence-output models are the
candidates for the confidence-covariate variant. Adequacy is checked on fault-free development
views: the mean of the whitened statistic divided by its degrees of freedom must be $1\pm0.1$ and
the false-alarm rate must match $\alpha$ without per-scene retuning (H6); if not, the model is
misspecified, for example in its tails or its $\ell$.

\subsection{Inverse Problem: Pose Graph Optimisation with Depth Maps}
\label{sec:method:inverse}
The poses and the alignments are estimated jointly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \min_{\{\mathbf{T}_i\},\{\mathbf{a}_{i}\}}\;
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\Bigl[&\rho_{g}\bigl(\|\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{geo}}_{ij}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_g}\bigr)\\
  &+g_{i}\,g_{j}\,
  \rho_{d}\bigl(\|\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{dep}}_{ij}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_d}\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{split}
  \label{eq:pgo}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{geo}}_{ij}$ are epipolar or reprojection residuals of the
feature matches between views $i$ and $j$, $\rho_g$ and $\rho_d$ are robust kernels
(a quadratic kernel when no robust baseline is used), and the depth-induced residual
back-projects a pixel $u$ of view $i$ with its aligned depth, transforms it into view
$j$, and compares its depth there with the depth of view $j$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  r^{\mathrm{dep}}_{ij}(u)&=\tilde D_{j}\bigl(\pi_j(\mathbf{q})\bigr)-q_z,\\
  \mathbf{q}&=\mathbf{T}_j^{-1}\mathbf{T}_i\,\pi_i^{-1}\bigl(u,\tilde D_{i}(u)\bigr).
\end{split}
  \label{eq:rdep}
\end{equation}
The weights $g_ig_j$ are where the gate acts: a depth constraint is built only from two
kept depth maps. These constraints are pairwise because the pose graph is, but they are
built after the gate, only from kept depth maps, and they are not part of the gate's cost
(Section~\ref{sec:method:cost}). The problem is \emph{partly end-to-end} in the following
sense: the matcher and the depth model are learned and kept fixed, while the back-end is a
differentiable least-squares solver \cite{opt_theseus} and the surface is renderable, so that
the depth residual of a view against the rendered surface, $\tilde D_i-\hat S_i(\mathbf{T}_i)$,
can be used as an additional, linear-cost constraint on $\mathbf{T}_i$ and gradients flow to
residual weights; in this paper the gate is training-free and the back-end is used as an
ordinary solver \cite{opt_g2o,opt_factorgraphs}. The differentiable, renderable optimiser is
the test bed the gate plugs into, not the framing. Its closest relative of the gate is
\emph{soft down-weighting}: replacing $g_i\in\{0,1\}$ by a weight $w_i\in[0,1]$ estimated
inside the optimisation from the view's residual. This is a baseline (H5): it reduces the
influence of a view but neither excludes it nor states a false-alarm or missed-detection

\subsection{View-Level Gate}
\label{sec:method:gate}

\subsubsection{Requirements}
Given evidence from the other views about the depth of a view, an exclusion rule should
(R1)~give the same answer for any ordering of the views; (R2)~respond to
\emph{disagreement} between a view and the others, not only to the view's own precision,
since a sharp but biased depth map must not be favoured; (R3)~return \emph{no consensus}
rather than guess when attribution is not identifiable, for example where fewer than three
other views observe the region; (R4)~keep the false-exclusion rate on fault-free data at a
stated level; (R5)~have a cost that grows with the number of views, not with the number
of view pairs; and (R6)~state its integrity from an explicit measurement model: a false-alarm probability, a
minimum detectable bias for a stated missed-detection probability, and an alarm when these
cannot be met. The entropy criterion of our earlier work does not meet (R1) and (R2) as
published (challenge C6), and a comparison with every overlapping view does not meet (R5)
when overlap is dense (challenge C7); this subsection gives a gate that meets all six.

\subsubsection{Shared-surface consensus by cross-fitting}
\label{sec:method:surface}
Instead of comparing a depth map with the depth maps of every overlapping view, the gate
compares it once with a single shared surface $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ rendered at the view. A
surface fitted to the view under test would absorb its fault, so the surface is
\emph{cross-fitted}: the views are split into $K$ folds of neighbouring views, and for each
fold $f$ a surface $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$ is fitted to the depth maps of the views outside $f$
and rendered at the views in $f$. A depth map is therefore never compared with a surface
that was fitted to it (challenge C3), and a block of neighbouring faulty views is held out
together, so it cannot support itself. The fit is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \min_{\theta,\{\mathbf{a}_{i}\}}\;&
  \sum_{i\notin f}\sum_{u}\rho\!\left(
  \frac{\bigl(\tilde D_{i}(u)-\hat S_i(u;\theta)\bigr)^{2}}{\tilde v_{\mathrm{res},i}(u)}\right)\\
  &+\lambda_\alpha L_\alpha(\theta)+\lambda_r R(\theta),
\end{split}
  \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
where $\hat S_i$ is the depth rendered from the current poses, $\rho$ is a robust kernel,
$L_\alpha$ penalises the accumulated alpha on valid and invalid pixels, and $R$ holds the
opacity and scale regularisers of 3DGS-MCMC \cite{gs_mcmc}. The number of splats $N_s$ is
fixed and dead splats are relocated to live ones rather than densified \cite{gs_mcmc};
this bounds the capacity with which the surface could add a second layer that absorbs a
faulty depth map (H6c). The fit uses depth only, so floaters are controlled by $L_\alpha$, a
cap on splat anisotropy, and the depth-distortion and normal-consistency terms of planar
splats \cite{gs_2dgs}, and the noise injected by the MCMC sampler is kept below the
normal-direction standard deviation $\sqrt{v_n}$ of Appendix~\ref{app:variance}. Splat
covariances are initialised from $v_n$. The rendered surface gives, at each pixel of a held-out view, the
depth $\hat S_i(u)$, a variance $v_{S,i}(u)$ (the spread of the splat depths along the
ray plus a floor from the splat scale) and the accumulated alpha $\alpha_i(u)$. The
alignment of a held-out depth map is fitted afterwards, by a robust affine fit to
$\hat S_i$ with the prior at $(1,0)$ for a metric model, so only the non-affine part of its
deviation remains.

\subsubsection{Visibility-aware residuals and statistics}
\label{sec:method:consensus}
A pixel $u$ of a held-out depth map enters the comparison only if it is valid, if the surface
supports it ($\alpha_i(u)\ge\tau_\alpha$; occlusion is handled by the depth-ordered
rendering), and if the incidence angle is below the grazing gate. On these $n$ pixels the
residual $\mathbf{r}_0$, its covariance $\Sigma_r$ of \eqref{eq:Sigmar} and the projected
precision $M$ of \eqref{eq:M} are formed (Section~\ref{sec:method:residual}). The gate uses two
statistics, both defined on the whitened, nuisance-projected residual. The \emph{energy
statistic}
\begin{equation}
  T_i=\mathbf{r}_0^{\top}M\,\mathbf{r}_0\ \sim\ \chi^{2}_{n-2}\quad\text{under the nominal model,}
  \label{eq:Ts}
\end{equation}
is sensitive to any pattern, including zero-mean warps. The \emph{region-scan statistic} tests
a family of $R$ candidate patterns $\mathbf{g}_1,\dots,\mathbf{g}_R$, the indicators of
superpixels or connected regions of the residual scaled by $\sigma$, with the matched statistic
$z_j=\mathbf{g}_j^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_0/\sqrt{\mathbf{g}_j^{\top}M\mathbf{g}_j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)$
and the threshold $\max_j|z_j|>z_{\alpha/R}$ of a Bonferroni correction. The signed mean of the
residual is not used: with a free offset it is zero by construction. Thresholds are set from
the false-alarm probability $\alpha$ (5\% per view), and calibrated on fault-free development
runs to absorb heavy tails (Section~\ref{sec:setup:protocol}). We also record Moran's~I of the
whitened residual as a diagnostic: a view-level fault leaves spatially structured residuals,
whereas sparse outliers do not. If too few pixels of a view are supported
($|\{u:\alpha_i(u)\ge\tau_\alpha\}|<s_{\min}P$), the surface cannot test the view and the
gate returns \emph{no consensus} for it.

\subsubsection{Detection theory and minimum detectable bias}
\label{sec:method:detection}
A fault is a deviation $\boldsymbol\phi=\beta\,\mathbf{g}$ of pattern $\mathbf{g}$ and size
$\beta$, in units of $\sigma$ when $\mathbf{g}$ is scaled by $\sigma$. Its \emph{detectability
index} is
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{\mathbf{g}}=\sqrt{\mathbf{g}^{\top}M\,\mathbf{g}},
  \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
the length of the fault pattern after whitening and after removal of whatever the scale and
offset explain. For a pattern that lies in $\mathrm{span}(A)$, $\kappa_{\mathbf{g}}=0$. The
matched statistic $z_{\mathbf{g}}$ is $\mathcal{N}(\beta\kappa_{\mathbf{g}},1)$, so its power is
$\Phi(\beta\kappa_{\mathbf{g}}-z_\alpha)$ up to the far tail, and the \emph{minimum detectable bias}
for a missed-detection probability $\beta_{\mathrm{md}}$ is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \beta_{\min}&=\frac{z_{\alpha}+z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}}}{\kappa_{\mathbf{g}}},\\
  z_\alpha&=\Phi^{-1}(1-\alpha/2),\quad z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}}=\Phi^{-1}(1-\beta_{\mathrm{md}}),
\end{split}
  \label{eq:mdb}
\end{equation}
the analogue of the minimum detectable bias of RAIM. The energy statistic is non-central
$\chi^{2}_{n-2}(\lambda)$ with $\lambda=\beta^{2}\kappa_{\mathbf{g}}^{2}$; its minimum detectable bias is
$\sqrt{\lambda_0}/\kappa_{\mathbf{g}}$, with $\lambda_0$ the non-centrality at which the missed-detection
probability is $\beta_{\mathrm{md}}$, and it needs no knowledge of the pattern. A scan over $R$
patterns replaces $z_\alpha$ by $z_{\alpha/R}$ in \eqref{eq:mdb}. For independent pixels with
unit variance, and a bias of $\delta$ on a fraction $f$ of $n$ pixels,
$\kappa^{2}=n\,f(1-f)$: the offset removes the mean, so the detectability peaks at $f=\tfrac12$
and a uniform bias ($f=1$) is invisible. Table~\ref{tab:mdb} gives the values for $n=1000$,
$\alpha=0.05$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{md}}=0.1$. With correlated noise, $\kappa$ and the energy
non-centrality follow from \eqref{eq:M} and are not simply a function of $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of
\eqref{eq:neff}, which summarises the energy statistic: a sharp-edged region is more

A pixel-wise filter flags a pixel when its standardised residual exceeds $k$, with $k=3$
typically. Under the same bias the flagging probability rises only from $2\Phi(-k)=0.0027$ to
$\Phi(\delta-k)+\Phi(-\delta-k)$, which is $0.0040$ at $\delta=0.3$ and, to second order, exceeds
the null by $k\phi(k)\delta^{2}$: a pixel at $0.3\sigma$ is practically indistinguishable from a
healthy one. Even if the flagged pixels were counted over the view, the resulting test is
weaker than the matched statistic by a factor of about 12 at $\delta=0.3$ and $f=0.5$ (about 25
at $f=0.2$), and the count overtakes the matched statistic only above $\delta\approx1.6$
($f=0.5$) or $2.3$ ($f=0.2$) (Appendix~\ref{app:detection}). The gate therefore wins when a
fault is sub-noise per pixel but coherent across a part of the view that the scale and offset
cannot absorb, and pixel filters are the better tool when the bias is large and confined to few
pixels, which is what the regime map of H1 measures. The weak point is the nominal model: the
minimum detectable bias is only as good as the calibration of $\Sigma_r$.

\subsubsection{Corrected admission criterion}
\label{sec:method:admission}
In our earlier work \cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, each sensor $i$ has a PDF $f_i$ over a
common grid $\Sgrid$, the product over a consensus set $C$ is formed, and $C$ is grown
greedily from the best pair using an entropy test. Revisiting that work, the prose and
Eq.~(14) seed the set with the pair of \emph{maximum} entropy after fusion, whereas the
corresponding line of Algorithm~1 tests $h_{ij}\le h^{*}$ with $h^{*}$ initialised to
$-\infty$, so the seed branch never executes; the expansion test accepts a sensor when
entropy does not decrease, which is the opposite of the stated aim of sharpening the
joint PDF, and it depends on sensor order.

A more basic problem is that the entropy of a normalised product cannot detect a
biased source. For one-dimensional Gaussians $\mathcal{N}(m_C,\sigma_C^2)$ and
$\mathcal{N}(m_k,\sigma_k^2)$, the normalised product has variance
$\sigma_C^2\sigma_k^2/(\sigma_C^2+\sigma_k^2)$ regardless of the offset $m_C-m_k$, so
\begin{equation}
  H(C\cup k)-H(C)=-\tfrac12\log\!\bigl(1+\sigma_C^{2}/\sigma_k^{2}\bigr)<0
  \label{eq:dH}
\end{equation}
for every source, healthy or faulty; a sharp biased source lowers entropy the most.
Entropy measures the precision of a consensus, not its consistency.

We therefore test consistency directly. For a pixel $u$, the consensus is the rendered
surface, a Gaussian $p_{C,u}=\mathcal{N}(\hat S_i(u),v_{S,i}(u))$, and the depth map of view
$i$ has the PDF $f_{i,u}$ on an interval $\Sgrid_u$ of admissible depths. The view is
\emph{compatible} with $C$ at $u$ when its Bayes factor against a uniform null on $\Sgrid_u$
is at least one:
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{i,u}(C)=|\Sgrid_u|\int_{\Sgrid_u}p_{C,u}\, f_{i,u}\,\mathrm{d}z\;\ge\;1 .
  \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
For Gaussians, $\Lambda_{i,u}(C)\ge 1$ is equivalent to
$\tfrac12\nu^{2}/(v_S+\sigma_i^2)\le \log\!\bigl(|\Sgrid_u|/\sqrt{2\pi(v_S+\sigma_i^2)}\bigr)$
with $\nu=\hat S_i(u)-\tilde D_i(u)$, so the offset enters exponentially. A view is kept on
the \emph{mean log Bayes factor} over its visible pixels,
$\bar\ell_i(C)=|\Omega_i^{\mathrm{vis}}|^{-1}\sum_u\log\Lambda_{i,u}(C)\ge\tau$, where
$\tau=0$ is the nominal null and the calibrated $\tau$ absorbs the correlation of
pixels. The interval volume $|\Sgrid_u|$ is part of the test and must be fixed and
reported. Applied to the whitened, projected residual, the pixels are independent with unit variance,
and for equal variances the mean log Bayes factor is a decreasing function of
$T_i/(n-2)$, which is why the evidence criterion ranks views like the chi-square test
(Proposition~\ref{prop:loo}) and differs from it only where the whitened residual is not
Gaussian. Because every view is tested against a surface that does not depend on it, the
result does not depend on the order of the views (R1), and the gate returns \emph{no
consensus} when the surface does not support the view (R3). Algorithm~\ref{alg:admission}
summarises the gate.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Shared-surface view-level gate}
\label{alg:admission}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE views split into $K$ folds, depth maps $\{D_i\}$, provisional poses, threshold $\tau$, support $(\tau_\alpha,s_{\min})$
\FOR{each fold $f$}
  \STATE fit $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$ to the depth maps of views outside $f$ \hfill (fixed $N_s$, eq.~\eqref{eq:fit})
  \FOR{each view $i\in f$}
    \STATE render $\hat S_i,\;v_{S,i},\;\alpha_i$ from $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$
    \IF{supported pixels $<s_{\min}P$}
      \STATE $g_{i}\gets0$; \textbf{continue} \hfill (no consensus)
    \ENDIF
    \STATE fit the alignment of $D_i$ to $\hat S_i$; compute $\bar\ell_{i}$ on visible pixels
    \STATE $g_{i}\gets\mathbb{1}[\bar\ell_{i}\ge\tau]$
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\{g_{i}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Relation to leave-one-out and solution-separation tests]
\label{prop:loo}
Let source $k$ measure a state $x$ directly with noise covariance $R_k$, let
$\hat x_{-k}$ be the estimate without it with covariance $P_{-k}$, and let
$\nu=z_k-\hat x_{-k}$ with $S=R_k+P_{-k}$. Then the solution-separation statistic
$d^{\top}\mathrm{Cov}(d)^{-1}d$, with $d=\hat x_{\mathrm{all}}-\hat x_{-k}$,
equals the leave-one-out innovation statistic $\nu^{\top}S^{-1}\nu$. For Gaussian
PDFs the negative log-evidence of $k$ under the consensus,
$-\log\!\int p_C f_k=\tfrac12\nu^{\top}S^{-1}\nu+\tfrac12\log\det(2\pi S)$, is the
leave-one-out statistic plus a covariance-volume term.
\end{proposition}
(Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.)
\begin{remark}
Under equal covariances and Gaussian residuals, ranking depth maps by this evidence is
therefore the same as ranking by the leave-one-out chi-square statistic, and the
evidence criterion can add value only where noise is heteroscedastic (as depth noise is),
residuals are non-Gaussian or multimodal, or occlusion and grazing geometry break the
chi-square assumptions. We test exactly this in H2 (Section~\ref{sec:setup:hyp}) and treat
a tie as a reportable outcome.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Cost}
\label{sec:method:cost}
A pairwise gate warps the depth maps of the neighbouring views into the view under test
(Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise}). With $P$ pixels per view, mean co-visibility degree $\bar d$
and a warp cost $c_w$ per pixel, its cost is $C_{\mathrm{pair}}\approx N\bar d\,P\,c_w$,
which grows with the number of view pairs when overlap is dense ($\bar d\propto N$). The
shared-surface gate fits $K$ surfaces of $N_s$ splats for $n_{\mathrm{it}}$ iterations and
renders every view once, at a render cost $c_r$ per pixel,
$C_{\mathrm{surf}}\approx\bigl((K+1)\,n_{\mathrm{it}}+1\bigr)\,N\,P\,c_r$, which is linear
in $N$ for fixed $K$, $N_s$ and $n_{\mathrm{it}}$ (the $+1$ in $K+1$ is the final refit
below). The surface therefore wins only when overlap is dense: the break-even degree is
$\bar d^{*}\approx\bigl((K+1)\,n_{\mathrm{it}}+1\bigr)\,c_r/c_w$, and for sparse view graphs
the pairwise gate is cheaper. Depth inference adds $N$ model evaluations per scene, which
can be cached and which do not depend on the gate; with $M$ models the ensemble ablation
multiplies this term by $M$. H6 measures the scaling and the break-even point.

\subsubsection{Exclusion, no-consensus alarm and availability}
\label{sec:method:elim}
The gate returns $g_i=1$ for kept depth maps and $0$ for excluded ones, and the excluded view
is removed from the depth constraints and from fusion; it still takes part in the pose graph
through its geometric constraints. Two alarms are raised. A \emph{no-consensus} alarm is raised
for a view that the other views cannot test, because the surface does not support it, or
because too few other views observe the region; the view is then not trusted, which is the
analogue of a loss of integrity monitoring for a satellite that is not covered by the redundancy.
A \emph{scene-level} alarm is raised if more than half of the views are rejected: the gate
cannot then tell a failing model from a surface that is itself wrong, and the result should not
be relied on. \emph{Availability} is the fraction of views that are tested and kept, and it is
reported next to the false-alarm rate, because a monitor that excludes too much is also
unusable. After pose graph optimisation, the surface is refitted once on all kept depth maps at
the refined poses; this surface is the fused output. Excluded views are reported in clusters of
neighbouring views, because region-specific failures (a glass surface seen from many views)
produce runs of rejections.

\subsubsection{Protection level on the fused output (conditional)}
\label{sec:method:pl}
Integrity monitoring does not stop at detection: a protection level bounds the error in the
output that an undetected fault can cause. For a scalar output $q$ of the reconstruction, for
example the fused depth at a query pixel or one coordinate of a camera position, let
$\mathbf{s}_i\in\R^{n}$ be the sensitivity of $q$ to a perturbation $\boldsymbol\phi$ of the depth
of view $i$, $\Delta q=\mathbf{s}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol\phi$, estimated by perturbing the view and
refitting; the response to a perturbation in $\mathrm{span}(A)$ is absorbed by the alignment. A
fault that the energy test misses has a non-centrality below $\lambda_0$, that is
$\boldsymbol\phi^{\top}M\boldsymbol\phi\le\lambda_0$, so if at most one view is faulty the remaining
error is bounded by the extremum of $\mathbf{s}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol\phi$ over this ellipsoid,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \mathrm{PL}_q&=\max_i\sqrt{\lambda_0\,\mathbf{s}_i^{\top}\tilde\Sigma_r\,\mathbf{s}_i}+z_q\,\sigma_q,\\
  \tilde\Sigma_r&=\Sigma_r-A\bigl(A^{\top}\Sigma_r^{-1}A\bigr)^{-1}A^{\top},
\end{split}
  \label{eq:pl}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_q$ is the standard deviation of $q$ under the healthy fit and $z_q$ a Gaussian
quantile. For independent pixels this is $\sqrt{\lambda_0}\,\sigma\,\|\mathbf{s}_{i,\perp}\|$, a
slope times a minimum detectable bias, the structure of a RAIM protection level
\cite{flt_araim_pl_exclusion}, and for correlated residuals it uses $\Sigma_r$ directly. Equation
\eqref{eq:pl} uses the energy test only, which is conservative: the scan statistic tightens it.
There is an important difference from RAIM: RAIM allocates integrity risk using prior fault
probabilities, which are unknown for learned depth, so \eqref{eq:pl} is \emph{conditional on a fault
being present}. It rests on four assumptions, each of which fails in a regime we test: (A1) at most
one faulty view, (A2) $\Sigma_r$ calibrated on clean data, (A3) a linear response for faults of the
size of the minimum detectable bias, and (A4) a surface that is not itself corrupted, which fails
for model-level faults. We therefore state \eqref{eq:pl} as a bound whose coverage is tested (H9),
not as a guarantee, and it is the first item cut if the schedule slips.

\subsection{Ensemble Variant (Ablation)}
\label{sec:method:ensemble}
The ensemble ablation gives each view $M$ depth maps $D_{m,i}$ from $M$ models and asks two
questions: whether models that share training data fail on the same views, and whether
voting across models breaks where the gate does not. Cross-model voting takes, at each
pixel, the median (or majority) of the $M$ aligned depth maps and flags a view when the
models disagree beyond their noise; it needs no other view. The ensemble gate tests each of
the $M$ depth maps of a view against the same surface rendered at the view, so that
$g_{m,i}$ is defined per model and a view is dropped when every model is rejected or when
the kept models disagree. Both are evaluated by H7 (Section~\ref{sec:setup:hyp}): they
should differ most on views where several models fail together, since cross-model voting then
agrees with itself, whereas the surface is built from other views.

\subsection{Test Bed: Differentiable, Renderable Pose Graph}
\label{sec:method:pipeline}
The test bed runs five steps. (i)~\emph{Depth and matches:} each view is processed by the
depth model, and feature matches are computed for view pairs. (ii)~\emph{Provisional poses:}
a pose graph with the geometric constraints only is solved with a robust kernel, to give
poses that are good enough to render a surface at each view. (iii)~\emph{Gate:}
Section~\ref{sec:method:gate}: cross-fit the shared surface and test every depth map against
its rendering. (iv)~\emph{Pose graph optimisation:} \eqref{eq:pgo}, with gated depth
constraints and, optionally, a robust kernel, a soft per-view weight (baseline) or the
render-based constraint of Section~\ref{sec:method:inverse}. (v)~\emph{Fused output:} the surface is
refitted on the kept depth maps at the refined poses; its rendered depth is the fused depth,
and a truncated signed distance function \cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion} of the same
kept depth is a secondary check. The gate may be repeated once with the refined poses.
Because the gate relies on provisional poses, errors in those poses can corrupt both the
surface and the gate; the repetition, and the comparison of gating before versus after the
pose graph (H4), test this. The baseline families replace or complement step (iii)
(Section~\ref{sec:setup:baselines}).
