\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

In this section, the manuscript provides its principal contribution: a view-level gate
that decides which depth sources to trust before pose graph optimisation and fusion.
Depth sources are produced by an ensemble of monocular estimators on RGB images. A
pose graph couples the views through geometric and depth-induced constraints. The gate
tests each source \emph{once} against a single shared Gaussian-splat surface rendered at
its view, instead of against every overlapping view, admits the sources that agree with
it, and passes only admitted sources to the graph and to fusion
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). The surface is fitted without the views it is tested on, so
its cost grows with the number of views rather than the number of view pairs, and the
surface refitted on the admitted sources is the fused output. The gate, including this
consensus, is the contribution; the surrounding pipeline
(Section~\ref{sec:method:pipeline}) is the test bed on which it is evaluated.

\subsection{Definitions and Notation}
\label{sec:method:defs}
A static scene is observed by $N$ RGB images $I_i$, $i\in\mathcal{V}=\{1,\dots,N\}$, with
approximately known intrinsics $\mathbf{K}_i$ and unknown poses
$\mathbf{T}_i\in SE(3)$ (camera to world). An ensemble of $M$ monocular estimators
$f_m$, $m\in\mathcal{M}=\{1,\dots,M\}$, predicts a depth map
$D_{m,i}:\Omega_i\to\R_{>0}$ for each view. A \emph{depth source} is a pair $(m,i)$.
Estimators that predict affine-invariant depth are used through a per-source scale and
shift $\mathbf{a}_{m,i}=(s_{m,i},t_{m,i})$, giving the aligned depth
$\tilde D_{m,i}=s_{m,i}D_{m,i}+t_{m,i}$; metric estimators use the same form with
initial values $(1,0)$. A pose graph $\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ has an edge
$(i,j)$ for each pair of views with enough feature matches and co-visibility. The gate
outputs $g_{m,i}\in\{0,1\}$ for each source, where $g_{m,i}=0$ means the source is
excluded from the depth constraints and from fusion; a view whose sources are all
excluded still takes part in the graph through its geometric constraints.
A \emph{shared surface} $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ is a set of $N_s$ Gaussians with means,
covariances and opacities $\theta$; rendering it at view $i$ with pose $\mathbf{T}_i$
gives a depth $\hat S_i(u)$, a depth variance $v_{S,i}(u)$ and an accumulated alpha
$\alpha_i(u)$ at each pixel.

A set $\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{V}$ of sources is \emph{faulty}:
their depth deviates from the true depth by a spatially structured error that is not
removed by the scale-and-shift alignment. We consider three classes.
\emph{Smooth warp:} the inverse depth is perturbed by a low-frequency field,
$1/D'_{m,i}=1/D_{m,i}+w_i(u)$. \emph{Region failure:} on a connected region covering a
fraction of the view, the depth is replaced by one that is unrelated to the true
geometry (for example a plane or a blurred copy). \emph{Non-affine distortion:}
$D'_{m,i}=D_{m,i}^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma\neq1$. A fault is \emph{view-level} if it
affects a subset of the views of an estimator, either scattered or clustered in
neighbouring views, and \emph{estimator-level} if it affects all of them. Neither
$\mathcal{F}$ nor its size is known; we assume that at most $F<M/2$ sources of any view
are faulty, and return to this assumption in Section~\ref{sec:disc:limits}.
Scale-and-shift errors are not faults in this sense, because the alignment, both in the
gate and in the pose graph, removes them; the gate targets the structure that
alignment cannot remove. The goal is a trajectory and a fused surface that are close to
those obtained from the healthy sources alone, together with the identity of the
excluded sources. The concrete magnitudes and the injection procedure are given in
Section~\ref{sec:setup:faults}.

\subsection{Forward Problem: Monocular Depth as a Measurement}
\label{sec:method:forward}
For a healthy source, the aligned depth equals the true depth $d_i(u)$ up to noise whose
spread grows with depth,
$\tilde D_{m,i}(u)=d_i(u)+\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\sigma_m^{2}(d_i(u))\bigr)$,
with $\sigma_m(d)=\sigma_{0,m}+\kappa_m d$ fitted per estimator on clean development
data. When a source is compared with the surface rendered at its view, the uncertainty of
the pose used for rendering adds a pointing error to the ray. For a ray of length $r$ that meets a locally planar surface at
incidence angle $\theta$ (the angle between ray and surface normal), a range noise
$\sigma_r=\sigma_m(r)$ and a pointing noise $\sigma_\varphi$ taken from the rotational
covariance of the pose give a depth \emph{residual} variance
\begin{equation}
  v_{\mathrm{res}}(r,\theta)=\sigma_r^{2}+\bigl(r\,\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta\bigr)^{2}.
  \label{eq:vres}
\end{equation}
This follows from $\mathrm{d}r/\mathrm{d}\theta=r\tan\theta$ for a plane at
perpendicular distance $h$, where $r=h/\cos\theta$ (Appendix~\ref{app:variance}); it is
the variance of the discrepancy between the measured depth and the intersection of the
nominal ray with the surface. Equation~\eqref{eq:vres} diverges at grazing incidence and
its linearisation fails once $\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta\gtrsim 0.3$, so we use the
regularised form
\begin{equation}
  \tilde v_{\mathrm{res}}=\operatorname{clip}\!\Bigl(\sigma_{r}^{2}
  +r^{2}\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}\,\tfrac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta+\epsilon^{2}},\;
  v_{\min},\,v_{\max}\Bigr),
  \label{eq:vreg}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon\approx\cos 80^{\circ}$ and a hard gate at $\theta>85^{\circ}$. It is not the
covariance of the back-projected 3D point: in the ray frame $(\ray,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})$
that covariance is $\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_r^2,(r\sigma_\varphi)^2,(r\sigma_\varphi)^2)$, and
its component along the surface normal,
\begin{equation}
  v_{n}(r,\theta)=\sigma_r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+r^{2}\sigma_\varphi^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,
  \label{eq:vn}
\end{equation}
stays bounded as $\theta\to\pi/2$. We therefore use \eqref{eq:vres} to weight depth
residuals and \eqref{eq:vn} to initialise splat covariances and to floor their scale
(Section~\ref{sec:method:surface}). Pixels that are not visible in the rendered view, or whose
incidence angle exceeds the gate, do not enter any comparison. Natural failures of estimators (reflective or transparent
surfaces, sky, textureless regions) are not variance but structured errors, and are the
faults the gate is designed to catch.

\subsection{Inverse Problem: Pose Graph Optimisation with Depth Sources}
\label{sec:method:inverse}
The poses and the alignments are estimated jointly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \min_{\{\mathbf{T}_i\},\{\mathbf{a}_{m,i}\}}\;
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}\Bigl[&\rho_{g}\bigl(\|\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{geo}}_{ij}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_g}\bigr)\\
  &+\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}} g_{m,i}\,g_{m,j}\,
  \rho_{d}\bigl(\|\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{dep}}_{m,ij}\|^{2}_{\Sigma_d}\bigr)\Bigr],
\end{split}
  \label{eq:pgo}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{geo}}_{ij}$ are epipolar or reprojection residuals of the
feature matches between views $i$ and $j$, $\rho_g$ and $\rho_d$ are robust kernels
(a quadratic kernel when no robust baseline is used), and the depth-induced residual
back-projects a pixel $u$ of view $i$ with its aligned depth, transforms it into view
$j$, and compares its depth there with the depth of source $(m,j)$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  r^{\mathrm{dep}}_{m,ij}(u)&=\tilde D_{m,j}\bigl(\pi_j(\mathbf{q})\bigr)-q_z,\\
  \mathbf{q}&=\mathbf{T}_j^{-1}\mathbf{T}_i\,\pi_i^{-1}\bigl(u,\tilde D_{m,i}(u)\bigr).
\end{split}
  \label{eq:rdep}
\end{equation}
The weights $g_{m,i}g_{m,j}$ are where the gate acts: a depth constraint is built only
from two trusted sources. These constraints are pairwise because the pose graph is, but
they are built after the gate, only from admitted sources, and they are not part of the
gate's cost (Section~\ref{sec:method:cost}). The problem is \emph{partly end-to-end} in the following
sense: the matcher and the depth ensemble are learned and kept fixed, while the
back-end is a differentiable least-squares solver \cite{opt_theseus}, so that residual
weights or the gate itself could be trained through it; in this paper the gate is
training-free and the back-end is used as an ordinary solver
\cite{opt_g2o,opt_factorgraphs}. \TODO{Author to confirm this reading of ``partly

\subsection{View-Level Gate}
\label{sec:method:gate}

\subsubsection{Requirements}
Given evidence from several sources about the depth of a view, an exclusion rule should
(R1)~give the same answer for any ordering of the sources; (R2)~respond to
\emph{disagreement} between a source and the others, not only to the source's own
precision, since a sharp but biased source must not be favoured; (R3)~return
\emph{no consensus} rather than guess when attribution is not identifiable, for
example with $M<3$ or where nothing else observes the view; (R4)~keep the false-exclusion
rate on fault-free data at a stated level; and (R5)~have a cost that grows with the
number of views, not with the number of view pairs. The entropy criterion of our earlier
work does not meet (R1) and (R2) as published (challenge C5), and a comparison with every
overlapping view does not meet (R5) when overlap is dense (challenge C6); this subsection
gives a gate that meets all five.

\subsubsection{Shared-surface consensus by cross-fitting}
\label{sec:method:surface}
Instead of comparing a source with the sources of every overlapping view, the gate
compares it once with a single shared surface $\mathcal{S}_\theta$ rendered at the view.
A surface fitted to the source under test would absorb its fault, so the surface is
\emph{cross-fitted}: the views are split into $K$ folds of neighbouring views, and for each
fold $f$ a surface $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$ is fitted to the sources of the views outside $f$ and
rendered at the views in $f$. A source is therefore never compared with a surface that was
fitted to it (challenge C2), and a block of neighbouring faulty views is held out together,
so it cannot support itself. The fit is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \min_{\theta,\{\mathbf{a}_{m,i}\}}\;&
  \sum_{(m,i)\in\mathrm{train}}\sum_{u}\rho\!\left(
  \frac{\bigl(\tilde D_{m,i}(u)-\hat S_i(u;\theta)\bigr)^{2}}{\tilde v_{\mathrm{res},m,i}(u)}\right)\\
  &+\lambda_\alpha L_\alpha(\theta)+\lambda_r R(\theta),
\end{split}
  \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
where $\hat S_i$ is the depth rendered from the current poses, $\rho$ is a robust kernel,
$L_\alpha$ penalises the accumulated alpha on valid and invalid pixels, and $R$ holds the
opacity and scale regularisers of 3DGS-MCMC \cite{gs_mcmc}. The number of splats $N_s$ is
fixed and dead splats are relocated to live ones rather than densified \cite{gs_mcmc};
this bounds the capacity with which the surface could add a second layer that absorbs a
faulty source (H6c). The fit uses depth only, so floaters are controlled by $L_\alpha$, a
cap on splat anisotropy, and the depth-distortion and normal-consistency terms of planar
splats \cite{gs_2dgs}, and the noise injected by the MCMC sampler is kept below the
normal-direction standard deviation from \eqref{eq:vn}. Splat covariances are initialised
from \eqref{eq:vn}. The rendered surface gives, at each pixel of a held-out view, the
depth $\hat S_i(u)$, a variance $v_{S,i}(u)$ (the spread of the splat depths along the
ray plus a floor from the splat scale) and the accumulated alpha $\alpha_i(u)$. The
alignment of a held-out source is fitted afterwards, by a robust affine fit to
$\hat S_i$, so only the non-affine part of its deviation remains.

\subsubsection{Visibility-aware residuals and statistic}
\label{sec:method:consensus}
A pixel $u$ of a held-out source enters the comparison only if it is valid, if the surface
supports it ($\alpha_i(u)\ge\tau_\alpha$; occlusion is handled by the depth-ordered
rendering), and if the incidence angle is below the grazing gate. With
$\Omega_{k}^{\mathrm{vis}}$ the set of such pixels, the normalised residual and the
per-source statistic are
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \rho_k(u)&=\frac{\tilde D_k(u)-\hat S_i(u)}{\sqrt{\tilde v_{\mathrm{res},k}(u)+v_{S,i}(u)}},\\
  T_k&=\frac{1}{|\Omega_k^{\mathrm{vis}}|}\sum_{u\in\Omega_k^{\mathrm{vis}}}\rho_k(u)^{2}.
\end{split}
  \label{eq:Ts}
\end{equation}
Pixel residuals are spatially correlated, so the nominal $\chi^2$ quantile is not used
as a threshold; thresholds are calibrated on fault-free development runs to a 5\%
per-view false-exclusion rate (Section~\ref{sec:setup:protocol}). We also record
Moran's~I of $\rho_k$ as a diagnostic: a view-level fault leaves spatially structured
residuals, whereas sparse outliers do not. If too few pixels of a view are supported
($|\{u:\alpha_i(u)\ge\tau_\alpha\}|<s_{\min}P$), the surface cannot test the view and the
gate returns \emph{no consensus} for it.

\subsubsection{Corrected admission criterion}
\label{sec:method:admission}
In our earlier work \cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, each sensor $i$ has a PDF $f_i$ over a
common grid $\Sgrid$, the product over a consensus set $C$ is formed, and $C$ is grown
greedily from the best pair using an entropy test. Revisiting that work, the prose and
Eq.~(14) seed the set with the pair of \emph{maximum} entropy after fusion, whereas the
corresponding line of Algorithm~1 tests $h_{ij}\le h^{*}$ with $h^{*}$ initialised to
$-\infty$, so the seed branch never executes; the expansion test accepts a sensor when
entropy does not decrease, which is the opposite of the stated aim of sharpening the
joint PDF, and it depends on sensor order.

A more basic problem is that the entropy of a normalised product cannot detect a
biased source. For one-dimensional Gaussians $\mathcal{N}(m_C,\sigma_C^2)$ and
$\mathcal{N}(m_k,\sigma_k^2)$, the normalised product has variance
$\sigma_C^2\sigma_k^2/(\sigma_C^2+\sigma_k^2)$ regardless of the offset $m_C-m_k$, so
\begin{equation}
  H(C\cup k)-H(C)=-\tfrac12\log\!\bigl(1+\sigma_C^{2}/\sigma_k^{2}\bigr)<0
  \label{eq:dH}
\end{equation}
for every source, healthy or faulty; a sharp biased source lowers entropy the most.
Entropy measures the precision of a consensus, not its consistency.

We therefore test consistency directly. For a pixel $u$, the consensus is the rendered
surface, a Gaussian $p_{C,u}=\mathcal{N}(\hat S_i(u),v_{S,i}(u))$, and source $k$ has the
depth PDF $f_{k,u}$ on an interval $\Sgrid_u$ of admissible depths. Source $k$ is
\emph{compatible} with $C$ at $u$ when its Bayes factor against a uniform null on $\Sgrid_u$
is at least one:
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_{k,u}(C)=|\Sgrid_u|\int_{\Sgrid_u}p_{C,u}\, f_{k,u}\,\mathrm{d}z\;\ge\;1 .
  \label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
For Gaussians, $\Lambda_{k,u}(C)\ge 1$ is equivalent to
$\tfrac12\nu^{2}/(v_S+\sigma_k^2)\le \log\!\bigl(|\Sgrid_u|/\sqrt{2\pi(v_S+\sigma_k^2)}\bigr)$
with $\nu=\hat S_i(u)-\tilde D_k(u)$, so the offset enters exponentially. A source is
admitted on the \emph{mean log Bayes factor} over its visible pixels,
$\bar\ell_k(C)=|\Omega_k^{\mathrm{vis}}|^{-1}\sum_u\log\Lambda_{k,u}(C)\ge\tau$, where
$\tau=0$ is the nominal null and the calibrated $\tau$ absorbs the correlation of
pixels. The interval volume $|\Sgrid_u|$ is part of the test and must be fixed and
reported. Because every source is tested against the same reference, the result does not
depend on the order of the sources (R1), and the gate returns \emph{no consensus} when the
surface does not support the view or rejects every source (R3).
Algorithm~\ref{alg:admission} summarises the gate.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Shared-surface view-level gate}
\label{alg:admission}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE views split into $K$ folds, sources $\{(m,i)\}$, provisional poses, threshold $\tau$, support $(\tau_\alpha,s_{\min})$
\FOR{each fold $f$}
  \STATE fit $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$ to the sources of views outside $f$ \hfill (fixed $N_s$, eq.~\eqref{eq:fit})
  \FOR{each view $i\in f$}
    \STATE render $\hat S_i,\;v_{S,i},\;\alpha_i$ from $\mathcal{S}_{-f}$
    \IF{supported pixels $<s_{\min}P$}
      \STATE $g_{m,i}\gets0$ for all $m$; \textbf{continue} \hfill (no consensus)
    \ENDIF
    \FOR{each source $(m,i)$}
      \STATE fit alignment of $(m,i)$ to $\hat S_i$; compute $\bar\ell_{m,i}$ on visible pixels
      \STATE $g_{m,i}\gets\mathbb{1}[\bar\ell_{m,i}\ge\tau]$
    \ENDFOR
  \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $\{g_{m,i}\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{proposition}[Relation to leave-one-out and solution-separation tests]
\label{prop:loo}
Let source $k$ measure a state $x$ directly with noise covariance $R_k$, let
$\hat x_{-k}$ be the estimate without it with covariance $P_{-k}$, and let
$\nu=z_k-\hat x_{-k}$ with $S=R_k+P_{-k}$. Then the solution-separation statistic
$d^{\top}\mathrm{Cov}(d)^{-1}d$, with $d=\hat x_{\mathrm{all}}-\hat x_{-k}$,
equals the leave-one-out innovation statistic $\nu^{\top}S^{-1}\nu$. For Gaussian
PDFs the negative log-evidence of $k$ under the consensus,
$-\log\!\int p_C f_k=\tfrac12\nu^{\top}S^{-1}\nu+\tfrac12\log\det(2\pi S)$, is the
leave-one-out statistic plus a covariance-volume term.
\end{proposition}
(Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.)
\begin{remark}
Under equal covariances and Gaussian residuals, ranking sources by this evidence is
therefore the same as ranking by the leave-one-out chi-square statistic, and the
evidence criterion can add value only where noise is heteroscedastic (as depth noise is),
residuals are non-Gaussian or multimodal, or occlusion and grazing geometry break the
chi-square assumptions. We test exactly this in H2 (Section~\ref{sec:setup:hyp}) and treat
a tie as a reportable outcome.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Cost}
\label{sec:method:cost}
A pairwise gate warps the consensus of each neighbouring view into the view under test
(Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise}). With $P$ pixels per view, mean co-visibility degree $\bar d$
and a warp cost $c_w$ per pixel, its cost is $C_{\mathrm{pair}}\approx N\bar d\,P\,c_w$,
which grows with the number of view pairs when overlap is dense ($\bar d\propto N$). The
shared-surface gate fits $K$ surfaces of $N_s$ splats for $n_{\mathrm{it}}$ iterations and
renders every view once, at a render cost $c_r$ per pixel,
$C_{\mathrm{surf}}\approx\bigl((K+1)\,n_{\mathrm{it}}+1\bigr)\,N\,P\,c_r$, which is linear
in $N$ for fixed $K$, $N_s$ and $n_{\mathrm{it}}$ (the $+1$ in $K+1$ is the final refit
below). The surface therefore wins only when overlap is dense: the break-even degree is
$\bar d^{*}\approx\bigl((K+1)\,n_{\mathrm{it}}+1\bigr)\,c_r/c_w$, and for sparse view graphs
the pairwise gate is cheaper. H6 measures the scaling and the break-even point.

\subsubsection{Output, refit and persistence}
\label{sec:method:elim}
The gate returns $g_{m,i}=1$ for admitted sources and $0$ otherwise; if the answer is
\emph{no consensus}, all $g_{m,i}=0$ and the view is excluded from depth constraints and
fusion. After pose graph optimisation, the surface is refitted once on all admitted
sources at the refined poses; this surface is the fused output. To separate a failing view
from a failing estimator, we track, along the view order, how often each estimator is
rejected: if one estimator is rejected on a run of neighbouring views, it is flagged by a
CUSUM test and excluded for the whole scene (estimator-level exclusion), whereas isolated
rejections remain view-level.

\subsection{Test Bed: Gate, Pose Graph and Fusion}
\label{sec:method:pipeline}
The test bed runs five steps. (i)~\emph{Depth ensemble and matches:} each view is
processed by the $M$ estimators, and feature matches are computed for view pairs.
(ii)~\emph{Provisional poses:} a pose graph with the geometric constraints only is
solved with a robust kernel, to give poses that are good enough to render a surface at
each view. (iii)~\emph{Gate:} Section~\ref{sec:method:gate}: cross-fit the shared surface
and test every source against its rendering. (iv)~\emph{Pose graph optimisation:}
\eqref{eq:pgo}, with gated depth constraints and, optionally, a robust kernel.
(v)~\emph{Fused output:} the surface is refitted on the admitted sources at the refined
poses; its rendered depth is the fused depth, and a truncated signed distance function
\cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion} of the same admitted depth is a secondary check. The
gate may be repeated once with the refined poses. Because the gate relies on provisional
poses, errors in those poses can corrupt both the surface and the gate; the repetition,
and the comparison of gating before versus after the pose graph (H4), test this. The
baseline families replace or complement step (iii) (Section~\ref{sec:setup:baselines}).
