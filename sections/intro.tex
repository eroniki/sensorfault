\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Monocular metric depth models \cite{est_metric3dv2,est_unidepth,est_depthpro} are now accurate
enough to supervise 3D reconstruction from images: depth-supervised Gaussian splatting
\cite{gs_3dgs,gs_2dgs,gs_dnsplatter} uses one predicted depth map per image as a geometric prior. We
study this for aerial survey imagery, flown in parallel strips with GNSS/IMU poses, where a depth prior
per image could complement or replace dense multi-view stereo. The predictions fail in a
characteristic way. The failures are \emph{regional} and \emph{appearance-driven}: on glass, water or
other specular surfaces the model predicts the depth of the reflection, and on textureless roofs, roads
or walls it has no cue and predicts a smooth guess. Inside such a region the error is large, often many
times the nominal noise, and outside it the same depth map is good. The models are also trained mostly
on imagery taken near the ground, so nadir views from survey altitude are a different, less familiar
regime. Depth supervision bakes the faulty regions into the surface, and nothing in a single image
says which depth map, or which part of it, to distrust.

The cheap defences act per pixel or per ray. Robust losses \cite{rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron}
and distractor-aware splatting such as RobustNeRF and SpotLessSplats
\cite{gs_robustnerf,gs_spotless} down-weight residuals that look like outliers, mostly from
photometric evidence; a per-view RMS of the aligned depth residual, or the depth model's own
confidence, gives a view score. None of them states a false-alarm rate, and none uses the fact
that the other views of the same scene observe the same surface with known uncertainty.

\paragraph{Approach}
The contribution is a \emph{depth-only view gate}. It is built on a standard representation: 2DGS
surfels, each augmented with a $10\times10$ information block over its mean, its tangent-space rotation
error, its log scales and its opacity, kept apart from the shape used for rendering. Every view adds the
alpha-weighted rasteriser information $J^{\top}R^{-1}J$ to the surfels it sees, so the fused surface is a
Laplace posterior obtained by rendering. Leaving a view out, or a block of neighbouring frames that tend
to share its errors, is a subtraction of their information and one Newton step. The gate renders the
remaining views' surface and its variance into the tested view and tests the whitened depth residuals at
a stated false-alarm rate; pixels that no other view sees are untestable, and the tested view spawns no
surfels of its own. The representation is sparse, and both fusion and testing are correspondence-free,
unlike dense volumetric fusion in a TSDF \cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion}, dense radiance fields
\cite{rec_nerf} or multi-view stereo cost volumes \cite{rec_mvsnet}. Geometry is fitted to depth only:
colour is a separate appearance block that may refine the poses but never updates the geometry, so
reflectance cannot corrupt the surface except through the depth model itself. Poses come from the
survey's GNSS/IMU as a smooth trajectory spline, refined by render-and-compare without correspondences.

\paragraph{What is and is not new}
The representation is not new: 2DGS surfels \cite{gs_2dgs} with confidence-weighted fusion are close to
point-based fusion \cite{fus_keller_surfels}, and per-view alignment of monocular depth is standard
\cite{rec_monosdf}. The test is classical (Baarda's data snooping, Teunissen's DIA procedure
\cite{stat_teunissen_dia} and GNSS solution separation \cite{flt_raim_joerger}); removing a view by
subtraction is the cavity step of expectation propagation \cite{mp_minka_ep}, used here once per round
and without moment matching; the outer loop of rounds is iterative data snooping and hard EM
\cite{stat_dempster_em}; and spline trajectories are standard in continuous-time estimation
\cite{traj_furgale_basis}. What is new, to our knowledge, is the gate: a per-Gaussian uncertainty that
the splatting rasteriser renders into depth variance, used with exact leave-view-out and leave-block-out
to test whole views at a stated false-alarm rate (Section~\ref{sec:related}).

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A depth-only view gate that tests each view's whitened depth residuals against the rendered
        cavity of the other views, with untestable pixels, no spawning by the tested view, and a
        Holm-controlled outer loop (Sections~\ref{sec:method:gate}--\ref{sec:method:loop}).
  \item Leave-block-out: exact subtraction of a block of neighbouring frames, so that neighbours that
        repeat a view's error cannot mask it (Section~\ref{sec:method:block}).
  \item A pre-registered evaluation on aerial survey imagery, with ETH3D as a sanity check, that starts by
        measuring the residuals of a real depth model, tests whether it repeats its errors across
        neighbouring views, and requires the gate to beat cheap baselines on regional, appearance-driven
        failures (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
\end{itemize}
