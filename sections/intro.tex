\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{M}{onocular} metric depth models \cite{est_metric3dv2,est_unidepth,est_depthpro} are now
accurate enough to supervise 3D reconstruction: depth-supervised Gaussian splatting
\cite{gs_3dgs,gs_2dgs,gs_dnsplatter} uses one predicted depth map per image as a geometric prior. For aerial
survey imagery, flown in parallel strips with GNSS/IMU poses, such a prior could complement or replace dense
multi-view stereo. The predictions, however, fail in a characteristic way: \emph{regionally} and for reasons
of \emph{appearance}. On glass, water and other specular surfaces the model returns the depth of the
reflection; on textureless roofs, roads and walls it has no cue and returns a smooth guess. Inside such a
region the error is many times the nominal noise, while the rest of the same depth map is accurate. The
models are also trained mostly on near-ground imagery, so nadir views from survey altitude are an
unfamiliar regime. Depth supervision bakes the faulty regions into the surface, and nothing in a single
image says which depth map to distrust.

Existing defences act per pixel or per ray. Robust losses \cite{rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron} and
distractor-aware methods such as RobustNeRF and SpotLessSplats \cite{gs_robustnerf,gs_spotless}
down-weight residuals that look like outliers, mostly on photometric evidence; the aligned depth RMS of a
view, or the depth model's own confidence, gives a view score. None of them states a false-alarm rate, and
none uses the fact that the other views observe the same surface with known uncertainty.

We propose a \emph{depth-only view gate} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Each 2DGS surfel \cite{gs_2dgs}
carries a $10\times10$ information block over its mean, rotation error, log scales and opacity, kept apart
from the shape used for rendering. Every view adds the alpha-weighted rasteriser information
$J^{\top}R^{-1}J$ to the surfels it renders, so the fused surface is a Laplace posterior obtained by
rendering. Fusion and testing are sparse and need no correspondences, unlike TSDF fusion
\cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion}, radiance fields \cite{rec_nerf} or multi-view stereo cost volumes
\cite{rec_mvsnet}. Leaving out a view, or a block of neighbouring frames that tend to share its errors, is
a subtraction of their information and one Newton step. The gate renders the remaining views' surface and
its variance into the tested view and tests the whitened depth residuals at a stated false-alarm rate.
Geometry is fitted to depth only, so reflectance cannot corrupt it, and poses come from GNSS/IMU as a
trajectory spline refined by render-and-compare.

The ingredients are established: surfel fusion \cite{fus_keller_surfels}, Baarda's data snooping and
Teunissen's DIA procedure \cite{stat_teunissen_dia}, solution separation \cite{flt_raim_joerger}, the
cavity step of expectation propagation \cite{mp_minka_ep} and iterative data snooping as hard EM
\cite{stat_dempster_em}. To our knowledge, what is new is a per-Gaussian uncertainty that the splatting
rasteriser renders into depth variance, used with exact leave-view-out and leave-block-out to test whole
views at a stated false-alarm rate. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a depth-only view gate that tests each view against the rendered cavity of the other views, with
        untestable pixels, no surfels spawned by the tested view, and a Holm-controlled outer loop
        (Sections~\ref{sec:method:gate} and~\ref{sec:method:loop});
  \item leave-block-out, an exact subtraction of neighbouring frames, so that neighbours that repeat a
        view's error cannot mask it (Section~\ref{sec:method:fusion});
  \item controlled experiments on an aerial MatrixCity block that measure false-alarm control, swamping
        and the response to pose errors, and a pre-registered protocol for real depth models on UseGeo,
        MatrixCity and ETH3D (Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}
