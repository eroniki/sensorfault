\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Image-based 3D reconstruction increasingly uses monocular depth estimators as a
geometric prior. A depth map predicted from a single image constrains the relative
pose of two views, fills in regions where feature matching fails, and provides the
surface that is fused at the end. Several estimators are available
\cite{est_midas,est_dav2,est_metric3dv2,est_unidepth,est_depthpro,est_moge,est_marigold},
they differ in architecture, training data and output convention (metric depth versus
affine-invariant depth), and their errors are not independent of the scene: an
estimator may be accurate on most views of a scene and fail coherently on a few, for
example on reflective or transparent surfaces, in textureless regions, or where its
training data does not cover the scene type. Henceforth, a \emph{depth source} denotes
the depth map that one estimator predicts for one view, and a \emph{view-level fault}
denotes a source whose depth is wrong in a spatially structured way (a smooth warp, a
failed region, an inconsistent scale), as opposed to sparse pixel noise.

This paper proposes a \emph{view-level gate}: a test that decides which depth sources
to trust \emph{before} pose graph optimisation and fusion. The gate is the
contribution. To make the setting concrete we embed it in a test bed whose depth maps
come from an ensemble of monocular estimators applied to RGB images, whose poses come
from a partly end-to-end pose graph optimisation, and which serves as an alternative to
a classical structure-from-motion (SfM) pipeline. The pipeline is the test bed: it
supplies the depth maps, the poses and the downstream task on which the gate is
evaluated, and we do not claim it as a new reconstruction system.

\subsection{Relevant Literature}
\label{sec:intro:lit}
\runin{Monocular depth estimators.}
MiDaS learns depth that is invariant to range and scale by mixing datasets
\cite{est_midas}; Depth Anything V2 reports finer and more robust predictions from
synthetic labels and pseudo-labelled real images \cite{est_dav2}. Metric3D v2,
UniDepth and Depth Pro target zero-shot metric depth \cite{est_metric3dv2,est_unidepth,est_depthpro};
MoGe predicts an affine-invariant point map \cite{est_moge}, and Marigold repurposes
diffusion-based image generators for depth \cite{est_marigold}. Outputs therefore differ
in whether they carry metric scale, and none of these works states when a prediction
should not be used for downstream geometry. Ensembles are a standard route to
predictive uncertainty \cite{ens_deepensembles}; here the members are different
estimators, and we use their disagreement as evidence.

\runin{SfM, view graphs and multi-view consistency.}
Incremental and global SfM estimate poses from feature matches
\cite{fus_colmap_sfm,sfm_glomap}; view-graph filtering removes inconsistent
pairwise constraints before global estimation \cite{sfm_1dsfm}. In multi-view stereo, a
depth map is kept pixel by pixel when enough neighbouring views agree on depth and
normal \cite{fus_colmap_mvs}, or when other views do not see free space in front of it
\cite{fus_merrell}, and surviving depth is fused volumetrically
\cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion}. These filters decide per pixel.

\runin{Robust pose graph optimisation.}
Pose graph and factor-graph solvers \cite{opt_g2o,opt_factorgraphs} are made robust to
bad constraints with robust cost functions \cite{flt_huber,rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron,rob_mactavish},
graduated non-convexity \cite{rob_gnc}, switchable constraints \cite{rob_switchable},
dynamic covariance scaling \cite{rob_dcs} and max-mixture models \cite{rob_maxmix}. These
act on the residual of each constraint, after the constraint has been built from the
depth map.

\runin{Learned reconstruction and differentiable optimisation.}
DUSt3R reconstructs image collections without calibration or poses
\cite{rec_dust3r}; MASt3R casts matching as a 3D task \cite{rec_mast3r}, and MASt3R-SfM
builds a fully integrated SfM solution on it \cite{rec_mast3rsfm}. VGGT is a
feed-forward network that infers camera parameters, point maps, depth maps and tracks
from one to hundreds of views \cite{rec_vggt}. DROID-SLAM updates poses and pixelwise
depth through a dense bundle-adjustment layer \cite{rec_droidslam}, and Theseus provides
differentiable nonlinear least squares on PyTorch \cite{opt_theseus}. We use MASt3R-SfM and
VGGT as reference points rather than rivals: they replace the pipeline, whereas the
gate is a component that can precede one.

\runin{Source-level fault detection and exclusion.}
Excluding a faulty source from a redundant set is a classical problem. Byzantine fault
tolerance \cite{flt_lamport}, interval-based fusion of continuous-valued sensors
\cite{flt_marzullo,flt_brooksiyengar}, and receiver autonomous integrity monitoring
with residual-based, solution-separation and multiple-hypothesis tests
\cite{flt_raim_parkinson,flt_raim_brown,flt_raim_joerger,flt_raim_pervan} detect and
exclude a faulty source, and random-sample-consensus methods select a consistent subset
\cite{flt_ransac,flt_msac}. In our earlier work we eliminated Byzantine accelerometers
from a vibration-based localisation by building a consensus of per-sensor PDFs
\cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, and used a joint likelihood over several sensors for dispersive
wave propagation \cite{flt_ambarkutuk2024}.

\runin{Positioning.}
Pixel filters, robust kernels and voting decide at a different unit and stage from the
gate: pixel filters per pixel and after poses are fixed, kernels per constraint and after
the constraint exists, voting per pixel across sources. A coherent view-level fault is
neither sparse nor independent across pixels, so none of these is designed to attribute
it to a source. To our knowledge, a gate that decides trust per depth source before pose
graph optimisation has not been studied in this setting; this statement rests on a
limited literature search and will be re-checked before submission.

\subsection{Challenges in Using Monocular Depth Before Pose Estimation}
\label{sec:intro:challenges}
Several challenges limit current approaches.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{C1.}] \textbf{View-level faults are coherent.} When an estimator
        fails on a view, its error is smooth or confined to a region, so most pixels
        of the view still look plausible. Per-pixel consistency filters decide pixel
        by pixel and can keep a warped depth map whose pixels agree with each other,
        and a robust kernel only down-weights a constraint after the faulty depth has
        entered the graph. Where the faulty view is covered by few neighbours, or
        where several neighbouring views fail together, a vote over pixels has no
        majority to rely on.
  \item[\textbf{C2.}] \textbf{Monocular depth has no reference scale.} Many estimators
        output affine-invariant depth, and even metric ones are only approximately
        metric, so a source can be compared with others only after fitting a scale and
        shift per view. That fit can absorb part of a fault and make the faulty source
        look consistent, so the comparison must be made after a fit that excludes the
        source under test.
  \item[\textbf{C3.}] \textbf{Estimators share failure modes.} Estimators trained on
        overlapping data tend to fail on the same content, so a majority of an
        ensemble can be wrong together, and with fewer than three sources attribution
        is not identifiable. A gate must be able to answer \emph{no consensus}.
  \item[\textbf{C4.}] \textbf{Disagreement is not always a fault.} Occlusion, low
        overlap and moving content make sources disagree for reasons unrelated to
        their quality, so residuals must be compared only on pixels that are visible
        in both views and have enough support.
  \item[\textbf{C5.}] \textbf{The consensus criterion must detect disagreement.} A
        criterion that rewards the precision of the combined estimate, as the entropy
        test of our earlier work does as published, cannot distinguish an agreeing
        source from a sharp but biased one.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baseline Study and Overview of the Fundamental Differences}
\label{sec:intro:baseline}
The gate must beat three families of baselines, which act at different units and
stages (Table~\ref{tab:diff}): robust kernels in the pose graph, the geometric
consistency filter of COLMAP \cite{fus_colmap_mvs}, and plain voting. MASt3R-SfM and
VGGT are reference points. The consensus criterion adapted for the gate comes from our
earlier Byzantine sensor elimination (BSE) \cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, which localised
footsteps from the signal energies of floor-mounted accelerometers: each sensor gave a
PDF of the location over a grid, the PDFs were multiplied, and a consensus set was grown
greedily from the best pair under an entropy criterion. Here the sources are
estimator--view pairs, the PDFs are per-pixel depth distributions, and the admission
test is corrected (Section~\ref{sec:method:gate}).

\subsection{Summary of the Contributions}
\label{sec:intro:contrib}
This paper presents a view-level gate for depth sources. The following points summarise
the contributions.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A view-level gate for depth sources} (addresses C1 and C3): each
        estimator--view pair is admitted or rejected before pose graph optimisation
        and fusion, with \emph{no consensus} as an explicit outcome
        (Section~\ref{sec:method:gate}).
  \item \textbf{Leave-one-source-out comparison with alignment and visibility}
        (addresses C2 and C4): sources are compared after a scale-and-shift fit that
        excludes the source under test, on pixels visible in both views
        (Section~\ref{sec:method:gate}).
  \item \textbf{A corrected admission criterion} (addresses C5): a Bayes-factor test
        with an analysis of when the entropy criterion reduces to a leave-one-out
        chi-square test and when it does not (Section~\ref{sec:method:gate}).
  \item \textbf{A test bed and a pre-registered protocol}: an RGB-plus-monocular-depth
        ensemble pipeline with a partly end-to-end pose graph, five falsifiable
        hypotheses, injected and natural view-level faults, explicit breakdown scenarios,
        and the three baseline families (Sections~\ref{sec:method:pipeline} and~\ref{sec:setup}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Organization of the Paper}
\label{sec:intro:org}
The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section~\ref{sec:method} presents the
method: the notation, the forward model of a depth source, the inverse problem of pose
graph optimisation with depth sources, the view-level gate, and the test-bed pipeline.
Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes the experiments, and Section~\ref{sec:results} reports
