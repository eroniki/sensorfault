\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A satellite navigation receiver cannot trust every satellite it tracks. Receiver autonomous
integrity monitoring (RAIM) therefore checks the redundant measurements against each
other, detects that one of them is inconsistent, excludes it, and raises an alert when it
cannot guarantee the solution
\cite{flt_raim_parkinson,flt_raim_sturza,flt_raim_brown,flt_raim_pervan}. Monocular-depth
reconstruction, that is, image-based 3D reconstruction that uses a depth estimator's
prediction for each image as a geometric prior, has the same problem. Strong metric
estimators are now available \cite{est_metric3dv2,est_unidepth,est_depthpro}, but a
single-image prediction carries no redundancy, nothing in the image says whether it is right,
and its errors are not independent of the scene: a model may be accurate on most views and
fail coherently on a few, on glass and other reflective surfaces, on sky, in textureless
regions, or in scenes outside its training distribution. The redundancy is supplied by the
other views of the same scene, and the question is the one RAIM asks of satellites: which
view should not be trusted, and can the system say so? Henceforth, a \emph{depth source}
denotes the depth map that one model predicts for one view, and a \emph{view-level fault}
denotes a fault that moves a whole view coherently: a bias or warp of its depth, or an error
in its pose.

\paragraph{Core argument.}
A coherent view-level fault adds a small, spatially structured bias across a view: a shift of the
depth over part of it, a warp, or a pose error that moves the whole rendering. None of these is
explained by the monocular scale and offset, which the alignment absorbs; a bias that is
uniform over the whole view is only an offset, and not a fault at all. At a per-pixel magnitude
below the noise, for example $0.3\sigma$, each pixel still looks healthy: pixel-wise
consistency filters, which test one pixel at a time against a threshold of several $\sigma$, see
almost nothing, because the probability that a pixel crosses the threshold hardly changes.
Aggregated over the view's co-visible pixels, whitened by the covariance that an explicit
measurement model predicts and projected orthogonally to the scale and offset, the same
pattern is a large test statistic. For a bias on a fraction $f$ of $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ effectively
independent pixels its non-centrality grows as $n_{\mathrm{eff}}f(1-f)\delta^{2}$, so the minimum
detectable bias is $\propto1/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}f(1-f)}$ (Section~\ref{sec:method:detection}),
about $0.2\sigma$ at $f=\tfrac12$ for $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=10^{3}$. The gate therefore wins exactly
when faults are sub-noise per pixel but coherent across part of a view, which is how monocular
depth fails on glass, sky and out-of-distribution scenes, and it should not win, and is not
expected to, when a fault is large but confined to a few pixels, where pixel filters are the right
tool. Robust kernels, the other standard defence, only down-weight a constraint when its residual
is large, so a sub-noise coherent bias passes through them, and even when they act they give no
statement about trust: they neither exclude the view nor tell the user that the output should not
be relied on. The gate excludes the view and can raise a \emph{no consensus} alarm, as RAIM does.
The claim is only as good as the measurement model: the minimum detectable bias is derived from
the residual covariance $\Sigma_r$, which must be calibrated, not assumed.

\paragraph{Approach.}
This paper proposes \emph{view-level fault rejection} for monocular-depth reconstruction: a
\emph{gate} that performs fault detection and exclusion (FDE) on views before pose graph
optimisation and fusion. The gate tests each depth map once against a single shared surface of
planar Gaussian primitives, built from the depth maps alone in closed form and fused in
information form. The view's own contribution is removed by an exact subtraction, so the cost
grows with the number of views rather than the number of view pairs, and the surface of the kept
depth maps doubles as the fused output, with a posterior, a protection level and a view count in
every region. The gate, with this consensus and its integrity statements, is the contribution. We
embed it in a test bed, a differentiable, renderable pose graph fed by one depth model per image;
the pipeline is not claimed as a new reconstruction system, soft down-weighting inside it is a
baseline, and a multi-model ensemble is an ablation only.

\paragraph{Related work in brief.}
RAIM and fault detection and exclusion
\cite{flt_raim_parkinson,flt_raim_sturza,flt_raim_brown,flt_raim_pervan,flt_araim_pl_exclusion}
supply the framework; strong metric depth estimators
\cite{est_metric3dv2,est_unidepth,est_depthpro} supply the depth maps; Gaussian primitives
\cite{gs_3dgs,gs_2dgs,gs_mcmc} fit surfaces to given supervision, and at most mask pixels of it;
MASt3R-SfM and VGGT \cite{rec_mast3rsfm,rec_vggt} are reference points rather than rivals.
Robust kernels and loop-closure back-ends down-weight constraints but do not exclude a view or
state an integrity level. To our knowledge, integrity monitoring of this kind has not been
applied to the views of a learned-depth reconstruction; this rests on a limited literature
search. The full review, the challenges (C1--C7) and the baseline study are in
Supplement~\ref{sup:related}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An explicit measurement model and a gate derived from it (C1--C3): the residual
        covariance $\Sigma_r$, tests on whitened, nuisance-projected residuals at a stated
        false-alarm rate, and an effective number of pixels and minimum detectable bias that
        follow from $\Sigma_r$ (Section~\ref{sec:method:forward}).
  \item A closed-form shared Gaussian surface as the consensus (C3, C7), with exact
        leave-view-out equal to RAIM solution separation, cost linear in the number of views,
        and per-region posterior, protection level and view count
        (Section~\ref{sec:method:surface}).
  \item Exclusion, a no-consensus alarm, availability and a conditional protection level
        (Sections~\ref{sec:method:elim} and~\ref{sec:method:pl}), and a corrected admission
        criterion (C6, Section~\ref{sec:method:admission}).
  \item Depth and pose fault models, a regime map of per-pixel bias against the fraction of the
        view affected, and ten pre-registered hypotheses, with breakdown scenarios, that state
        when gating helps and when it does not (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
\end{itemize}
