\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Depth-based 3D reconstruction increasingly combines several range sensors with
different physics: LiDAR, continuous-wave time-of-flight (ToF) cameras,
structured-light RGB-D cameras and stereo rigs. Their error models differ in
range dependence, incidence-angle dependence and failure modes
\cite{noise_khoshelham,noise_nguyen,noise_lange,noise_sarbolandi,noise_matthies}.
Fusing them is attractive because each sensor covers weaknesses of the others,
but fusion also couples the sensors: one sensor with a wrong extrinsic
calibration, a depth bias, or multipath contamination can corrupt a shared
surface that every other sensor is trying to estimate.

Existing robustness mechanisms mostly act \emph{per pixel}. Depth-map fusion
rejects a measurement when too few other views support it or when other views
see free space in front of it
\cite{fus_merrell,fus_colmap_mvs,fus_openmvs}; robust Gaussian-splatting
methods down-weight or mask pixels whose residuals are large
\cite{gs_spotless,gs_robustsplat}; learned multi-sensor fusion predicts
per-voxel sensor weights \cite{fus_senfunet}. These mechanisms work well when
errors are sparse and spatially incoherent. They are weaker when a sensor fails
\emph{coherently}: a drifted extrinsic or a smooth depth bias moves a whole
image in the same direction, and the corrupted measurements can agree with each
other, with the fitted surface, or both. Where only one or two other sensors
cover a pixel, or where the faulty sensor supplies most of the coverage, a
per-pixel vote cannot attribute the disagreement to the right source. In
navigation, this attribution problem is classical, and \emph{sensor-level}
fault detection and exclusion has a long history
\cite{flt_raim_parkinson,flt_raim_brown,flt_raim_joerger,flt_lamport,flt_marzullo}.
We are not aware of work that tests whole depth sensors against a Gaussian-splat
consensus surface and excludes them before dense fusion;
Section~\ref{sec:related} states what the closest works do and do not do.

\paragraph{Approach.}
We represent the shared surface with a fixed budget of $N$ Gaussian splats
\cite{gs_3dgs,gs_mcmc}, fit it from all sensors' depth, render the consensus
back into each sensor, and compute a per-sensor disagreement statistic from
visibility-aware, noise-normalised residuals. A sensor (or frame) is admitted to
the consensus only if it passes an evidence test; the survivors are fused.
The admission test adapts the consensus-growing idea of Ambarkutuk
\emph{et~al.}\ \cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, who eliminate Byzantine sensors by
back-propagating each sensor's signal to a common source space and building a
consensus. As written, their entropy criterion cannot distinguish an agreeing
sensor from a sharp but biased one (Section~\ref{sec:method}); we replace it
with a Bayes-factor admission test and use entropy only to select among
admissible consensus sets.

\paragraph{Contributions.} This manuscript makes the following contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A sensor-level fault-elimination method for multi-sensor depth fusion on
        a fixed-budget Gaussian-splat surface, with per-ray variance models that
        set the splat covariance and weight the residuals
        (Sections~\ref{sec:method:noise}--\ref{sec:method:consensus}).
  \item A corrected, self-consistent admission criterion for consensus-based
        sensor elimination, with an analysis of when an entropy criterion
        reduces to a leave-one-out chi-square test and when it does not
        (Section~\ref{sec:method:admission}).
  \item A pre-registered evaluation protocol with five falsifiable hypotheses,
        a deterministic fault-injection grid (extrinsic drift, depth bias,
        multipath surrogate), explicit breakdown scenarios, and baselines from
        three families (OpenMVS-style depth-map fusion, classical fusion, learned
        fusion) (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
\end{enumerate}
