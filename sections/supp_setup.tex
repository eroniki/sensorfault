\subsubsection{Depth model}
\label{sec:setup:model}
\runin{Default: Depth Pro.} The main setup applies one metric model to each image. We use
Depth Pro \cite{est_depthpro} for three reasons taken from its description: it predicts
depth with absolute scale, so depth maps of different views are comparable without a
per-view scale fit, which matters when each view has a single source and a free alignment
could absorb a fault; it does not rely on camera intrinsics or other metadata; and it is fast
(its authors report a 2.25-megapixel depth map in 0.3 seconds on a standard GPU), which
bounds the cost of $N$ inferences per scene. It also produces sharp boundaries, which
helps a surface fit. UniDepth and Metric3D v2 \cite{est_unidepth,est_metric3dv2} are
comparable metric alternatives. The default is confirmed on development scenes by comparing
the aligned depth error of the candidates against the laser reference; it is never selected
on test scenes. \TODO{Check Depth Pro's weight licence and GPU memory on an 8~GB card,
and record the development-scene comparison; if the check disfavours it, switch to the
best candidate and say so.}

\runin{Ensemble ablation.} The ablation adds UniDepth and Metric3D v2, and optionally the
affine-invariant MoGe \cite{est_moge}, to give $M\in\{2,3,4\}$ models per view
(Section~\ref{sec:method:ensemble}). The overlap of their training data is only coarsely
documented, so the ablation measures how often models fail together instead of assuming it.

\subsubsection{Fault injection}
\label{sec:setup:faults}
The fault classes are defined in Section~\ref{sec:method:defs}; this subsection fixes the
grid. Faults are injected into the depth map or the pose prior of selected views, by
deterministic, seeded scripts, so every realisation is reproducible from a manifest. Seeds are
derived as $\mathrm{SeedSequence}([\text{master},\,h(\text{view id}),\,\text{fault id}])$ with a
stable hash and at least five master seeds. The grid is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{coherent bias (the regime map):} a per-pixel bias of
        $\delta\in\{0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,1,2,4\}\,\sigma$ on a fraction
        $f\in\{0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1,0.2,0.5,0.8\}$ of the pixels of one view, either in a contiguous
        region or scattered at random (the two differ in the effective number of independent
        pixels), with both signs; and, as an \emph{affine control}, the same bias on all pixels
        ($f=1$) and a bias proportional to $\sigma(d)$, which lie in the span of the scale and
        offset and are predicted to be neither detected nor harmful (H1d);
  \item \emph{smooth warp:} a random low-frequency field added to the inverse depth,
        with amplitude $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ times the standard deviation of the
        inverse depth of the view;
  \item \emph{region failure:} a connected region covering $20$--$40\%$ of the pixels is
        replaced by a plane fitted to the whole view or by a blurred copy;
  \item \emph{non-affine distortion:} $D'=D^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma\in\{0.8,0.9,1.1,1.25\}$;
  \item \emph{spoofed pose prior:} a consistent or linearly drifting offset of the pose prior
        over a block of views, of $\{1,2.5,5,10\}$~cm and $\{0.1,0.25,0.5,1\}^{\circ}$;
  \item \emph{intermittent pose glitches:} isolated views whose prior jumps by
        $\{10,25,50\}$~cm and $\{2,5,10\}^{\circ}$;
  \item \emph{false loop closures:} $\{1,3,5\}$ spurious relative-pose constraints between
        non-neighbouring views, with a random wrong relative pose and a plausible inlier ratio;
  \item \emph{persistence:} a fraction $p\in\{10,20,40,60\}\%$ of the views, scattered at
        random or clustered in contiguous views; a fault on all views is the model-level
        breakdown scenario;
  \item \emph{controls:} sparse independent outliers at the same corrupted-pixel rate, and
        fault-free runs;
  \item \emph{ensemble ablation:} 1 to $\lceil M/2\rceil$ of the $M$ models corrupted on the
        same views, with independent parameters and, separately, identical parameters
        (common mode).
\end{itemize}
Natural failures need no injection: a view is labelled faulty when, after robust affine
alignment, its error against the laser reference exceeds a threshold fixed on development
scenes. \TODO{Fix the labelling threshold on development scenes.}
Overlap between views, the clustering of faulty views, the fold layout relative to the
clusters, and the spatial layout of the bias mask are controlled variables, because a pixel
filter or a kernel does not fail merely because a fault is coherent: it is expected to fail
when the per-pixel bias is below the noise, when faulty views have few neighbours, or when
neighbouring views fail together (challenge C1).

\subsubsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:setup:baselines}
Every method receives the same images, matches, depth maps, fault realisations and
pose-graph solver; thresholds are tuned on development scenes only, with the tuning
budget reported. Re-implementations are labelled as such and released. Methods that do not
exclude a view, namely robust kernels, soft weights and pixel filters, are compared on
detection by an \emph{action rate} calibrated to the same false-alarm rate as the gate: a view
counts as detected when its weight falls below one half (or, for a pixel filter, when it loses
more than the pixel fraction that removes 5\% of healthy views).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Robust kernels in the pose graph (no gate).} Huber, Cauchy and
        Geman--McClure kernels \cite{flt_huber,rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron,rob_mactavish},
        graduated non-convexity \cite{rob_gnc}, switchable constraints \cite{rob_switchable},
        dynamic covariance scaling \cite{rob_dcs} and max-mixtures \cite{rob_maxmix}, applied to
        the geometric and the depth constraints; the last three are the strong baselines for
        false loop closures.
  \item \textbf{Soft view down-weighting.} A per-view weight $w_i\in[0,1]$ estimated inside
        the differentiable pose graph from the view's residual (a Student-$t$ or learned-scale
        weight), with no hard exclusion and no alarm.
  \item \textbf{COLMAP geometric consistency filter.} The pixel-wise cross-view depth and
        reprojection consistency test of COLMAP \cite{fus_colmap_mvs}, run with the same
        poses, masking depth constraints and fusion pixel by pixel; and, as the strongest
        pixel-based competitor, the same pixel flags \emph{aggregated} over the view into a
        count test with the same false-alarm rate.
  \item \textbf{Plain voting.} Per-pixel median and majority across the views that observe
        a pixel, with no gate.
  \item \textbf{No gate, no robust kernel;} and an \textbf{oracle gate} that drops
        exactly the faulty views (from the injection manifest, or from the laser
        reference for natural failures), as an upper bound.
  \item \textbf{Gate variants:} a chi-square (energy) gate and the evidence gate of
        Section~\ref{sec:method:admission}, a region-scan gate, and their combination, each
        against the closed-form shared surface (leave-view-out by subtraction)
        and against a \emph{pairwise}
        consensus that warps the neighbouring views into the view under test
        (Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise}); three ablations of the shared surface: no
        leave-view-out (the surface contains the view under test, a joint fit),
        a fixed-budget 3DGS-MCMC surface \cite{gs_mcmc} fitted by optimisation in place of the closed
        form (with explicit leave-view-out refits as its reference), and a single-covariance
        variant in which extent and uncertainty are not separated; and, as a context for the choice of dropping over correcting, an
        estimate-and-correct baseline that refits the alignment of every view without
        dropping any.
  \item \textbf{Ensemble ablation (H10).} Cross-model voting (per-pixel median or majority
        across the $M$ models, flagging a view when the models disagree) and the ensemble
        gate of Section~\ref{sec:method:ensemble}.
  \item \textbf{Reference points, not rivals.} MASt3R-SfM \cite{rec_mast3rsfm} and VGGT
        \cite{rec_vggt}, run as released on the same images, with classical COLMAP SfM
        \cite{fus_colmap_sfm} as a classical reference. They do not use our depth
        maps and therefore do not see the injected faults; they are reported to place
        the test bed in context, and no claim is made to beat them.
\end{itemize}
All methods fuse their kept depth with the same closed-form surface (the baselines without
gating, with a robust kernel in the information weights); a TSDF of the same depth is a secondary check.
Every method is timed. The test bed is an offline pipeline, and we report latency and memory
per stage and state which methods do not meet a per-frame real-time budget.

\subsubsection{Metrics and statistics}
\label{sec:setup:metrics}
Integrity metrics are the primary ones, and the measurement model is checked first: the fitted
$\sigma(\cdot)$, $\ell$ and $\nu$ of each model, the effective number of pixels of
\eqref{eq:neff} per view, the mean of $T_i/(n-2)$ on fault-free views and the false-alarm rate
against $\alpha$ (the adequacy of the nominal model). Per view and per fault cell, the gate is scored by its
\emph{detection power} at a fixed 5\% false-alarm probability (the regime map is the
heatmap of power over $(\delta,f)$), the \emph{empirical minimum detectable bias} (the smallest
$\delta$ with power $0.9$, to be compared with \eqref{eq:mdb} computed from the measured
$\Sigma_r$), the false-alarm and
missed-detection rates, the rate of no-consensus alarms, and \emph{availability}, the fraction
of views that are tested and kept. Precision and recall against the oracle labels and the
\emph{elimination curve AUC}, the normalised partial area under power versus clean-run
false-alarm rate on $[0,0.2]$ (it equals the ROC AUC at $\mathrm{FER}_{\max}=1$), summarise
the trade-off. For H9 the realised error of the output $q$ is compared with the protection
level \eqref{eq:pl}: the exceedance rate. Downstream accuracy is reported as absolute
trajectory error after similarity alignment, relative pose error, the area under the curve of
the relative pose error at fixed angular thresholds, and fused-surface accuracy, completeness
and F-score against the laser reference; accuracy and completeness are reported separately,
because dropping a view lowers completeness wherever only that view saw a region. Depth
quality is the absolute relative error and the fraction within a ratio of the reference after
alignment. Gate cost is reported as wall-clock time and peak GPU memory against the number of
views $N$ (log--log slope), the number of surface fits, and the mean co-visibility degree
$\bar d$. For the ablation, the failure indicator of a model on a view is that its aligned
error against the laser reference exceeds the same threshold as for natural failures;
agreement between models is reported as the joint failure frequency, its ratio to the product
of the marginals (lift), and the phi coefficient. Intervals are scene-level bootstrap
confidence intervals, with paired Wilcoxon tests against baselines and scenes as the unit.


\subsection{Implementation}
\label{sec:setup:protocol}
Hypotheses, thresholds, metrics, the tuning protocol and the dev/test split are fixed in a
preregistration file and tagged in version control before any test scene is processed;
the runner refuses the test split without that tag. The measurement model is calibrated once per depth model on clean development data
(Section~\ref{sec:method:calibration}): $\sigma(r,\tau,\theta)$, $\ell$ and $\nu$ by maximum
likelihood of the residuals against the laser reference depth, $\Sigma_q$ from the marginal
covariances of the provisional pose graph, and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{surf}}$ online from the composite variance of the closed-form surface; the confidence-covariate variant uses Metric3D v2 or UniDepthV2, which
output a confidence, as ablation models. The gate threshold is then calibrated by permutation on
clean development runs, with $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ computed from $\Sigma_r$ by \eqref{eq:neff}, to a
5\% per-view false-alarm rate. At least six
scenes (three development, three test) and five seeds are used per dataset. The
test bed uses an off-the-shelf feature matcher, a Gauss--Newton or Levenberg--Marquardt
pose-graph solver \cite{opt_g2o,opt_factorgraphs,opt_theseus}, and the closed-form Gaussian surface of Section~\ref{sec:method:surface}, with a TSDF
\cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion} as a secondary check. The surface has no iterations and no
folds; its parameters are the association gate $(\tau_h,\tau_{\mathrm{ext}},\cos_{\min})$,
the footprint scale of the extent, and the support $\tau_\alpha$ and $s_{\min}$. The
fixed-budget MCMC ablation uses a fixed $N_s$ and a fixed number of iterations $n_{\mathrm{it}}$.
Depth is computed once per view and cached, so the
default setup runs $N$ model inferences per scene and the ensemble ablation $MN$; the
surface build and render, the pose graph and, for the reference points, MASt3R-SfM and VGGT are the
other costs, and their memory on an 8~GB card is checked before the scenes are fixed. The sensitivities of H9 are the closed-form slopes of \eqref{eq:plh} for primitives and, for scalar outputs, estimated by perturbing a view and re-fusing.

\subsection{Hypotheses in Full}
\label{sup:hyp}
The following predictions are pre-registered. Each states what would falsify it, and several
state where the gate is expected \emph{not} to help.
\begin{description}
  \item[H1] \emph{(regime map: where pixel filters and robust kernels fail and the gate does not).}
        With one metric model whose depth carries a coherent bias of $\delta\sigma$ on a
        fraction $f$ of one view, at 5\% false alarm: (a)~the gate's empirical minimum
        detectable bias follows \eqref{eq:mdb} computed from the measured $\Sigma_r$ within a
        factor of 2; (b)~in the sub-noise regime ($\delta\le0.5$, $0.2\le f\le0.8$) the gate
        detects at least 80\% of the faulty views, whereas the best pixel-wise filter, robust
        kernel and soft weight each detect at most 20\%; (c)~in the sparse-outlier regime
        ($\delta\ge4$, $f\le0.02$) the best pixel filter detects at least as many views as the
        gate, and the gate is not expected to win; (d)~a uniform bias ($f=1$) and a bias
        proportional to $\sigma$ are neither detected (power at most the false-alarm rate plus 5
        points) nor harmful (no increase of the trajectory or fused-surface error above the
        clean value by more than 5\%), because the per-view alignment absorbs them.
        \emph{Falsified if} (a)~the empirical and predicted minimum detectable bias differ by
        more than a factor of 2, (b)~the detection gap at $\delta=0.3$, $f=0.5$ is below 30 points
        or its interval includes zero, (c)~the gate beats the best pixel filter by 10 points or
        more in the sparse-outlier regime, or (d)~the uniform bias is detected or degrades the
        output.
  \item[H2] \emph{(consequence for the trajectory and the fused surface).} In the sub-noise regime
        of H1, the gated pipeline reduces the absolute trajectory error and the fused-surface
        error by at least 25\% relative to the best tuned of the robust kernels, soft weights,
        the COLMAP consistency filter and plain voting, and lands within 20\% of the oracle
        gate; in the sparse-outlier regime they tie or win, and that is reported.
        \emph{Falsified if} the paired confidence interval of the improvement includes zero in
        the sub-noise regime, or the median error is more than 20\% above the oracle gate.
  \item[H3] \emph{(statistic).} (a)~At a fixed 5\% false-alarm rate the evidence criterion exceeds the
        chi-square (energy) gate by at least 10 points of detection power, only where
        heteroscedastic depth noise, grazing geometry, occlusion or non-Gaussian residuals break
        the chi-square assumptions, and ties under idealised Gaussian noise
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:loo}). (b)~The region-scan statistic beats the energy statistic by at
        least 20 points for a bias confined to a region, the energy statistic beats the scan by at
        least 20 points for a smooth warp that is diffuse over the view, and the combined gate
        loses at most 3 points to the better of the two. \emph{Falsified if} (a)~there is no gain
        in any pre-registered regime (the fallback is to report the gate with the chi-square
        statistic, with the entropy relation as an equivalence result) or (b)~either gap is below
        10 points.
  \item[H4] \emph{(leave-view-out, alignment and support).} Testing a depth map against a
        surface from which its view has been \emph{subtracted}, after a scale-and-shift fit of
        the depth map to the rendering, and only on pixels the surface supports, keeps the
        false-alarm rate on clean data at or below 5\%; testing against a joint surface that
        contains the view lets the surface absorb the fault and
        lowers detection power by at least 10 points, and removing the support mask at
        least doubles the false-alarm rate. \emph{Falsified if} the ablations leave
        the rate and the power unchanged.
  \item[H5] \emph{(placement, soft down-weighting and complementarity).} Gating before pose graph
        optimisation gives lower trajectory error than applying the same statistic after the
        graph, and lower than soft per-view down-weighting inside the differentiable
        optimisation, in the sub-noise regime; the gate combined with a robust kernel is at least
        as accurate as either alone. \emph{Falsified if} soft down-weighting matches the gate in
        the sub-noise regime (it would then suffice), if gating after the graph matches gating
        before it, or if the combination is worse than the better component.
  \item[H6] \emph{(adequacy of the measurement model, price of monitoring on clean data, and reference         points).} The nominal model is adequate: on fault-free views the mean of $T_i/(n-2)$ is
        $1\pm0.1$ and the false-alarm rate is within $[0.5\alpha,1.5\alpha]$ without per-scene
        retuning, with $\sigma(\cdot)$, $\ell$ and $\nu$ fitted once on development scenes. On
        fault-free data the per-view false-alarm rate is at most 5\%, availability is at least 90\%, and the gate
        changes trajectory and fused-surface error by at most 5\% relative to the pipeline
        without it; on natural failures the gated test bed narrows the gap to the reference
        points MASt3R-SfM and VGGT, which are reported as context. \emph{Falsified if} the
        mean of $T_i/(n-2)$ leaves $1\pm0.1$ or the false-alarm rate leaves its band (the model is
        misspecified and the minimum detectable bias of H1 cannot be trusted), the price of gating
        on clean data exceeds 5\%, or availability falls below 90\%.
  \item[H7] \emph{(shared surface versus pairwise checks).} (a)~\emph{Efficiency:} the wall-clock
        time of the closed-form shared-surface gate grows linearly with the number of views (log--log
        slope below 1.2), whereas the pairwise gate grows with $N\bar d$ (slope above 1.5 on
        dense overlap graphs), and for $N\ge100$ views at mean co-visibility degree
        $\bar d\ge10$ the shared-surface gate is at least 2$\times$ faster; for sparse
        graphs the pairwise gate is expected to be cheaper, and the break-even degree is
        reported. (b)~\emph{Robustness:} at 5\% false alarm, the shared-surface gate's
        detection power is within 5 points of the pairwise gate's (non-inferiority) in the
        regimes of H1. (c)~\emph{Closed form versus optimised:} the closed-form surface is
        within 3 points of the fixed-budget MCMC surface with explicit leave-view-out refits
        at a fraction of its cost, and each region yields a posterior, a protection level and
        a view count whose coverage is reported. (d)~\emph{Association:} the gate is robust to
        the order of the views, and the cold start and association failure are measured as
        breakdown scenarios. \emph{Falsified if} (a)~the speed-up is below 2$\times$ at
        dense overlap or the slopes do not separate, (b)~the pairwise gate is more than
        5 points better, or (c)~the closed form is more than 3 points worse than the
        optimised surface.
  \item[H8] \emph{(pose faults).} (a)~For spoofed pose priors and glitches, the gate detects at
        least 80\% of the views whose pose error exceeds the minimum detectable bias that
        \eqref{eq:mdb} implies through the pose Jacobian \eqref{eq:Jq}, and excluding them lowers the
        trajectory error relative to robust kernels. (b)~For large false loop closures,
        switchable constraints, dynamic covariance scaling and max-mixtures, which were
        designed for them, match the gate within 5 points, and the gate is not expected to win.
        (c)~For small coherent pose offsets ($\le0.5^{\circ}$) the gate beats every robust kernel
        by at least 20 points of detection power. \emph{Falsified if} (a)~the power above the
        minimum detectable bias is below 80\%, (b)~the gate beats the best loop-closure
        baseline by more than 10 points, or (c)~the interval of the gap in (c) includes zero.
  \item[H9] \emph{(protection level; optional).} Under single-fault injections that satisfy
        assumptions A1--A4 of Section~\ref{sec:method:pl}, with a fault at the minimum detectable
        bias, the realised error of the output $q$ exceeds the protection level \eqref{eq:pl}, computed
        from the measured $\Sigma_r$, in at most $\beta_{\mathrm{md}}+5$ points of the runs; under two faulty views in a region or a model-level
        fault, where the assumptions fail, the exceedance rate is more than $2\beta_{\mathrm{md}}$, which
        documents where the bound does not hold. \emph{Falsified if} the exceedance under single
        faults is above $\beta_{\mathrm{md}}+5$ points.
  \item[H10] \emph{(ensemble ablation).} (a)~\emph{Shared failures:} on natural failures, models
        that overlap in training data fail on the same views more often than independence
        predicts: for each pair of models the lift of the joint failure frequency is at
        least 2 and the phi coefficient at least 0.3. (b)~\emph{Where voting breaks:} on
        views where at least half of the models fail, cross-model voting detects the failing
        view in at most 20\% of cases (the models agree with each other), whereas the
        single-model gate against the surface detects at least 20 points more of them; the
        converse is reported, not predicted: on views the surface cannot support (too few
        other views), voting is expected to detect what the gate cannot. (c)~\emph{Value of the
        ensemble:} adding the models to the gate raises detection power at 5\% false alarm
        by at least 5 points over the single-model gate, which would justify the $M\times$
        inference cost. \emph{Falsified if} (a)~the lift interval includes 1 (failures are
        independent), (b)~voting's power on common-mode views is within 10 points of the
        gate's, or (c)~the gain from the ensemble includes zero.
\end{description}




\subsection{Depth-Model Confidence Outputs}
Per their public documentation
(checked 2026-11-14), Metric3D v2 returns a depth confidence (and a normal confidence) for its
ViT models, and UniDepthV2 lists a confidence output; Depth Pro returns depth and an estimated
focal length but no confidence, MoGe returns a binary validity mask rather than a confidence, and
the documentation of Depth Anything V2 and Marigold describes no uncertainty output. The default
model therefore needs its $\sigma(\cdot)$ fitted, and the confidence-output models are the
candidates for the confidence-covariate variant.
