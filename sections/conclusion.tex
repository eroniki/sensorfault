\section{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have proposed a view-level gate that decides which views' depth maps to keep before pose
graph optimisation and fusion. One monocular metric depth model is applied to each RGB
image; the gate tests each depth map once against a single shared Gaussian-splat surface,
fitted with a fixed number of splats from the other views and rendered at its view, so that
its cost grows with the number of views rather than the number of view pairs. A depth map is
compared after a scale-and-shift alignment, on supported pixels, and kept only if its mean
log Bayes factor against the rendered surface passes a calibrated threshold; the surface
refitted on the kept depth maps is the fused output. The admission test corrects the entropy
criterion of our earlier Byzantine sensor elimination, which could not distinguish an
agreeing source from a sharp but biased one, and returns \emph{no consensus} when attribution
is not identifiable. A test bed, consisting of a depth model, a partly end-to-end pose graph
and the fused surface, provides the depth maps and the downstream task; MASt3R-SfM and VGGT
are reference points, robust kernels, the COLMAP geometric consistency filter and plain
voting are the baselines the gate must beat, a pairwise gate is the comparison for the shared
surface, and a multi-model ensemble is an ablation that asks whether models fail on the same
views and whether cross-model voting breaks where the gate does not. The empirical case rests

\subsection{Limitations and Failure Conditions}
\label{sec:disc:limits}
The gate is built on a consensus of views and fails where the consensus is wrong or not
identifiable. First, it needs the other views to expose a faulty one: a model-level fault,
where all views are wrong in the same way, makes the surface agree with the faulty depth
and is undetectable from depth alone, and a minority assumption is needed in every region.
Second, with very few views, or in regions that few other views observe, the surface cannot
test a view and the gate can only return no consensus. Third, the gate targets structure that
the scale-and-shift alignment cannot remove; a pure scale or shift error is absorbed by the
alignment, in the gate and in the pose graph, and is not detected. Fourth, the gate uses
provisional poses to render the surface, so poor provisional poses can corrupt both the
surface and the gate. The surface can also be corrupted by faulty depth maps in the folds
used to fit it: cross-fitting holds out a block of neighbouring views together, but faulty
views in other folds that support the same fault, or faulty views that supply most of the
coverage of a region, can make the surface follow the fault and mask it; a fixed splat
budget limits but does not remove this. A depth-only surface is weakly constrained and needs
the alpha, anisotropy and normal-consistency regularisers, and the choice of the number of
folds trades coverage of the held-out views against masking. The cost advantage holds only
for dense overlap; for sparse view graphs the pairwise gate is cheaper. Fifth, faults below
the per-pixel noise can be detected only by aggregating over many pixels, so there is a
detection floor that depends on overlap and pixel count. Sixth, dropping a view lowers
completeness wherever only that view covered a region. Seventh, the injected faults are
controlled surrogates of natural failures; natural failures are scored against a laser
reference on a small number of scenes, and the scenes are static. Eighth, a single default
model was chosen on development scenes, and the conclusions are for that model; the
ensemble ablation tests whether they carry over. Finally, the surface fits and the depth
model cost memory and time; the test bed is an offline pipeline.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{sec:disc:future}
Planned extensions, none yet started, are: adding a photometric term to the surface fit,
which would give an evidence source that does not come from the depth model and could
help against model-level failures; training the gate through the differentiable back-end; running it online on video under a per-frame latency budget; handling dynamic
scenes; and applying the same gate to depth from physical sensors (LiDAR, time-of-flight,
