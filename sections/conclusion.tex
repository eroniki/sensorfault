\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have proposed a depth-only view gate for depth-supervised Gaussian splatting. Each surfel keeps a
$10\times10$ uncertainty apart from its rendering shape, the rasteriser renders it into depth variance,
and leaving a view or a block of neighbouring frames out is a subtraction and one Newton step. Each view is
tested against the others' rendered surface at a stated false-alarm rate, in rounds under a Holm schedule.
In controlled experiments with exact depth, the loop kept the family-wise false alarm at zero, also with
miscalibrated noise, and avoided the swamping of clean neighbours that a single pass causes. Of the two
pose predictions, detection held and recovery was falsified, because the perturbed frame's own GNSS/IMU
prior holds its pose.

\runin{Limitations.} Independent per-surfel blocks ignore cross-surfel and within-view correlations, so the
rendered variance is overconfident; the threshold is calibrated on clean data, and H5 measures the gap.
Errors repeated across whole strips are invisible to the gate, and leave-block-out trades power and
availability for robustness to repetition. Pixels that no other view sees are untestable, and a region in
which faulty views are a majority pulls the cavity towards the fault. The leave-out is exact only for the
linear model. A full list is in Supplement~\ref{S-sup:limits}.

\runin{Future work.} Low-rank cross-surfel correlation, joint testing of poses and depth, using the
rendered uncertainty for view selection, and relighting the appearance block \cite{data_beyondlight} so
that colour can help without shaping the geometry.
