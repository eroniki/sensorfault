\section{Conclusions, Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We have proposed view-level fault detection and exclusion, in the spirit of receiver autonomous
integrity monitoring, for monocular-depth reconstruction. A coherent view-level fault adds a small,
spatially structured bias that the monocular scale and offset cannot absorb; it is sub-noise at
each pixel, so pixel filters and robust kernels do not see it, but it is a large statistic once the
view's residual is whitened by the covariance of an explicit measurement model and projected
orthogonally to the scale and offset. The gate tests each depth map once against a closed-form
shared surface of Gaussian primitives from which the view's information is subtracted exactly,
excludes it when its statistic exceeds a calibrated threshold, raises a no-consensus alarm when the
other views cannot test it, and returns the fused surface with a posterior, a conditional
protection level and a view count per region. The empirical case rests on hypotheses H1--H10,
none of which has been tested in this version.

\subsection{Limitations and Failure Conditions}
\label{sec:disc:limits}
The gate is a monitor built on a consensus of views, and it fails where the consensus is wrong or
not identifiable. (1)~A model-level fault, where all views are wrong alike, makes the surface agree
with the faulty depth and is undetectable from depth alone; a minority of faulty views in every
region is assumed. (2)~With very few views, or in regions that few other views observe, the gate
can only raise a no-consensus alarm. (3)~The detection floor $(z_\alpha+z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}})/\kappa$ and the
false-alarm rate are only as good as the measurement model, which is calibrated on clean data and
checked by H6; a smooth, textureless scene has a small $n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and a high floor for
diffuse faults. (4)~The protection level is conditional on a fault being present and rests on a
single faulty view, calibrated noise, a linear response and an uncorrupted surface; unlike RAIM we
have no prior fault probabilities. (5)~A pure scale or shift error is absorbed by the alignment and
is neither detected nor harmful. (6)~A depth bias and a pose fault look alike to the gate, which
does not isolate which is at fault. (7)~Provisional poses render the surface, so poor poses can
corrupt both. (8)~Several faulty views supporting the same fault, or faulty views supplying most of
a region's coverage, can make the surface follow the fault. (9)~Association is order dependent at
the cold start, and a faulty view with a large offset may spawn primitives that no other view can
test. (10)~Planar primitives fit thin or curved structure poorly; information-form fusion treats
observations as independent and is overconfident under correlated depth error and shared pose
error; the offset variance omits normal uncertainty; the surface variance is diagonal.
(11)~The cost advantage holds only for dense overlap, and loop-closure baselines are the right
tool for large false loops. (12)~Injected faults are surrogates of natural failures, scored on a
few static scenes for one default model.

\subsection{Future Work}
\label{sec:disc:future}
Planned, none started: separating depth from pose faults by re-alignment to the surface;
allocating an integrity risk from measured fault frequencies; training the gate through the
differentiable back-end; online and dynamic-scene operation; and the same gate for physical depth
sensors (LiDAR, time of flight), where extrinsic drift and multipath are fault classes.
