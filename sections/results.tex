\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\textcolor{red}{\textbf{Two controlled experiments on one MatrixCity block have been run, E1 and the H7
pose check, together with an RGB rendering check and a surface comparison. Every other table and figure
is a placeholder and must be regenerated by the experiment code before submission.}}

\subsection{E1: Controlled Fault Injection with Exact Depth}
\label{sec:results:e1}
E1 uses one MatrixCity aerial block \cite{data_matrixcity} (small city, block~3: 185 frames on an
$8\times8$ grid of positions flown in four headings, 30$^{\circ}$ off nadir, 300~m altitude). Only its
float32 depth and poses were used, at $96\times54$ pixels per view. It tests the statistical gate with
exact truth and nothing else: surfels are placed on the exact geometry (81{,}152 surfels from 3~m voxels),
the measured depth is the surfels' rendered depth plus independent noise with standard deviation 1\%
of depth, plus injected faults, and the linearised fusion is exact for this model. There is no gsplat
training, no opacity, no pose refinement and no real depth model, and the noise is spatially
independent; H1, H2, H4, H5 and H7 are therefore not addressed. Faults are rectangular regions offset
by $\delta\sigma$ on one view, or the same $8\sigma$ offset on a 25~m ground patch in three neighbouring
frames of a strip. Holm runs over $M=NR_{\max}=1850$ tests; the region scan uses $4\times6$ image tiles.
Table~\ref{tab:e1} summarises the outcomes, Figures~\ref{fig:e1grid}--\ref{fig:e1repeated} compare
the variants, and the runtime is in Supplement~\ref{S-sup:results} (Figure~\ref{S-fig:e1runtime}); qualitative
checks of the test, the residual uncertainty and the surfel map are in Supplement~\ref{S-sup:e1qual}
(Figures~\ref{S-fig:e1qualviews}--\ref{S-fig:e1qualtop}); the data behind them are in the code repository
(\texttt{results/e1}).

The Holm-controlled loop kept the family-wise false alarm at zero in 20 clean scenes, and the scene
temperature was what kept it there when the noise was 30\% above its calibrated level. Without the
temperature every view was flagged; with it, the outcome was the same as at nominal noise. Single-view
regional faults of 2--16$\sigma$ over 5--40\% of a view were all detected, so this grid is too easy to
separate the gate from the aligned-RMS baseline. The baseline differed only in the weakest cell, $2\sigma$
over 5\%, where it detected 7 of 10. Against the predictions of H6, two results do not hold here.
Leave-block-out ($B=3$) detected no more of the three repeated faulty frames than $B=1$: in this block,
three frames supply only a small share of the coverage of the faulty patch, so they did not mask each
other. The outer loop did not raise detection either. What the loop changed was false exclusions. A
single pass that excludes every rejected view at once excluded 5--8\% of clean views, neighbours
\emph{swamped} by the faulty frames. The loop, dropping one view per round, excluded none. Two faulty
frames out of 30 were never detected, in two of the ten scenes. In this exact, independent-noise setting
the energy statistic was slightly conservative, mean $T/m=0.965$, and the per-view false-alarm rate of a
single test at $p<0.05$ was 7.5\% with the temperature. The temperature is fitted from the conservative
energy statistics and so slightly loosens the region scan; the Holm schedule absorbs this. Whitening kept the correlation between pixels that one surfel renders (Section~\ref{sec:method:gate});
without it, the region scan raised false alarms on weakly supported views in a dry run. Figure~\ref{fig:e1compact} shows the test on single views of one
scene: the fault stands out in the standardised residual and the region scan, and the neighbour that it
swamps is flagged only while the faulty view is still in its cavity. The loop
ranked views by $\log p$, so that a swamped neighbour whose p-value underflows to zero cannot be
dropped before the faulty view.

\subsection{RGB Rendering Check}
\label{sec:results:rgb}
The gate uses depth only. To check that the same block also supports a photometric surfel model, one
2DGS model was trained with gsplat on the 185 clean RGB training images of MatrixCity block~3 at
$960\times540$ (Supplement~\ref{S-sup:rgb}). On the 19 held-out views, which lie between the training
positions and point in headings that the training views do not use, PSNR was $23.1\pm4.3$~dB and SSIM
$0.778\pm0.096$ (Table~\ref{S-tab:rgb}). Figure~\ref{fig:rgbcompact} shows the median test view; the
weakest views look beyond the area that the training views cover (Figure~\ref{S-fig:rgbviews}).

\subsection{Surfaces Against Ground Truth}
\label{sec:results:surface}
Both models of MatrixCity block~3 were also compared as surfaces. Each view's depth was back-projected,
each view kept its highest point per grid cell, and the surface model (DSM) is the median of these over
the views that see the cell; the same operator, applied to the exact depth of the 185 frames, gives the
ground truth (Supplement~\ref{S-sup:surface}). For the RGB 2DGS model (median depth, 2~m cells), the
height residual on land had median $+0.49$~m, RMSE 6.0~m, and 81\% of cells within 1~m. Water, 46\% of
the covered area and a flat plane in the ground truth, was not recovered: median $+5.7$~m, 95th percentile
$+48$~m, 5\% of cells within 1~m (Figure~\ref{fig:surfacecompact}b). The textureless water surface is the
kind of region where image-based geometry fails. For the E1 surfels (4~m cells), the scene of
Figure~\ref{fig:e1compact} was fused twice: with all views, and without the one view the gate's loop
excluded (the faulty view, after two rounds). Over the cells under the injected fault, the median
residual was $-2.16$~m with the faulty view and $-0.16$~m without it, against $-0.19$~m for the same
noise without a fault; 18\%, 54\% and 57\% of these cells were within 1~m. Over the whole block the three
surfaces differ by at most 0.03~m in median and RMSE, because the fault covers 3\% of the cells.

\subsection{H7: Pose Perturbation Check}
\label{sec:results:h7}
H7 was run on MatrixCity block~3 in the E1 setting (exact geometry, independent noise of 1\% of depth,
$96\times54$ pixels per view), after the gate had run on the fault scene of Figure~\ref{fig:e1compact} and
excluded its faulty view. The input (GNSS/IMU) poses of chosen views were perturbed by known amounts: an
isolated clean view, a block of three consecutive clean views of a strip sharing one perturbation, or the
flagged view, one group at a time. Rotations of 0.05--2$^{\circ}$ about a random axis, translations of 1--50
ground sampling distances (GSD, 0.141~m at the block's median range of 327~m) in a random direction, and
both at paired levels; two seeds, 16 perturbed clean views per level, and a size-0 control. Every view is
rendered at its pose by one surfel renderer, the map is fused at the given poses, and each view is tested
with the pose term $J_q\Sigma_qJ_q^{\top}$ of \eqref{eq:res}, $\Sigma_q$ from its strip's spline without its own
depth. A view is \emph{flagged} if Holm rejects it in the round ($\alpha=0.05$, $M=1850$). The refinement fits
each strip's spline to the input poses and refines it in a pose graph with the GNSS/IMU prior
(0.3~m, 0.02$^{\circ}$), a second-difference smoothness prior (0.3~m, 0.02$^{\circ}$) and robust
render-and-compare depth factors with the per-view alignment; these settings, and the criteria (a detection
rate and a median removed fraction of at least 80\% at every level in range), were fixed before the run
(Supplement~\ref{S-sup:h7}).

Prediction (a) holds: the gate flagged every perturbed clean view at every level, 100\% in all nine levels
at or above 0.5$^{\circ}$ or 10~GSD, and also at the smallest sizes, 0.05$^{\circ}$ and 1~GSD, which are within the
GNSS/IMU prior (Figure~\ref{fig:h7}a--c, Table~\ref{tab:h7}). The size-0 control flagged none. The
sensitivity at small sizes is not the pose term at work. At 1~GSD, 0.2--0.7\% of a view's pixels see a
different surfel at the perturbed pose, at building edges of point-sampled exact depth, and these pixels
carry 97--99\% of the squared standardised residual; a real depth model smooths edges, so this sensitivity
should not be expected there. The perturbed views also swamped others: a single perturbed group led Holm to
reject on average 1.2 (0.05$^{\circ}$) to 27.5 (2$^{\circ}$ and 50~GSD) unperturbed views in the same round, at most 67.
The outer loop, which drops one view per round, was not run here.

Prediction (b) is falsified: no level up to 1$^{\circ}$ and 25~GSD reached a median of 80\% of the error
removed (Figure~\ref{fig:h7}d--f). The refinement removed 63--64\% of a rotation at every size and
38--80\% of a translation (79.6\% at 25~GSD, just below the target); with both, 59--64\% of the rotation
and 34--62\% of the translation. At 1$^{\circ}$ and 25~GSD the remaining error was 0.37$^{\circ}$ and 9.9~GSD
(medians). Blocks recovered less than isolated views (at 1$^{\circ}$ and 25~GSD: 35\% and 11\% against 66\%
and 67\%), because the shared perturbation also moves the spline, and in 17 of 192 cases the translation
error grew. The main cause is the GNSS/IMU prior of the perturbed frame, which holds the estimate near its
faulty input; in an exploratory variant that is not part of the prediction, the perturbed frame's prior
was dropped, as if the gate's flag marked that reading as faulty. The refinement then removed 85--89\% of
rotations of 0.5$^{\circ}$ and more, and 86\% of a 25~GSD translation, passing 4 of the 15 levels; small
perturbations stay below 80\% because the refinement leaves a floor: at size 0 it moves clean views by a median
of 0.005$^{\circ}$ and 0.4~GSD, and after a 0.05$^{\circ}$ rotation 0.02$^{\circ}$ remains. The faulty view, whose own
depth is wrong, was moved by 0.07$^{\circ}$ and 6.1~GSD when its pose was not perturbed.
