\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Gaussian splatting for geometry.}
3D Gaussian splatting represents a scene by anisotropic Gaussians rendered
with a visibility-aware rasteriser \cite{gs_3dgs}. 2DGS collapses the primitives
into oriented planar disks with depth-distortion and normal-consistency terms
for surface accuracy \cite{gs_2dgs}. 3DGS-MCMC treats the Gaussians as MCMC
samples and replaces densification and pruning by relocation, which allows a
fixed primitive budget \cite{gs_mcmc}; we use this to keep the primitive count
fixed. Depth-driven variants include dense RGB-D SLAM with explicit Gaussians
\cite{gs_splatam}, Gaussians embedded in a volumetric (TSDF) mapping system
\cite{gs_gsfusion}, and depth and normal priors for indoor scenes
\cite{gs_dnsplatter}. All of these assume a single, trusted depth source.

\paragraph{Robust and uncertainty-aware splatting.}
SpotLessSplats uses pre-trained features and robust optimisation to ignore
transient distractors \cite{gs_spotless}; RobustSplat delays Gaussian growth
and bootstraps transient masks at several scales \cite{gs_robustsplat}. Both act
on photometric evidence and decide per pixel or per region. Uncertainty-aware
mapping weights the loss by learned or modelled uncertainty: UncLe-SLAM
learns pixel-wise depth uncertainty in dense neural SLAM
\cite{gs_multisensor_implicit}, and BayesianGS-SLAM estimates colour and depth
predictive uncertainty from sensor noise and opacity to weight tracking
residuals and select keyframes \cite{gs_bayesiangsslam}. These methods weight
measurements from one sensor stream; they do not test whole sensors against one
another.

\paragraph{Splatting with several sensors.}
ToF-Splatting merges sparse time-of-flight depth, monocular colour and
multi-view geometry into dense depth for splatting-based SLAM
\cite{gs_tofsplatting}. 3DGS-Calib uses Gaussian splatting for targetless
multimodal spatiotemporal calibration \cite{gs_3dgscalib}; it estimates
extrinsics and time offsets rather than detecting that a sensor has become
unreliable. DROID-Splat pairs a learned tracker with a splatting renderer
\cite{gs_droidsplat}. YOGO describes a multi-sensor registration protocol
alongside a resource controller \cite{gs_yogo}.

\paragraph{Depth-map and volumetric fusion.}
Volumetric fusion averages truncated signed distances weighted per
measurement \cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion}, with efficient
implementations such as VDBFusion \cite{fus_vdbfusion}. Robustness has been added
through visibility reasoning that rejects measurements contradicted by free
space seen from other views \cite{fus_merrell}, $L^1$ data terms with total
variation regularisation \cite{fus_zach07}, and probabilistic inlier models
\cite{fus_psdf,fus_vogiatzis}. In multi-view stereo, depth maps from
PatchMatch-style estimators \cite{fus_patchmatch,fus_shen,fus_gipuma,fus_acmm}
are filtered by geometric consistency across neighbouring views before fusion
\cite{fus_colmap_mvs,fus_colmap_sfm}; OpenMVS provides such a fusion stage as
open-source software \cite{fus_openmvs}. All of these decide per pixel or per
voxel, from the support of neighbouring measurements.

\paragraph{Learned multi-sensor fusion.}
RoutedFusion and NeuralFusion learn the fusion update for a single depth
stream, in the TSDF and in a latent feature space respectively
\cite{fus_routedfusion,fus_neuralfusion}. SenFuNet extends learned fusion to
several depth sensors with learned sensor-specific noise and outlier behaviour
\cite{fus_senfunet}, and V-FUSE learns to fuse multi-view-stereo depth and
confidence maps with visibility constraints \cite{fus_vfuse}. Learned
weights are tied to the fault statistics seen in training; whether a smooth
bias or a drifted extrinsic is down-weighted is an empirical question we test (H5).

\paragraph{Sensor fault detection and exclusion.}
Excluding faulty sources is a classical problem. Byzantine fault tolerance
\cite{flt_lamport}, interval-based fusion of continuous-valued sensors
\cite{flt_marzullo,flt_brooksiyengar}, and receiver autonomous integrity
monitoring (RAIM) with residual-based, solution-separation and multiple-hypothesis
tests \cite{flt_raim_parkinson,flt_raim_brown,flt_raim_joerger,flt_raim_pervan}
detect and exclude a faulty source from a redundant set; random-sample-consensus
methods select a consistent subset \cite{flt_ransac,flt_msac}; robust estimators
bound the influence of outliers \cite{flt_huber}. Ambarkutuk \emph{et~al.}\
\cite{flt_ambarkutuk} bring Byzantine sensor elimination to vibration-based
localisation by building a consensus of sensor PDFs under an entropy criterion;
we adapt the consensus idea to dense depth fusion and correct the criterion
(Section~\ref{sec:method:admission}). To our knowledge, dense depth fusion with a
renderable surface consensus and exclusion of whole depth sensors has not been
studied in this setting; this statement rests on a limited literature search
and will be re-checked before submission.

\paragraph{Sensor noise models.}
Range noise of structured-light and stereo sensors grows quadratically with
distance \cite{noise_khoshelham,noise_nguyen,noise_matthies}; time-of-flight
noise depends on signal amplitude and suffers from multipath and flying pixels
\cite{noise_lange,noise_sarbolandi,noise_freedman}. We use these models to set the
variance in Section~\ref{sec:method:noise}.
