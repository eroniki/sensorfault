\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\runin{Robust and uncertainty-aware reconstruction.} Robust losses \cite{rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron}
and distractor-aware methods \cite{gs_robustnerf,gs_spotless,gs_robustsplat} down-weight pixels whose
residual, mostly photometric, looks like an outlier; multi-view consistency filters test pixels by
correspondence \cite{fus_merrell,fus_colmap_mvs}. FisherRF uses the Fisher information of radiance-field
parameters for view selection \cite{unc_fisherrf}, and Bayes' Rays fits a post-hoc Laplace uncertainty to a
trained NeRF \cite{unc_bayesrays}. These methods weight pixels or quantify uncertainty; none decides on a
whole view at a stated false-alarm rate. We render a per-primitive uncertainty into depth variance and use
it to test views, and H6 compares both uncertainties inside the same test.

\runin{Surfels, splatting and monocular priors.} Point-based fusion keeps surfels with a normal, a radius
and a confidence, and fuses depth by projective association \cite{fus_keller_surfels}. Gaussian splatting
renders primitives with a differentiable rasteriser \cite{gs_3dgs,gs_gsplat}; 2DGS uses planar Gaussians
for accurate surfaces \cite{gs_2dgs}; monocular depth and normal priors supervise splatting and implicit
surfaces after per-view alignment \cite{gs_dnsplatter,rec_monosdf}. We fuse through the rasteriser's own
Jacobians rather than by association, and keep the uncertainty apart from the rendering shape.

\runin{Testing and message passing.} Data snooping, its reliability theory and the DIA procedure test a
linear Gaussian model with nuisance parameters for misspecified observations and give a minimum detectable
bias \cite{stat_teunissen_dia}; in satellite navigation, solution separation equals the residual-based test
\cite{flt_raim_joerger}. Removing one source's Gaussian message is the cavity step of expectation
propagation \cite{mp_minka_ep}, and Gaussian belief propagation has been proposed for spatial AI
\cite{mp_davison_gbp}. Our gate is an instance of this testing theory in which the observations are
pixels, the nuisance is a per-view scale and offset, and the leave-one-out solution is a rendered surface.
A longer review is in Supplement~\ref{S-sup:related}.
