\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\runin{Robust and uncertainty-aware reconstruction.} Robust losses \cite{rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron} and
distractor-aware methods \cite{gs_robustnerf,gs_spotless,gs_robustsplat} down-weight outlying pixels;
multi-view filters test pixels by correspondence \cite{fus_merrell,fus_colmap_mvs}. FisherRF
\cite{unc_fisherrf} and Bayes' Rays \cite{unc_bayesrays} quantify radiance-field uncertainty. None decides
on a whole view at a stated false-alarm rate; H6 compares their uncertainties inside our test.

\runin{Surfels and splatting.} Point-based fusion associates depth to surfels projectively
\cite{fus_keller_surfels}; Gaussian splatting uses a differentiable rasteriser \cite{gs_3dgs,gs_gsplat},
2DGS planar Gaussians \cite{gs_2dgs}, and monocular priors are aligned per view \cite{gs_dnsplatter,rec_monosdf}.
We fuse through the rasteriser's Jacobians and keep the uncertainty apart from the rendering shape.

\runin{Testing and message passing.} Data snooping and the DIA procedure test a linear Gaussian model for
misspecified observations \cite{stat_teunissen_dia}; solution separation is equivalent
\cite{flt_raim_joerger}; removing a Gaussian message is the cavity step of expectation propagation
\cite{mp_minka_ep}, and Gaussian belief propagation serves spatial AI \cite{mp_davison_gbp}. Our gate is
such a test with pixels as observations and a rendered surface as the leave-one-out solution
(Supplement~\ref{S-sup:related}).
