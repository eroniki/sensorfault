\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

All experiments use public datasets, public sensor models and public code. The
protocol, hypotheses and decision thresholds are written down and tagged in the
code repository before any test scene is run (Section~\ref{sec:setup:protocol}).

\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:setup:data}
\paragraph{Skoltech3D (primary real data).} Skoltech3D \cite{data_skoltech3d}
provides several real depth sensors observing the same tabletop objects
(two phone ToF cameras, a Kinect-class ToF camera, an active-stereo RealSense
camera), per-sensor depth and calibration, and a structured-light reference
scan. Faults are injected on top of the calibrated, otherwise clean data, so
the true fault is known exactly. Scenes are small, single-object scenes without
room-scale multipath, and frames are independent viewpoints; ``time'' is defined by
viewpoint order. Data are used under their non-commercial licence and are not
redistributed. \TODO{Fix the scene subset and the dev/test split after the

\paragraph{Replica (simulated multi-sensor rig).} We render the same trajectories
in Replica \cite{data_replica} with per-sensor intrinsics, field of view,
resolution and rig extrinsics, and apply per-sensor noise models
(Section~\ref{sec:form:sensor}). Exact ground-truth geometry and concave room
corners make this the testbed for multipath and grazing-angle effects. Its terms
do not clearly permit redistributing renderings, so only the generator,
seeds and configurations are released.

\paragraph{Synthetic planes and rooms.} A generator in the code repository
produces box rooms with an obstacle, with no dataset required. It is used for unit
tests and for the controlled sweeps over overlap and coverage share.

\subsection{Fault injection}
\label{sec:setup:faults}
The fault classes are defined in Section~\ref{sec:form:faults}; this subsection fixes
the grid. Faults are injected by deterministic, seeded scripts, so every realisation is
reproducible from a manifest. Seeds are derived as
$\mathrm{SeedSequence}([\text{master},\,h(\text{sensor id}),\,\text{fault id}])$
with a stable hash and at least five master seeds. The grid is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{extrinsic drift:} rotation $\{0.5,1,2,5\}^{\circ}$ and
        translation $\{1,2.5,5,10\}$~cm, applied as a step, a linear ramp or a
        random walk (rotation-only and translation-only cells);
  \item \emph{depth bias:} additive, $1$--$5\%$ of the median range, and
        range-proportional, $\beta\in\{1,2,3,5\}\%$ of both signs;
  \item \emph{multipath surrogate:} a positive bias near concave regions,
        amplitude $2$--$10$~cm (tabletop) or $5$--$30$~cm (rooms), clamped to be
        non-negative. This is a controlled heuristic, not a physical multipath
        model;
  \item \emph{persistence:} sensor-level persistent faults (onset at 20\% or 40\%
        of the sequence) versus frame-level transient bursts (10\% or 30\% of
        frames in bursts of 3--10), reported separately;
  \item \emph{fraction faulted:} 1 of 3, 1 of 4 and 2 of 4 sensors (independent
        and same-direction), always with a fault-free control, sweeping which
        sensor is faulted.
\end{itemize}
Overlap fraction, the faulty sensor's coverage share and fault correlation across
sensors are controlled variables (Section~\ref{sec:form:regimes}).

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:setup:baselines}
Every method receives the same fault-injected depth maps, the same (possibly
drifted) poses and intrinsics, the same view graph, the same confidence (or
none), and the same fault realisations. Thresholds are tuned on development
scenes only, with the tuning budget reported. Re-implementations are labelled
as such and released.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Family A: OpenMVS depth-map fusion.} The fusion stage of
        OpenMVS \cite{fus_openmvs}, run on externally supplied depth maps
        (PatchMatch skipped): merge-all as the no-filter floor, the per-pixel
        consistency vote (relative depth and normal agreement in at least $n$
        views, with a free-space filter), and its newer fusion mode as a separate
        variant. We sweep the number of views to fuse and the thresholds.
  \item \textbf{Family B: classical fusion.} Weighted-average TSDF
        \cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion}; per-pixel median across sensors;
        geometric-consistency voting in the style of COLMAP
        \cite{fus_colmap_mvs}; visibility-based fusion \cite{fus_merrell}; TV-$L^1$
        range integration \cite{fus_zach07}; and optionally a
        probabilistic inlier-ratio method \cite{fus_psdf,fus_vogiatzis}.
  \item \textbf{Family C: learned fusion.} SenFuNet \cite{fus_senfunet}
        (pretrained, fine-tuned on a fault-free split, and fine-tuned with
        fault-like bias) and one single-sensor learned fuser
        \cite{fus_routedfusion,fus_neuralfusion}.
  \item \textbf{Family D: sensor-level elimination.} Iterated
        leave-one-sensor-out normalised-residual test, enumerated subset
        consensus with a MSAC score \cite{flt_ransac,flt_msac}, an oracle that
        knows the faulty sensor, and an estimate-and-correct baseline that
        jointly optimises extrinsics and bias.
  \item \textbf{Secondary, same representation.} 3DGS-MCMC \cite{gs_mcmc} and
        2DGS \cite{gs_2dgs} with a depth loss and the same $N$. These need RGB as
        shipped; we report a depth-only variant with placeholder colour and, separately,
        an RGB-available variant. Photometric transient-masking methods
        \cite{gs_spotless,gs_robustsplat} are not run, since their masks are
        photometric and do not target depth faults; the depth-space analogue is
        the robust per-pixel mask baseline.
\end{itemize}
OpenMVS is an offline batch tool and does not meet a per-frame real-time budget.
We report latency for every method and state which ones are accuracy baselines
only.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}
\label{sec:setup:metrics}
Reconstruction quality is reported as accuracy, completeness, Chamfer distance and
F-score against the reference geometry (F-score thresholds $1/2/5$~mm for
Skoltech3D, $2/5/10$~cm for Replica); accuracy and completeness are reported
separately, because excluding a sensor lowers completeness whenever the
reference includes surfaces that only that sensor observed. Detection is
reported as per-sensor precision and recall, time-to-detect (median and
90th percentile, right-censored), and false alarms per 100 clean frames. The
\emph{elimination curve AUC} is the normalised partial area under power versus
clean-run false-elimination rate on $[0,0.2]$; it equals the ROC AUC at
$\mathrm{FER}_{\max}=1$. Intervals are scene-level bootstrap confidence
intervals, with paired Wilcoxon tests against baselines and scenes as the unit.

\subsection{Hypotheses and breakdown scenarios}
\label{sec:setup:hyp}
The following predictions are pre-registered. Each states what would falsify it.
\begin{description}
  \item[H1 (coherent faults).] With $K\ge 3$ and one coherent fault of at least
        $3\sigma$, sensor-level elimination reduces Chamfer error by at least 25\%
        relative to the best tuned per-pixel baseline (OpenMVS consistency
        fusion, COLMAP-style voting, robust masking) and lands within 20\% of
        the oracle. The advantage is concentrated in low-overlap,
        faulty-dominant and sub-noise regimes; with high overlap and large $K$
        voting is expected to tie, and the tie is reported.
        \emph{Falsified if} the paired confidence interval of the improvement
        includes zero in those regimes.
  \item[H2 (entropy vs.\ chi-square).] At a fixed 5\% false-elimination rate, the
        evidence-based criterion exceeds the leave-one-out chi-square test by at
        least 10 points of detection power, only where heteroscedastic noise,
        grazing geometry, occlusion or non-Gaussian residuals break the
        chi-square assumptions, and ties under idealised Gaussian noise
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:loo}). \emph{Falsified if} there is no gain in
        any pre-registered regime; the fallback is to report the method as
        sensor-level exclusion with visibility and grazing weighting, with the
        entropy relation as an equivalence result.
  \item[H3 (visibility and grazing).] Visibility-aware residuals and grazing
        weighting keep the clean-run false-elimination rate at or below 5\%;
        ablating either at least doubles it. \emph{Falsified if} ablations leave
        the rate and error unchanged.
  \item[H4 (fixed $N$).] Fixed-$N$ MCMC resists absorbing the faulty sensor's
        surface: with adaptive densification at matched final $N$, detection
        power drops by at least 10 points, and there is a sweet spot in $N$.
        \emph{Falsified if} densification matches fixed-$N$ or the $N$ sweep
        is flat.
  \item[H5 (learned fusion).] Pretrained SenFuNet and single-sensor learned
        fusion degrade under smooth bias and extrinsic drift, because they
        were trained on zero-mean noise and outliers; fine-tuning SenFuNet with
        fault-like bias narrows but does not close the gap in the low-overlap,
        faulty-dominant regimes. \emph{Falsified if} the fine-tuned model matches
        elimination there; we report it either way.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Breakdown scenarios.} The following are tested and reported as honest
negatives: a majority of faulty sensors with correlated (same-sign) faults;
common-mode faults (a shared extrinsic or clock); $K=2$, where attribution
is unidentifiable ($\approx$50\% attribution accuracy) and only detection is
possible; sub-noise faults below a detection floor; sparse independent outliers,
where per-pixel methods should win or tie; and non-overlapping fields of view.

\subsection{Protocol and implementation}
\label{sec:setup:protocol}
Hypotheses, thresholds, metrics, the tuning protocol and the dev/test split are
fixed in a preregistration file and tagged in version control before any test
scene is processed; the runner refuses the test split without that tag. The
elimination threshold is calibrated by permutation on clean dev runs to a 5\%
per-run false-elimination rate. At least six scenes (three dev, three test) and
five seeds are used per dataset. Code, configurations, seeds and fault manifests
are released; restricted datasets are not redistributed.
