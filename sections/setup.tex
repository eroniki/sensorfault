\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:setup}

The experiments evaluate whether the view-level gate improves trajectory and fused-surface
accuracy when some depth sources are wrong in a coherent, view-level way, whether it
beats the three baseline families that act at other units and stages, and where it
should not help. All experiments use public datasets, public pretrained models and public
code. The protocol, hypotheses and decision thresholds are written down and tagged in the
code repository before any test scene is run (Section~\ref{sec:setup:protocol}). Below
are the details of the experiments employed in this study.

\subsubsection{Fault injection}
\label{sec:setup:faults}
The fault classes are defined in Section~\ref{sec:method:defs}; this subsection fixes the
grid. Faults are injected into the output of one estimator, by deterministic, seeded
scripts, so every realisation is reproducible from a manifest. Seeds are derived as
$\mathrm{SeedSequence}([\text{master},\,h(\text{source id}),\,\text{fault id}])$ with a
stable hash and at least five master seeds. The grid is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{smooth warp:} a random low-frequency field added to the inverse depth,
        with amplitude $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ times the standard deviation of the
        inverse depth of the view;
  \item \emph{region failure:} a connected region covering $20$--$40\%$ of the pixels is
        replaced by a plane fitted to the whole view or by a blurred copy;
  \item \emph{non-affine distortion:} $D'=D^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma\in\{0.8,0.9,1.1,1.25\}$;
  \item \emph{persistence:} a fraction $p\in\{10,20,40,60\}\%$ of the views of the faulted
        estimator, scattered at random or clustered in contiguous views, and
        estimator-level faults on all views;
  \item \emph{faulted estimators per view:} 1 or 2 of $M$, with independent parameters and,
        separately, identical parameters (common mode);
  \item \emph{controls:} sparse independent outliers at the same corrupted-pixel rate, and
        fault-free runs.
\end{itemize}
Natural failures need no injection: sources are labelled faulty when, after robust affine
alignment, their error against the laser reference exceeds a threshold fixed on
development scenes. \TODO{Fix the labelling threshold on development scenes.}
Overlap between views and the clustering of faulty views are controlled variables, because
a pixel filter or a kernel does not fail merely because a fault is coherent: it is
expected to fail when faulty views have few neighbours, when neighbouring views fail
together, or when the fault is below the per-pixel noise but detectable by aggregating
over many pixels (challenge C1).

\subsubsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:setup:baselines}
Every method receives the same images, matches, depth ensemble, fault realisations and
pose-graph solver; thresholds are tuned on development scenes only, with the tuning
budget reported. Re-implementations are labelled as such and released.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Robust kernels in the pose graph (no gate).} Huber, Cauchy and
        Geman--McClure kernels \cite{flt_huber,rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron,rob_mactavish},
        graduated non-convexity \cite{rob_gnc}, switchable constraints \cite{rob_switchable}
        and dynamic covariance scaling \cite{rob_dcs}, applied to the geometric and the
        depth constraints.
  \item \textbf{COLMAP geometric consistency filter.} The pixel-wise cross-view depth and
        reprojection consistency test of COLMAP \cite{fus_colmap_mvs}, run with the same
        poses, masking depth constraints and fusion pixel by pixel.
  \item \textbf{Plain voting.} Per-pixel median and majority across the ensemble and
        across neighbouring views, with no gate.
  \item \textbf{No gate, no robust kernel;} and an \textbf{oracle gate} that excludes
        exactly the faulty sources (from the injection manifest, or from the laser
        reference for natural failures), as an upper bound.
  \item \textbf{Gate variants:} a leave-one-source-out chi-square gate (the statistic
        $T_k$ of \eqref{eq:Ts} with a calibrated threshold) and the evidence gate of
        Section~\ref{sec:method:admission}; and, as a context for the choice of
        exclusion over correction, an estimate-and-correct baseline that refits the
        alignment of every source without excluding any.
  \item \textbf{Reference points, not rivals.} MASt3R-SfM \cite{rec_mast3rsfm} and VGGT
        \cite{rec_vggt}, run as released on the same images, with classical COLMAP SfM
        \cite{fus_colmap_sfm} as a classical reference. They do not use our depth
        sources and therefore do not see the injected faults; they are reported to place
        the test bed in context, and no claim is made to beat them.
\end{itemize}
Every method is timed. The test bed is an offline pipeline, and we report latency and memory
per stage and state which methods do not meet a per-frame real-time budget.

\subsubsection{Metrics and statistics}
\label{sec:setup:metrics}
Pose quality is reported as absolute trajectory error after similarity alignment, relative
pose error, and the area under the curve of the relative pose error at fixed angular
thresholds. Fused-surface quality is reported as accuracy, completeness and F-score
against the laser reference; accuracy and completeness are reported separately, because
excluding a source lowers completeness wherever only that source saw a region. Depth
quality is the absolute relative error and the fraction within a ratio of the reference
after alignment. The gate is scored per source: precision and recall against the oracle
labels, the false-exclusion rate on fault-free data, power at a fixed 5\% false-exclusion
rate, and the \emph{elimination curve AUC}, the normalised partial area under power versus
clean-run false-exclusion rate on $[0,0.2]$ (it equals the ROC AUC at
$\mathrm{FER}_{\max}=1$). Intervals are scene-level bootstrap confidence intervals, with
paired Wilcoxon tests against baselines and scenes as the unit.

\subsection{Hypotheses and Breakdown Scenarios}
\label{sec:setup:hyp}
The following predictions are pre-registered. Each states what would falsify it.
\begin{description}
  \item[H1 (view-level gate versus the three baselines).] With $M\ge3$ and one estimator
        corrupted by a view-level fault on a fraction of the views, the gated pipeline
        reduces the absolute trajectory error and the fused-surface error by at least 25\%
        relative to the best tuned of the robust kernels, the COLMAP consistency filter
        and plain voting, and lands within 20\% of the oracle gate. The advantage is
        concentrated where faulty views are clustered or have few neighbours and where
        the fault is smooth rather than sparse; with sparse independent outliers the pixel
        filters and kernels are expected to tie or win, and that tie is reported.
        \emph{Falsified if} the paired confidence interval of the improvement includes zero
        in those regimes.
  \item[H2 (evidence criterion versus chi-square).] At a fixed 5\% false-exclusion rate,
        the evidence criterion exceeds the leave-one-source-out chi-square gate by at
        least 10 points of detection power, only where heteroscedastic depth noise,
        grazing geometry, occlusion or non-Gaussian residuals break the chi-square
        assumptions, and ties under idealised Gaussian noise (Proposition~\ref{prop:loo}).
        \emph{Falsified if} there is no gain in any pre-registered regime; the fallback is
        to report the gate with the chi-square statistic, with the entropy relation as an
        equivalence result.
  \item[H3 (alignment and visibility).] Fitting the alignment without the source under test
        and comparing only on pixels visible in both views keeps the false-exclusion rate
        on clean data at or below 5\%; fitting the alignment jointly lets the fit absorb
        the fault and lowers detection power by at least 10 points, and removing the
        visibility mask at least doubles the false-exclusion rate. \emph{Falsified if}
        the ablations leave the rate and the power unchanged.
  \item[H4 (placement and complementarity).] Gating before pose graph optimisation gives
        lower trajectory error than applying the same statistic after the graph, and the
        gate combined with a robust kernel is at least as accurate as either alone.
        \emph{Falsified if} gating after the graph matches gating before it, or if the
        combination is worse than the better component.
  \item[H5 (cost on clean data and reference points).] On fault-free data the gate changes
        trajectory and fused-surface error by at most 5\% relative to the pipeline
        without it (the price of gating); on natural failures the gated test bed narrows
        the gap to the reference points MASt3R-SfM and VGGT, which are reported as context.
        \emph{Falsified if} the price of gating on clean data exceeds 5\%.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Breakdown scenarios.} The following are tested and reported as honest
negatives: a majority of estimators failing in the same way on a view (common-mode
failure, expected because estimators share training data); $M=2$, where attribution is
unidentifiable and the gate returns no consensus; faults below the detection floor;
sparse independent outliers, where pixel methods should win or tie; views with too little
overlap for a cross-view comparison; and provisional poses of poor quality.

\subsection{Implementation}
\label{sec:setup:protocol}
Hypotheses, thresholds, metrics, the tuning protocol and the dev/test split are fixed in a
preregistration file and tagged in version control before any test scene is processed;
the runner refuses the test split without that tag. The gate threshold is calibrated by
permutation on clean development runs to a 5\% per-view false-exclusion rate. At least six
scenes (three development, three test) and five seeds are used per dataset. The
test bed uses an off-the-shelf feature matcher, a Gauss--Newton or Levenberg--Marquardt
pose-graph solver \cite{opt_g2o,opt_factorgraphs,opt_theseus}, and TSDF fusion
\cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion}. Code, configurations, seeds and fault manifests are
released; restricted datasets are not redistributed.
