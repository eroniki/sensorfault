\section{Related Work, Challenges and Baseline Study}
\label{sup:related}
\subsection{Relevant Literature}
\label{sec:intro:lit}
\runin{Integrity monitoring and fault detection and exclusion.}
In satellite navigation, a consistency test on the residuals of redundant ranges detects a
fault when its statistic exceeds a threshold set by the allowed false-alarm probability
\cite{flt_raim_parkinson,flt_raim_sturza,flt_raim_brown}; the smallest fault that is detected
with a given probability of missed detection is the minimum detectable bias, and the
\emph{protection level} bounds the position error that an undetected fault of that size can
cause. Solution-separation and multiple-hypothesis tests compare the solution with and without
each measurement, which identifies the faulty one so that it can be excluded
\cite{flt_raim_brenner,flt_raim_pervan,flt_raim_joerger}, and advanced RAIM extends this to
several constellations and to protection levels after exclusion
\cite{flt_araim_baseline,flt_araim_pl_exclusion}. Integrity monitoring has been applied to
LiDAR-based navigation, to detect unwanted objects \cite{flt_integrity_lidar}. Byzantine fault
tolerance \cite{flt_lamport}, interval-based fusion of continuous-valued sensors
\cite{flt_marzullo,flt_brooksiyengar} and random-sample-consensus methods
\cite{flt_ransac,flt_msac} select a consistent subset of sources. In our earlier work we
eliminated Byzantine accelerometers from a vibration-based localisation by building a
consensus of per-sensor PDFs \cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, and used a joint likelihood over several
sensors for dispersive wave propagation \cite{flt_ambarkutuk2024}. Testing a unit against a
model that was fitted without it is the idea behind cross-validation and the jackknife; the
gate applies it to depth sources, with a Gaussian surface as the model, for which leaving a view out is exact. To our knowledge,
integrity monitoring of this kind has not been applied to the views of a learned-depth
reconstruction; this statement rests on a limited literature search.

\runin{Monocular depth estimators.}
MiDaS learns depth that is invariant to range and scale by mixing datasets
\cite{est_midas}; Depth Anything V2 reports finer and more robust predictions from
synthetic labels and pseudo-labelled real images \cite{est_dav2}. Metric3D v2,
UniDepth and Depth Pro target zero-shot metric depth \cite{est_metric3dv2,est_unidepth,est_depthpro};
MoGe predicts an affine-invariant point map \cite{est_moge}, and Marigold repurposes
diffusion-based image generators for depth \cite{est_marigold}. Outputs therefore differ
in whether they carry metric scale, and none of these works states when a prediction
should not be used for downstream geometry. Ensembles are a standard route to
predictive uncertainty \cite{ens_deepensembles}; here an ensemble of models appears only
as an ablation, because models trained on overlapping data may fail on the same views.

\runin{Gaussian splatting as a surface.}
3D Gaussian splatting represents a scene by anisotropic Gaussians rendered with a
visibility-aware rasteriser \cite{gs_3dgs}; 2D Gaussian splatting uses oriented planar
disks with depth-distortion and normal-consistency terms for surface accuracy
\cite{gs_2dgs}; 3DGS-MCMC treats the Gaussians as MCMC samples and replaces densification
by relocation, which allows a fixed primitive budget \cite{gs_mcmc}; we use it only as an
ablation of our closed-form surface. DN-Splatter adds
depth and normal priors, including monocular ones, to splatting of indoor scenes
\cite{gs_dnsplatter}. SpotLessSplats and RobustSplat mask transient distractors from
photometric evidence \cite{gs_spotless,gs_robustsplat}. These fit a surface to given
supervision and, at most, mask pixels or regions of it; none uses the rendered surface as
the reference against which whole depth sources are admitted or rejected.

\runin{SfM, view graphs and multi-view consistency.}
Incremental and global SfM estimate poses from feature matches
\cite{fus_colmap_sfm,sfm_glomap}; view-graph filtering removes inconsistent
pairwise constraints before global estimation \cite{sfm_1dsfm}. In multi-view stereo, a
depth map is kept pixel by pixel when enough neighbouring views agree on depth and
normal \cite{fus_colmap_mvs}, or when other views do not see free space in front of it
\cite{fus_merrell}, and surviving depth is fused volumetrically
\cite{fus_curless,fus_kinectfusion}. These filters decide per pixel.

\runin{Robust pose graph optimisation.}
Pose graph and factor-graph solvers \cite{opt_g2o,opt_factorgraphs} are made robust to
bad constraints with robust cost functions \cite{flt_huber,rob_blackrangarajan,rob_barron,rob_mactavish},
graduated non-convexity \cite{rob_gnc}, switchable constraints \cite{rob_switchable},
dynamic covariance scaling \cite{rob_dcs} and max-mixture models \cite{rob_maxmix}; the last
three were designed for false loop closures. These act on the residual of each constraint,
after the constraint has been built from the depth map or the match, and down-weight it
without a statement about trust.

\runin{Learned reconstruction and differentiable optimisation.}
DUSt3R reconstructs image collections without calibration or poses
\cite{rec_dust3r}; MASt3R casts matching as a 3D task \cite{rec_mast3r}, and MASt3R-SfM
builds a fully integrated SfM solution on it \cite{rec_mast3rsfm}. VGGT is a
feed-forward network that infers camera parameters, point maps, depth maps and tracks
from one to hundreds of views \cite{rec_vggt}. DROID-SLAM updates poses and pixelwise
depth through a dense bundle-adjustment layer \cite{rec_droidslam}, and Theseus provides
differentiable nonlinear least squares on PyTorch \cite{opt_theseus}. We use MASt3R-SfM and
VGGT as reference points rather than rivals: they replace the pipeline, whereas the
gate is a component that can precede one.

\runin{Positioning.}
Pixel filters, robust kernels and voting decide at a different unit and stage from the
gate: pixel filters per pixel and after poses are fixed, kernels per constraint and after
the constraint exists, voting per pixel across views (or, in the ablation, across models), and
none of them excludes a view or states that the output is not trustworthy. A coherent
view-level fault is neither sparse nor independent across pixels, so none of these is
designed to attribute it to a view, and comparing every view with every overlapping view makes
the check cost grow with the number of view pairs.

\subsection{Challenges in Monitoring the Integrity of Monocular Depth}
\label{sec:intro:challenges}
Several challenges limit current approaches.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[\textbf{C1.}] \textbf{Coherent faults are sub-noise per pixel.} A fault that moves a
        view coherently (a depth bias or warp over part of it, a pose prior error, a false loop
        closure) adds a small shift to many pixels, so each pixel stays within its noise and a
        per-pixel filter has almost no power. The evidence is in the aggregate over the view, and
        its strength depends on the number of effectively independent pixels, on the fraction of
        the view that is affected, and on what the scale and offset of the monocular depth can
        explain (Section~\ref{sec:method:detection}).
  \item[\textbf{C2.}] \textbf{Down-weighting gives no trust statement.} Robust kernels and soft
        view weights reduce the influence of a constraint with a large residual; they do not
        exclude the view, do not state a false-alarm or missed-detection probability, and do not
        tell the user when the output should not be relied on. An integrity monitor needs
        detection, exclusion and an alarm.
  \item[\textbf{C3.}] \textbf{Monocular depth has no exact reference scale, and its errors
        are not iid Gaussian.} Even metric models are only approximately metric, and some outputs
        are affine-invariant, so a view's depth can be compared with the others only after fitting
        a scale and shift. That fit can absorb part of a fault and make the faulty view look
        consistent, so the comparison must be made against a reference, and after an alignment,
        that were fitted without the view under test. The residual is also heteroscedastic and
        spatially correlated, and pose, intrinsics and surface errors add to it, so the test must
        be derived from an explicit covariance and not from one iid Gaussian $\sigma$.
  \item[\textbf{C4.}] \textbf{A single model gives no redundancy within a view.} Only
        other views can expose a failure, and an ensemble does not obviously help: models
        trained on overlapping data may fail on the same views, so a majority of an
        ensemble can be wrong together. A gate must therefore be able to answer \emph{no
        consensus}, for example when too few other views observe the region.
  \item[\textbf{C5.}] \textbf{Disagreement is not always a fault.} Occlusion, low
        overlap and moving content make views disagree for reasons unrelated to
        the quality of the depth, so residuals must be compared only on pixels that are
        visible at the view under test and supported by the reference.
  \item[\textbf{C6.}] \textbf{The consensus criterion must detect disagreement.} A
        criterion that rewards the precision of the combined estimate, as the entropy
        test of our earlier work does as published, cannot distinguish an agreeing
        source from a sharp but biased one.
  \item[\textbf{C7.}] \textbf{Pairwise checks do not scale with overlap.} Comparing every
        view with every overlapping view costs in proportion to the number of view
        pairs, which grows faster than the number of views when overlap is dense. A
        single shared surface avoids this, but a surface fitted to the view under
        test would absorb its fault, so it must be fitted without that view and must not
        be free to grow a second layer.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Baseline Study and Overview of the Fundamental Differences}
\label{sec:intro:baseline}
The gate must beat three families of baselines, which act at different units and
stages (Table~\ref{tab:diff}): robust kernels in the pose graph (with soft per-view
down-weighting inside the differentiable optimisation as their closest relative), the
geometric consistency filter of COLMAP \cite{fus_colmap_mvs}, and plain voting.
Cross-model voting is studied separately, in the ensemble ablation. MASt3R-SfM and
VGGT are reference points. The consensus criterion adapted for the gate comes from our
earlier Byzantine sensor elimination (BSE) \cite{flt_ambarkutuk}, which localised
footsteps from the signal energies of floor-mounted accelerometers: each sensor gave a
PDF of the location over a grid, the PDFs were multiplied, and a consensus set was grown
greedily from the best pair under an entropy criterion. Here the sources are views, the
PDF of a source is a per-pixel depth distribution, the consensus is a rendered Gaussian
surface instead of a product of source PDFs, and the admission test is corrected
(Section~\ref{sec:method:admission}); the earlier set-growing consensus survives only in
the pairwise comparison variant (Appendix~\ref{app:pairwise}). The cost of the pairwise
variant, and of the shared surface, is analysed in Section~\ref{sec:method:cost}.
