\section{Limitations and Failure Conditions}
\label{sec:discussion}

The method is built on a consensus of sensors and fails where the consensus
is wrong or not identifiable. First, it assumes fewer than half of the sensors are
faulty. When faults are correlated across sensors, or a shared extrinsic or
clock is wrong (common-mode faults), the faulty sensors form the largest mutually
consistent set and the healthy ones can be excluded; in these cases it is
expected to be no better than fusing everything. Second, with $K=2$ the
residuals are symmetric and attribution is not identifiable; only detection is
possible. Third, faults below the per-pixel noise can be detected only by
aggregating over many pixels, so there is a detection floor that depends on
overlap and pixel count. Fourth, leave-one-out refits cost compute, and
exclusion reduces completeness wherever a sensor observed surfaces no other
sensor saw. Fifth, the faults in our protocol are controlled surrogates; in
particular the multipath surrogate is a heuristic, and real multipath depends on
surface reflectance and modulation. Sixth, the Gaussian assumptions behind the
variance models fail near grazing incidence, which we handle by clamping and
gating rather than by modelling. Finally, results on tabletop objects with
injected faults need not transfer to large scenes with natural faults, for which
