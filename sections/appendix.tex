\section{Pose Jacobian and the Residual Variance}
\label{app:variance}
Let a pixel ray with unit direction $\boldsymbol\ell$ meet a locally planar surface with unit
normal $\boldsymbol\nu$ (oriented so that $\boldsymbol\nu\cdot\boldsymbol\ell>0$) at depth $r$ along
the ray, with incidence angle $\theta$, $\cos\theta=\boldsymbol\nu\cdot\boldsymbol\ell$. For a perturbation of
the camera pose by a translation $\delta\mathbf{t}$ and a rotation $\delta\boldsymbol\omega$, a point
in the old camera frame has the new coordinates $\mathbf{x}'\approx\mathbf{x}-\delta\mathbf{t}-\delta\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf{x}$.
The plane $\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}\mathbf{x}=c$ becomes, to first order,
$\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}\mathbf{x}'+\delta\boldsymbol\omega^{\top}(\mathbf{x}'\times\boldsymbol\nu)=c-\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}\delta\mathbf{t}$, and
the ray $\mathbf{x}'=r'\boldsymbol\ell$ meets it at
$r'=(c-\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}\delta\mathbf{t})/(\boldsymbol\nu\cdot\boldsymbol\ell+\delta\boldsymbol\omega^{\top}(\boldsymbol\ell\times\boldsymbol\nu))$.
The row of $J_q$ for this pixel is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial r}{\partial\delta\mathbf{t}}=-\frac{\boldsymbol\nu^{\top}}{\boldsymbol\nu\cdot\boldsymbol\ell},\qquad
  \frac{\partial r}{\partial\delta\boldsymbol\omega}=-\frac{r\,(\boldsymbol\ell\times\boldsymbol\nu)^{\top}}{\boldsymbol\nu\cdot\boldsymbol\ell},
  \label{eq:Jq}
\end{equation}
which we verified against exact ray--plane intersection by finite differences. The norm of the
rotation row is $r\,|\boldsymbol\ell\times\boldsymbol\nu|/|\boldsymbol\nu\cdot\boldsymbol\ell|=r\tan\theta$ exactly,
since $\mathrm{d}r/\mathrm{d}\theta=h\sin\theta/\cos^{2}\theta=r\tan\theta$ for a plane at
perpendicular distance $h$, $r=h/\cos\theta$. For an isotropic rotation error of standard
deviation $\sigma_\varphi$ the diagonal of the pose term of \eqref{eq:Sigmar} is therefore
\begin{equation}
  v_{\mathrm{res}}(r,\theta)=\sigma_r^{2}+\bigl(r\,\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta\bigr)^{2},
  \label{eq:vres}
\end{equation}
the variance of the discrepancy between the measured depth and the intersection of the nominal
ray with the surface, which is the independent-pixel special case of the covariance. It
diverges at grazing incidence, and its linearisation fails once $\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta\gtrsim0.3$,
so $\tan^{2}\theta$ is replaced by $\sin^{2}\theta/(\cos^{2}\theta+\epsilon^{2})$ with
$\epsilon\approx\cos80^{\circ}$, the result is clipped, and rays with $\theta>85^{\circ}$ are
gated,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde v_{\mathrm{res}}=\operatorname{clip}\!\Bigl(\sigma_{r}^{2}
  +r^{2}\sigma_{\varphi}^{2}\,\tfrac{\sin^{2}\theta}{\cos^{2}\theta+\epsilon^{2}},\;
  v_{\min},\,v_{\max}\Bigr).
  \label{eq:vreg}
\end{equation}
The point error is not the same as this depth discrepancy: in the ray frame
$(\ray,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})$ the covariance of the back-projected point is
$\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_r^2,(r\sigma_\varphi)^2,(r\sigma_\varphi)^2)$, whose component along the
surface normal,
\begin{equation}
  v_{n}(r,\theta)=\sigma_r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+r^{2}\sigma_\varphi^{2}\sin^{2}\theta,
  \label{eq:vn}
\end{equation}
stays bounded as $\theta\to\pi/2$; it initialises the splat covariances and floors their scale.
The derivation assumes a locally planar surface, small errors and independent Gaussian errors.

\section{Pairwise Gate (Comparison Variant)}
\label{app:pairwise}
The pairwise gate replaces the shared surface by the depth maps of the neighbouring views.
For view $i$ with neighbours $\mathcal{N}(i)$, the depth map of each neighbour $j$ is warped
into view $i$ with the provisional poses, giving a candidate depth $D_{j\to i}$ at the
pixels where it is visible; the view's own depth map is never part of the consensus. The
neighbours may themselves be faulty, so the consensus is built as in our earlier work: with
$F_C=\prod_{j\in C}f_{j\to i,u}$, $Z_C=\int F_C$, $p_C=F_C/Z_C$ and $H(C)=-\!\int p_C\log p_C$,
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pairwise} seeds with the pair of warped neighbours of largest pairwise
evidence, expands with the compatible neighbour of largest mean log Bayes factor (which
removes the order dependence), and returns the minimum-entropy set among admissible sets
with at most $F<|\mathcal{N}(i)|/2$ removals. The view's depth map is then tested against
the consensus $p_C$ with the Bayes factor \eqref{eq:lambda} and the statistic
\eqref{eq:Ts} after a robust affine alignment, with $\hat S_i,v_{S,i}$ replaced by the mean
and variance of $p_C$. Its cost is $C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:method:cost}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Pairwise consensus among warped neighbours (comparison variant)}
\label{alg:pairwise}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE depth PDFs $\{f_{j\to i,u}\}_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}$ of the warped neighbours of view $i$, removal budget $F<|\mathcal{N}(i)|/2$, threshold $\tau$
\STATE $(a^\star,b^\star)\gets\argmax_{a\ne b}\bar\ell_{b}(\{a\})$
\IF{$\bar\ell_{b^\star}(\{a^\star\})<\tau$}
  \RETURN no consensus
\ENDIF
\STATE $C\gets\{a^\star,b^\star\}$
\WHILE{$\exists\,k\notin C:\ \bar\ell_k(C)\ge\tau$}
  \STATE $k^\star\gets\argmax_{k\notin C}\bar\ell_k(C)$; \ $C\gets C\cup\{k^\star\}$
\ENDWHILE
\STATE Among admissible sets $C'$ with $|\mathcal{N}(i)|-|C'|\le F$, \textbf{return} $\argmin_{C'}H(C')$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Detection Analysis}
\label{app:detection}
\emph{Projected, whitened statistics.} Under the nominal model $\mathbf{r}_0=A\boldsymbol\eta+\mathbf{n}$
with $\mathbf{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_r)$. The matrix $M$ of \eqref{eq:M} satisfies
$MA=0$ and $M\Sigma_rM=M$, so $\mathbf{r}_0^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_0=\mathbf{n}^{\top}M\mathbf{n}\sim\chi^{2}_{n-2}$ whatever
$\boldsymbol\eta$ is. A fault $\boldsymbol\phi=\beta\mathbf{g}$ adds $\beta\mathbf{g}$ to $\mathbf{n}$, so the matched
statistic $z=\mathbf{g}^{\top}M\mathbf{r}_0/\kappa$ with $\kappa^{2}=\mathbf{g}^{\top}M\mathbf{g}$ has mean $\beta\kappa$ and unit
variance, and the energy statistic is non-central $\chi^{2}_{n-2}(\beta^{2}\kappa^{2})$. The power of the
two-sided matched test is $\Phi(\beta\kappa-z_\alpha)+\Phi(-\beta\kappa-z_\alpha)$, which is at least
$1-\beta_{\mathrm{md}}$ when $\beta\kappa\ge z_\alpha+z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}}$, giving \eqref{eq:mdb}. For the energy
test, $\lambda_0$ solves $\Pr[\chi^{2}_{n-2}(\lambda_0)>\chi^{2}_{n-2,1-\alpha}]=1-\beta_{\mathrm{md}}$; for
$n=1000$, $\alpha=0.05$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{md}}=0.1$ it is $138.8$, against $130.7$ from the leading-order
estimate $\sqrt{2(n-2)}(z_{\alpha}^{\mathrm{one}}+z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}})$. For independent unit-variance pixels
and an offset nuisance, $M=I-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}/n$, so a mask of fraction $f$ gives
$\kappa^{2}=nf(1-f)$, zero at $f=1$, and the entries of Table~\ref{tab:mdb} follow from
$(z_\alpha+z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}})/\sqrt{nf(1-f)}$, $(z_{\alpha/R}+z_{\beta_{\mathrm{md}}})/\sqrt{nf(1-f)}$ with $R=100$,
and $\sqrt{\lambda_0}/\sqrt{nf(1-f)}$. A uniform pattern, and a pattern $\sigma(\mathbf{d})\mathbf{1}$ with
$\sigma$ affine in depth, lie in $\mathrm{span}([\mathbf{d},\mathbf{1}])$ and have $\kappa=0$. With a Monte Carlo
check on a correlated field ($\ell=2$, $\nu=0.2$, $n=150$) the false-alarm rate and the power of the
matched statistic agree with theory to within the sampling error.

\emph{Effective number of pixels.} For $\boldsymbol\rho\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},C)$ with unit diagonal, the
energy $\boldsymbol\rho^{\top}\boldsymbol\rho$ has mean $n=\mathrm{tr}(C)$ and variance $2\,\mathrm{tr}(C^{2})$, so
$\mathrm{Var}[\boldsymbol\rho^{\top}\boldsymbol\rho/n]=2\,\mathrm{tr}(C^{2})/n^{2}=2/n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with
$n_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathrm{tr}(C)^{2}/\mathrm{tr}(C^{2})$, which is $n$ for $C=I$ and $1$ for $C=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}$.
A mean statistic has the variance $\mathbf{1}^{\top}C\mathbf{1}/n^{2}$, hence the different effective number
$n^{2}/\mathbf{1}^{\top}C\mathbf{1}$, which for the Gaussian kernel is $n/(2\pi\ell^{2})$ against
$n/(\pi\ell^{2})$ for the energy statistic: correlation hurts a mean more than an energy. On a
$40\times40$ grid with $\ell=1.5$, $2$ and $3$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=236$, $135$ and $62$ against the closed form
$226$, $127$ and $57$ (the closed form ignores the boundary). With a nugget $\nu$,
$\mathrm{tr}(C^{2})\approx n[(1-\nu)^{2}\pi\ell^{2}+2\nu(1-\nu)+\nu^{2}]$. Without a nugget the
covariance is numerically singular and any sharp-edged pattern appears infinitely detectable, so
the nugget is part of the model.

\emph{Pixel-wise filter.} A pixel is flagged when $|\rho_u|>k$. The flagging probability is
$g(\delta)=\Phi(\delta-k)+\Phi(-\delta-k)$ with $g(0)=2\Phi(-k)=P_{\mathrm{fa}}$, $g'(0)=0$ and
$g''(0)=2k\phi(k)$, so $g(\delta)-P_{\mathrm{fa}}\approx k\phi(k)\delta^{2}$. For $k=3$ and $\delta=0.3$,
$g=0.0040$ against $P_{\mathrm{fa}}=0.0027$. Counting the flagged pixels of the view gives the statistic
$z_{\mathrm{cnt}}=f\,(g(\delta)-P_{\mathrm{fa}})\sqrt{n}/\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{fa}}(1-P_{\mathrm{fa}})}$, and the ratio of the
matched statistic $\delta\sqrt{nf(1-f)}$ to it is
$R(\delta,f)=\delta\sqrt{(1-f)/f}\,\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{fa}}(1-P_{\mathrm{fa}})}/(g(\delta)-P_{\mathrm{fa}})$, equal to
12.4 at $\delta=0.3$, $f=0.5$ and to 24.9 at $f=0.2$, and reaching one at $\delta=1.65$ and $2.30$.
The sensitivity of the matched statistic is first order in $\delta$ and that of the count second order,
which is the reason for the sub-noise advantage; the unaggregated filter, which decides per pixel, is
weaker still.

\emph{Surface term.} For $K$ surfaces the delete-a-group jackknife covariance
$\tfrac{K-1}{K}\sum_k(\mathbf{s}_k-\bar{\mathbf{s}})(\mathbf{s}_k-\bar{\mathbf{s}})^{\top}$ is positive semi-definite of rank at
most $K-1$. A small $K$ gives a noisy estimate, so a floor is added; its adequacy is part of the
calibration check (H6).

\emph{Protection level.} The faults that the energy test misses satisfy
$\boldsymbol\phi^{\top}M\boldsymbol\phi\le\lambda_0$. The linear output $q=q_0+\mathbf{s}^{\top}\boldsymbol\phi$ is
maximised on this ellipsoid, for $\mathbf{s}$ orthogonal to the nuisance directions in the metric of
$\Sigma_r$, by $\boldsymbol\phi^{*}=c\,\tilde\Sigma_r\mathbf{s}$ with $\tilde\Sigma_r=\Sigma_r-A(A^{\top}\Sigma_r^{-1}A)^{-1}A^{\top}$,
which satisfies $\tilde\Sigma_rM\tilde\Sigma_r=\tilde\Sigma_r$, giving the maximum
$\sqrt{\lambda_0\,\mathbf{s}^{\top}\tilde\Sigma_r\mathbf{s}}$ of \eqref{eq:pl}. For independent pixels it is
$\sqrt{\lambda_0}\,\sigma\,\|\mathbf{s}-\bar{s}\mathbf{1}\|$. The tests of the code verify the maximum by random
search on the boundary and by attaining it with $\boldsymbol\phi^{*}$.
