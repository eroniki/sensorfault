\section{Derivation of the Residual Variance}
\label{app:variance}
Let a ray with unit direction $\ray$ meet a locally planar surface with unit normal
$\nrm$ at perpendicular distance $h$ from the camera, with incidence angle $\theta$
($\cos\theta=|\nrm\cdot\ray|$). The range to the plane is $r(\theta)=h/\cos\theta$,
so $\mathrm{d}r/\mathrm{d}\theta=h\sin\theta/\cos^{2}\theta=r\tan\theta$ exactly. A
pointing error $\delta\varphi$ in the plane of incidence therefore changes the
intersection range by $\delta r\approx r\tan\theta\,\delta\varphi$, whereas the
out-of-plane component has no first-order effect. With $\delta\varphi$ independent of
the range noise,
$\mathrm{Var}(r_{\mathrm{meas}}-r_{\mathrm{plane}})=\sigma_r^{2}+r^{2}\sigma_\varphi^{2}\tan^{2}\theta$,
which is \eqref{eq:vres}. The derivation assumes a locally planar surface, small errors
($\sigma_\varphi\tan\theta\ll1$) and independent Gaussian $\sigma_r$ and $\sigma_\varphi$.
The regularisation \eqref{eq:vreg} replaces $\tan^{2}\theta$ by
$\sin^{2}\theta/(\cos^{2}\theta+\epsilon^{2})$, which is bounded, and clips the result.
In the ray frame the point error is $\delta r\,\ray+r\,\delta\varphi\,\mathbf{u}$ with
$\mathbf{u}\perp\ray$ in the plane of incidence; its component along $\nrm$ is
$\delta r\cos\theta+r\,\delta\varphi\sin\theta$ up to sign, with variance
$\sigma_r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta+r^{2}\sigma_\varphi^{2}\sin^{2}\theta$, which is \eqref{eq:vn} and
remains bounded as $\theta\to\pi/2$.

\section{Pairwise Gate (Comparison Variant)}
\label{app:pairwise}
The pairwise gate replaces the shared surface by the depth maps of the neighbouring views.
For view $i$ with neighbours $\mathcal{N}(i)$, the depth map of each neighbour $j$ is warped
into view $i$ with the provisional poses, giving a candidate depth $D_{j\to i}$ at the
pixels where it is visible; the view's own depth map is never part of the consensus. The
neighbours may themselves be faulty, so the consensus is built as in our earlier work: with
$F_C=\prod_{j\in C}f_{j\to i,u}$, $Z_C=\int F_C$, $p_C=F_C/Z_C$ and $H(C)=-\!\int p_C\log p_C$,
Algorithm~\ref{alg:pairwise} seeds with the pair of warped neighbours of largest pairwise
evidence, expands with the compatible neighbour of largest mean log Bayes factor (which
removes the order dependence), and returns the minimum-entropy set among admissible sets
with at most $F<|\mathcal{N}(i)|/2$ removals. The view's depth map is then tested against
the consensus $p_C$ with the Bayes factor \eqref{eq:lambda} and the statistic
\eqref{eq:Ts} after a robust affine alignment, with $\hat S_i,v_{S,i}$ replaced by the mean
and variance of $p_C$. Its cost is $C_{\mathrm{pair}}$ of Section~\ref{sec:method:cost}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Pairwise consensus among warped neighbours (comparison variant)}
\label{alg:pairwise}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE depth PDFs $\{f_{j\to i,u}\}_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}$ of the warped neighbours of view $i$, removal budget $F<|\mathcal{N}(i)|/2$, threshold $\tau$
\STATE $(a^\star,b^\star)\gets\argmax_{a\ne b}\bar\ell_{b}(\{a\})$
\IF{$\bar\ell_{b^\star}(\{a^\star\})<\tau$}
  \RETURN no consensus
\ENDIF
\STATE $C\gets\{a^\star,b^\star\}$
\WHILE{$\exists\,k\notin C:\ \bar\ell_k(C)\ge\tau$}
  \STATE $k^\star\gets\argmax_{k\notin C}\bar\ell_k(C)$; \ $C\gets C\cup\{k^\star\}$
\ENDWHILE
\STATE Among admissible sets $C'$ with $|\mathcal{N}(i)|-|C'|\le F$, \textbf{return} $\argmin_{C'}H(C')$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Detection Analysis}
\label{app:detection}
Let $\rho_u$, $u=1,\dots,n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, be standardised residuals, independent with unit variance.

\emph{Mean-shift statistic.} Under a bias $\delta$ on a fraction $f$ of the pixels,
$\mathbb{E}[\rho_u]=\delta$ on $f n_{\mathrm{eff}}$ pixels and $0$ elsewhere, so
$z=n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/2}\sum_u\rho_u\sim\mathcal{N}(f\delta\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}},1)$. A two-sided test
at false-alarm probability $\alpha$ has power $\Phi(f\delta\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}-z_\alpha)+\Phi(-f\delta\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}-z_\alpha)$,
which is at least $1-\beta$ when $f\delta\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}\ge z_\alpha+z_\beta$, giving
\eqref{eq:mdb}. A Monte Carlo check with $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1000$, $\delta=0.3$, $f=0.5$ gives a
false-alarm rate of 0.051 and a power of 0.997, as predicted.

\emph{Energy statistic.} For a zero-mean field of rms amplitude $\delta$ on a fraction $f$ of
the pixels, $\mathbb{E}[T]=1+f\delta^{2}$ and $\mathrm{Var}[T]\approx(2+4f\delta^{2})/n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, so to
leading order the field is detected when $f\delta^{2}\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}/2}\ge z_\alpha+z_\beta$, that is
$\delta_{\min}=\bigl(\sqrt2(z_\alpha+z_\beta)/(f\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}})\bigr)^{1/2}\propto n_{\mathrm{eff}}^{-1/4}$. For
$\alpha=0.05$, $\beta=0.1$, $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1000$ and $f=1$ this is $0.38$. The mean of such a field is
zero, so the mean-shift statistic is blind to it: in a Monte Carlo check with an rms amplitude of
$0.5\sigma$ and $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1000$, the energy statistic has a power of 0.998 at 5\% false alarm,
and the mean-shift statistic of 0.118, close to the false-alarm rate.

\emph{Pixel-wise filter.} A pixel is flagged when $|\rho_u|>k$. The flagging probability is
$g(\delta)=\Phi(\delta-k)+\Phi(-\delta-k)$ with $g(0)=2\Phi(-k)=P_{\mathrm{fa}}$, $g'(0)=0$ and
$g''(0)=2k\phi(k)$, so $g(\delta)-P_{\mathrm{fa}}\approx k\phi(k)\delta^{2}$. For $k=3$ and $\delta=0.3$,
$g=0.0040$ against $P_{\mathrm{fa}}=0.0027$. Counting the flagged pixels of the view gives the
statistic $z_{\mathrm{cnt}}=f\,(g(\delta)-P_{\mathrm{fa}})\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}/\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{fa}}(1-P_{\mathrm{fa}})}$.
With $n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1000$ and $\delta=0.3$, $z_{\mathrm{cnt}}=0.76f$ against $z=9.5f$, a ratio of 12.4. The ratio
$R(\delta)=(g(\delta)-P_{\mathrm{fa}})/(\delta\sqrt{P_{\mathrm{fa}}(1-P_{\mathrm{fa}})})$ of the count
statistic to the mean-shift statistic is below one for small $\delta$ and reaches one at
$\delta\approx1.65$ for $k=3$, so the count is the better statistic only for larger bias, and
the unaggregated filter, which makes a decision per pixel, is weaker still. The first-order
sensitivity of the mean-shift statistic ($\propto\delta$) against the second-order sensitivity of
the count ($\propto\delta^{2}$) is the reason for the sub-noise advantage of the gate.
