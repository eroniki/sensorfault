\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\runin{Uncertainty on radiance fields and splats.} FisherRF \cite{unc_fisherrf} takes the Fisher information
of the rendering loss over all model parameters to select views; PUP 3D-GS \cite{unc_pup3dgs} uses a
spatial sensitivity score to prune primitives; OUGS \cite{unc_ougs} propagates covariance through the
rendering Jacobian for object-aware view selection; GauSS-MI \cite{unc_gaussmi} attaches a visual
reliability to each Gaussian and plans views by Shannon mutual information; Bayes' Rays
\cite{unc_bayesrays} fits a perturbation field after training. All keep one uncertainty, from the images,
and use it as a relative score. We keep one record per source on shared geometric coordinates, calibrate
its absolute value, and test one source against the other.

\runin{Monocular depth in splatting.} Depth priors are aligned per view and added as a loss
\cite{gs_dnsplatter,rec_monosdf}; large-scale systems accept them as an optional regulariser
\cite{gs_hierarchical}. Recent work selects where to trust the prior, by a rendered-depth consistency
check with a hand-set threshold \cite{dep_indepthwetrust} or by photometric agreement of an image-only
baseline \cite{dep_reliability}. These are masks without a stated error rate; they are baselines here.
Robust losses and distractor-aware splatting \cite{rob_barron,gs_robustnerf,gs_spotless} down-weight
outlying pixels, not a prior that is wrong consistently.

\runin{Fusion of correlated sources and testing.} Information-form fusion of surfels follows point-based
fusion \cite{fus_keller_surfels}; fusing sources whose correlation is unknown calls for conservative
rules such as covariance intersection \cite{fus_julier_ci}, of which our saturated prior is a special
case with the correlation estimated. Testing an estimate against independent evidence is data snooping and
solution separation \cite{stat_teunissen_dia,flt_raim_joerger}. Calibration is scored with a proper rule
\cite{stat_gneiting_scoring}; temperature scaling \cite{cal_guo_temperature} is the single-parameter
analogue of our dispersion.
