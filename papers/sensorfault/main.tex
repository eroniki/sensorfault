\begin{abstract}
Maps built from posed aerial video and monocular depth state no confidence and can be confidently wrong over
whole surfaces, identically in every frame. We store on each planar Gaussian surfel patch a Fisher
information record on its placement, an offset and two tilts, separately for the depth prior and for the
pixels. Because the surfel is a plane, the image Jacobian factors through the depth Jacobian, so both
records are quadratic forms on the same coordinates and their disagreement is a statistic with a null
distribution. Other frames enter by rendering the shared surfels; no features are matched. On a synthetic
aerial block with exact depth, a depth-only map states an error variance \ph{X} times too small, close to
the \ph{Y} predicted from the share of depth error that all frames repeat. The pixel-against-depth test
detects repeated faults at \ph{Z}\% true-positive rate at 5\% false alarms where depth-consensus gating
reaches \ph{W}\%. Calibrating pixel information by displacement injected through the renderer gives
\ph{C}\% coverage at the nominal 95\% and a surface log-score of \ph{S} nats per entity against \ph{S0} for
depth only. Checks on \ph{N} real scenes with laser truth \ph{agree}.
\end{abstract}
