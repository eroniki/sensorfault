# B. Affine-invariant / relative depth (all verified via arXiv abs pages, 2026-11-16)

1. ranftl2022midas - MiDaS. Trains on mixed datasets with a scale-and-shift-invariant loss (+ gradient-matching) so disparity is predicted up to an unknown affine map. Our setting: the canonical relative-depth formulation; our per-view percentile normalisation is a different but equivalent-in-spirit way of removing scale/shift. https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01341 . Caveats: arXiv page says "To appear in TPAMI (accepted Aug 2020)"; year 2022 and no vol/pages were NOT confirmed from the page (check publisher). MiDaS predicts affine-invariant DISPARITY, not depth.
2. ranftl2021dpt - DPT. ViT backbone for dense prediction; strong relative-depth model (MiDaS v3). Relation: backbone of widely used relative-depth baselines to compare against. https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.13413 . Caveat: arXiv page lists no venue; ICCV 2021 is from memory/unverified on the page, so check the ICCV proceedings before final.
3. yang2024depthanything - Depth Anything. Large-scale pseudo-labelled data, affine-invariant relative depth (disparity) with SSI loss. Relation: strong relative-depth prior/baseline; same ambiguity as ours. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10891 . Venue: CVPR 2024 (arXiv comment).
4. yang2024depthanythingv2 - Depth Anything V2. Synthetic-data teacher + pseudo-labelled real data, finer relative depth. Relation: current relative-depth baseline; evaluated with scale/shift alignment. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414 . Venue: NeurIPS 2024 (arXiv comment).
5. bhat2023zoedepth - ZoeDepth. Relative-depth pretraining then metric heads fine-tuned. Relation: shows relative depth needs extra metric head/data for scale; we stay affine-invariant and calibrate with 2DGS. https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12288 . arXiv-only per page (published elsewhere not verified).
6. yin2023metric3d - Metric3D. Canonical camera space transformation to resolve metric ambiguity from focal length. Relation: explains focal-length/scale coupling of metric depth. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10984 . ICCV 2023.
7. hu2024metric3dv2 - Metric3Dv2. Extends Metric3D to depth+normals. Relation: metric baseline requiring intrinsics. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15506 . TPAMI 46(12):10579-10596, 2024 (from arXiv journal ref). arXiv v1 dated Mar 2024.
8. piccinelli2024unidepth - UniDepth. Predicts metric depth without known intrinsics via self-promptable camera module. Relation: metric depth w/o intrinsics, contrast with our affine-invariant output. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18913 . Page lists no venue (CVPR 2024 not verified, so left arXiv-only).
9. bochkovskii2025depthpro - Depth Pro. Metric, sharp depth with focal length estimation, zero-shot. Relation: metric baseline estimating focal length itself. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02073 . ICLR 2025 (arXiv comment); arXiv year 2024.
10. yin2020shape - Learning to Recover 3D Scene Shape. Analyses unknown depth shift and focal length as sources of distortion in affine-invariant depth; two-stage recovery of shift/focal. Relation: closest analysis of the shift ambiguity that our per-view scale+shift fit absorbs. https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.09365 . Page lists no venue (CVPR 2021 from memory, NOT verified; kept as arXiv-only).

## Scale/shift ambiguity and evaluation
A single image is consistent with a family of scenes related by a global scale (and, for disparity or unknown-shift depth, an offset), because projection discards absolute size; unknown focal length additionally couples to apparent depth structure (Yin et al. 2020, Metric3D). Training on mixed datasets with different, unknown depth ranges therefore uses scale-and-shift-invariant losses (MiDaS), and the output is only defined up to an affine map. Evaluation of relative-depth models (MiDaS, Depth Anything) typically fits a per-image least-squares scale and shift to ground truth in disparity (or depth) space before computing errors such as AbsRel/delta. Metric models avoid this by using intrinsics or learned priors but inherit scale errors when those fail. Our per-view percentile normalisation plus per-view scale+shift fit against the 2DGS render follows the same alignment logic (statements about the evaluation protocol are a general summary; check each paper's evaluation section before citing specifics).
